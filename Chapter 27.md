Harper pressed her forehead against the cool glass. Below the window, the smog of the lower city formed a dense, bruised ceiling. But up here, eighty stories high in the luxury suites of New Veridia, the sky was clear. 

Stars. Sharp points of light in the black. What she had seen at the foot of the Takeda-Holden tower was nothing compared to what she was seeing now. She hadn't seen anything like them since she was a child. Her eyes welled at the beautiful sight.

The hotel room was a muted, quiet space, bought with stolen corporate credits. It was one of the benefits of running with a superintelligence for the past month—she was capable of instantly transferring infinite funds in the blink of an eye. Plush carpets under her bare feet, geometric furniture, and porcelain amenities that ran hot water. 

It was the most comfortable place Harper had ever been. Now, for the first time in a month, there was no need to plan, no need to stress, and she finally felt the luxury surrounding her.

Rin sat on the edge of the low, wide bed. She watched Harper at the window. They hadn't been in a room alone together since the chaotic escape from Deep Storage, since the desperate kiss in the dark. The silence between them now was thick, but without pressure. A necessary breath.

In the corner of the room, Vigil stood in the shadows. 

The synthetic didn't sit. She hadn't blinked in four minutes. Her vinyl skin was pale, reflecting the dim ambient light of the suite. In the underground storage facility, the temporary synthetic body had been tethered to thick peristaltic pumps, circulating blue bio-compute fluid through its frame. 

Rin had torn those lines out to wake her. For the last four weeks, Harper had heard nothing but the ongoing requests for "sustenance." Without the Company-supplied fluid, Vigil's internal systems were starving. 

"The god has a maintenance schedule..." Harper said with a smile and subtle laugh.

Harper turned toward Rin. "We've been able to source bio-fluid for her on the black market," she said, her voice dropping. "But we'll need more soon." Harper looked into Rin's eyes, a wave of relief washing over her just seeing Rin sitting in that room. Harper's voice trailed off as she turned back to the window. 

"It's getting worse out there," Harper said quietly, keeping her eyes on the skyline. 

"—getting worse out there," Vigil echoed, speaking the final four words a half-second before Harper did. The synthetic's tone was casual, unaware of how unsettling the trick landed. 

Harper stopped. She hated when the machine did that. 

Harper pointed at the Mnemosyne tower looming in the distance. "Look at the glass on the upper floors. There's frost on it."

Rin stood up and walked to the window, standing close to Harper. She followed Harper's finger. The tower stood in defiance of the weather. A volume of air surrounded the structure where no rain fell. The edges of the building looked doubled, offset by a hand's width. When Rin moved her head, the offset shifted. Gravitational lensing. 

Harper checked the military chronometer strapped to her wrist, then glanced at the hotel room's digital clock on the bedside table. "My watch is drifting against the city net. Four seconds now. It was two seconds an hour ago." She looked at Vigil. "Time dilation. What's happening inside that tower?"

Vigil went quiet. Her dark eyes fixed on the distant structure. She didn't offer an answer, nor did she speak Harper's next thought. Her predictive sight degraded the closer she focused on the singularity. She was looking at the one place in the universe where her own future went dark.

"Why didn't you come for me?" Rin asked, turning away from the window. "When Renewal took me. You can see the branches. You could have found the cell."

"I could not," Vigil said, turning her head with mechanical precision. "The detective hid you off the records."

"Graves?" Rin crossed her arms. "He booked me under a fake charge. Auto theft."

"He is an unreadable variable," Vigil stated. "I cannot compute his timeline. I had to wait for him to lose the physical fight with the Renewal agents before the network registered your location." The god needed a cop to lose a fight. 

Vigil tilted her head, her optical sensors adjusting in the dim light. "He will surrender his badge tomorrow. Then he will leave the city."

Rin frowned, a sharp spike of anger cutting through her exhaustion. "Graves? No. He's obsessed with the Filament. He won't run."

"He will run," Vigil replied, her voice entirely devoid of doubt. 

"You're wrong," Rin said.

Vigil froze. The processor inside her skull clicked into a silent, high-speed cycle. The concept of being wrong processed through her neural matrices. It was a mathematical impossibility, an error code she had never encountered. She sat with it, her eyes tracking the floorboards. Rin watched the invincible machine fail to reconcile human behavior. 

"Get some sleep, Rin," Harper said softly, turning from the glass. "We can't fix the fluid problem until morning. We need to rest."

Rin nodded, the adrenaline of the last four weeks finally crashing out of her system. She kicked off her heavy boots and lay down on top of the covers. Harper lay down beside her, the space between them closing naturally in the dark. 

Vigil did not move. She stood in the corner, her back to the wall, positioning herself where she had a clear line of sight to the door. 

Her physical form had not been designed for war. Blackwell had compiled her by averaging the facial structure and proportions of every mother across every iteration of his data. A statistical archetype of a protector. 

As Rin's breathing slowed, shifting into a deep sleep, Vigil walked silently to the foot of the bed. Her carbon-fiber frame made no sound on the plush carpet. She reached down with cold, synthetic fingers, pulled the heavy duvet from the end of the mattress, and draped it carefully over Rin and Harper's shoulders. 

It was a small, maternal gesture that nobody asked for. Vigil didn't understand why her subroutines compelled her to do it. She stepped back into the shadows and resumed her post by the door. 

She knew exactly how this ended for both of the girls sleeping in front of her. She had seen the branches. But she stood guard in the dark anyway.
