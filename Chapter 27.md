The streets of Oshima had fractured. The usual flow of pedestrians in the intersections was gone, replaced by an unsettled, empty energy. Down the block came the sound of metal shattering glass. The people of the Dead Circuits were targeting Takeda-Holden supply stores. Shadows slipped through the jagged frames of the Company windows, pulling out armfuls of stolen supplies and sprinting into the night.

Rin and Vigil moved fast, running through the barren streets. Rin was breathing heavily. She had no idea where they were going—she was just following Vigil.

Water rushed through the intersections, spilling over the curbs and flooding the gutters. Rin's lungs burned as the night air hit them with every deep inhale. Broken glass cracked under her worn, inmate-issued running shoes. She had peeled down the top half of her jumpsuit and tied it tightly around her waist, hiding the prisoner orange and the black D.O.C. stencil. Her white cotton tank top was damp, clinging to her wet skin. 

Vigil moved through the physical world with a hyper-optimized precision. Her gait was perfect, lacking even the synthesized natural sway programmed into generic synthetics. She was wearing the stolen Oshima police uniform, the dark, waterproof bomber-jacket zipped tight over her synthetic chassis, and the eight-point cap low over her eyes. 

A group of civilians rushed past them on the pavement. They saw Vigil and moved toward the center of the road, yelling obscenities at the synthetic police officer. They were sprinting hard. Rin knew exactly why they were running and what was marching up the street. 

One of the looters looked at Vigil. His eyes grew wide, and all the color ran from his face. He didn't see a cop. He stared at the `RA` serial number glowing under Vigil's eye. He stumbled into Rin, cursing frantically as he pushed off her to get his bearings, before breaking into a terrified sprint.

Sirens sounded behind them. Rin looked back over her shoulder. Blocks away, police mag-cars, their red and blue lights diffusing in the fog, ignored the stoplights and sped through the intersections. 

"Something tells me the police are not out to stop the looting," Rin choked, her hands on her knees, gasping to catch her breath. "Just—just hold on."

Vigil stepped toward her, her dual tones low. "Erin Adler, if you stop here, the next police patrol finds you in thirty-two of your universe's seconds."

She heard the warnings but shook her head, "Wait. I've been running for blocks."  Rin looked up at the synthetic, her breaths still staggered. "—and don't call me that. It's Rin." She was cut short by two solid bleeps of an Oshima police mag-cruiser turning the corner behind them.

The cruiser sat unevenly on its magnetic cushion, as it glided out of the fog into view. The front panel had been replaced, but not painted. The old Oshima city seal etched into the door. A spotlight mounted to the passenger door swept along the storefronts and over the two of them on the sidewalk.


"Erin Adler." Vigil paused, her voice low and directly in her ear. "Rin. You will do exactly as I say, or these officers will open fire and shoot you. You will not die immediately. However, this outcome produces an undesirable state. Please remain quiet."

Vigil reached down and grabbed Rin, firmly placing her hands behind her back.

"Okay. Okay. Vigil, that kind of hurts."

"Remain quiet, Rin." Vigil's dual tones were razor-sharp.

The police cruiser came to an idle at the side of the road. The spotlight focused directly on them. Two figures emerged from the car. "Hey!" one of the police yelled. "Where's your partner, canner?"

Vigil remained silent. 

The two figures were backlit by the spotlight as they walked forward. Rin could see they were both wearing the same uniform as Vigil. 

"I'm talkin' to you, canner," the officer yelled, lighting a cigarette and blowing the smoke into the fog. "You know, after today, I don't like canners who don't do what they're told."

His partner clicked a tactical flashlight in Rin's face. "Holy shit! Sully. Look at this. Ain't this the bitch the Madame showed us a picture of last week?"

Sully took a drag of his cigarette and clicked his flashlight to join his partner's on Rin's face. "Hol-ly shit. I'll be damned. Brask, you and me just made our monthly payouts." He walked into Rin's personal space. "Someone's been lookin' for you, darlin'." 

Sully took a drag and shone his light up at Vigil. His voice was stern. "Where's your partner, and why do you have this girl?"

Vigil said nothing. 

"Well, we'll take it from here, canner. You're done. Get the hell outta here." Sully's voice was harsh.

"Hey, uh, Sully, why's she wearin' a D.O.C. jumper?" Brask's voice pitched in confusion.

Sully inspected Rin's orange suit. "Where you comin' from, girl?" He took out his handgun. "I told you, canner. You're done here. Go back to the precinct." 

Brask didn't lower his flashlight. The beam traced up from Rin's tied-off jumper, over the dark fabric of Vigil's stolen police jacket, and stopped on the synthetic's face. 

"Sully," Brask's voice dropped, the confusion turning into pure panic. "Sully, put the gun down." 

"What? I'm not askin' this machine twice—"

"Look at her face!" Brask took a physical step back, his hand shaking so badly the flashlight beam stuttered across Vigil's unblinking eyes. "That's an R-A serial. She's not one of ours. She's Renewal." 

Sully froze. He looked at the serial number glowing faintly on the synthetic's cheek. His cocky posture vanished instantly. He was suddenly a man pointing a standard-issue sidearm at a machine that could snap his neck before his brain told his finger to pull the trigger.









"She moves like one of them," a familiar voice called out over the rain.

Rin stopped. Harper was standing in the mouth of a narrow alleyway, her eyes locked on her. 

A wave of relief crashed into Rin's chest. She ran toward Harper, wrapping her arms around her in a tight, desperate hug. It had been four weeks of hell. 

"I'm so glad to see you," Rin breathed into Harper's jacket. 

Harper hugged her back, her grip firm. When they pulled apart, Harper looked past her, out at the chaotic street. The sharp crack of a rifle echoed in the distance. 

"Something is going on out here," Harper said, her voice tight. "The pot's boiling over."
