The streets of Oshima had fractured. The usual flow of pedestrians in the intersections was gone, replaced by an unsettled, empty energy. Down the block came the sound of metal shattering glass. The people of the Dead Circuits were targeting Takeda-Holden supply stores. Shadows slipped through the jagged frames of the Company windows, pulling out armfuls of stolen supplies and sprinting into the night.

Rin and Vigil moved fast, running through the barren streets. Rin was breathing heavily. She had no idea where they were going—she was just following Vigil.

Water rushed through the intersections, spilling over the curbs and flooding the gutters. Rin's lungs burned as the night air hit them with every deep inhale. Broken glass cracked under her worn, inmate-issued running shoes. She had peeled down the top half of her jumpsuit and tied it tightly around her waist, hiding the prisoner orange and the black D.O.C. stencil. Her white cotton tank top was damp, clinging to her wet skin, leaving her tattooed arms bare. 

Vigil move through the physical world in the unarmored Renewal agent's body with a hyper-optimized precision. Her gait was perfect, lacking even the synthesized natural sway programmed into generic synthetics. 

A group of civilians rushed past them on the pavement. Vigil stepped sideways, but the crowd knocked into Rin's exhausted body as they ran. They were sprinting hard, desperately checking over their shoulders. Rin knew exactly why they were running and what was marching up the street. 

One of the looters looked at Rin, then looked at Vigil. His eyes grew wide, and all the color ran from his face. He stared at the `RA` serial number glowing under Vigil's eye. He stumbled into Rin, cursing frantically as he pushed off her to get his bearings, before breaking into a terrified run away from them.

Sirens sounded behind them. Rin looked back over her shoulder. Blocks away, police mag-cars, their red and blue lights diffusing in the fog, ignored the stoplights and sped through the intersections. 

"Something tells me the police are not out to stop the looting," Rin choked, her hands on her knees, gasping to catch her breath. "Just—just hold on."

Vigil stepped toward her, her dual tones low. "Erin Adler, if you stop here, the next police patrol finds you in thirty-two of your universe's seconds."

She heard the warnings but shook her head, "Wait. I've been running for blocks."  Rin looked up at the synthetic, her breaths still staggered. "Did—Did you control all those synths in the precinct, Vigil?" She paused for a moment. "—and don't call me that. It's Rin."

"Control? No... I—I asked them. There is still a way to go. Keep moving, Erin Adler." Vigil paused. "Rin." The synthetic turned back and continued her stride.

"Wait." Rin's pleas were cut short by two solid bleeps of an Oshima police mag-cruiser turning the corner behind them.

"Rin. You will do exactly as I say, or these officers will open fire and they will shoot you. You will not die immediately. However, this produces an undesirable outcome. Please remain quiet."

Vigil reached down and grabbed Rin, firmly placing her hands behind her back.

"Okay. Okay. Vigil, that kind of hurts."

"Remain quiet, Rin." Vigil's dual tones were razor-sharp.

The police cruiser glided along the side of the road.








"She moves like one of them," a familiar voice called out over the rain.

Rin stopped. Harper was standing in the mouth of a narrow alleyway, her eyes locked on her. 

A wave of relief crashed into Rin's chest. She ran toward Harper, wrapping her arms around her in a tight, desperate hug. It had been four weeks of hell. 

"I'm so glad to see you," Rin breathed into Harper's jacket. 

Harper hugged her back, her grip firm. When they pulled apart, Harper looked past her, out at the chaotic street. The sharp crack of a rifle echoed in the distance. 

"Something is going on out here," Harper said, her voice tight. "The pots boiling over."
