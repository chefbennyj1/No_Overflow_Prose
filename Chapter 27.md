Rin moved fast, following Vigil through the ruined streets of the lower city. The skeleton of a flipped mag-car burned in the intersection, the rain hissing into steam against the scorched metal. Heavy concrete Renewal barricades had been dragged across the pavement, covered in wet spray paint, washing away in the rain. No bodies. Every storefront on the block had its glass shattered across the sidewalk. The unfortunate price of the upheaval.

From the adjacent streets came the rhythmic chants of a large crowd. As Rin and Vigil ran, the chanting resolved into the overlapping roar of a mob. A synthesized Renewal agent voice spoke through a vehicle-mounted loud speaker, accompanied by a warning siren.

Water rushed through the intersections, spilling over the curbs and flooding the gutters. Rin's lungs burned as the night air hit them with every deep inhale. Broken glass cracked under her worn, inmate-issued running shoes. She had peeled down the top half of her jumpsuit and tied it tightly around her waist, trying to hide the black D.O.C. stencil stamped on her back. But the pants were still unmistakable prisoner orange. Her white cotton tank top was damp, clinging to her wet skin. 

Vigil moved through the physical world with a hyper-optimized precision. Her gait was perfect, lacking even the synthesized natural sway programmed into generic synthetics. She was wearing the stolen Oshima police uniform, the dark, waterproof bomber-jacket zipped tight over her synthetic chassis, and the eight-point cap low over her eyes. 

A looter rushed past them on the pavement. He looked at Vigil, his eyes growing wide as all the color ran from his face. He stumbled into Rin, cursing frantically as he pushed off her to get his bearings, before breaking into a terrified sprint.

Sirens sounded behind them. Rin looked back over her shoulder. Blocks away, police mag-cars, their red and blue lights diffusing in the fog, ignored the stoplights and sped through the intersections. 

"Something tells me the police are not out to stop the looting," Rin choked out. She stopped running, resting her hands on her knees. "Just—just hold on."

Vigil stepped toward her, her dual tones low. "Erin Adler, if you stop here, the next police patrol finds you in thirty-two of your universe's seconds."

She heard the warnings but shook her head. "Wait. I've been running for blocks."  Rin looked up at the synthetic. "—and don't call me that. It's Rin." She was cut short by two solid bleeps of an Oshima police mag-cruiser turning the corner behind them.

The cruiser sat unevenly on its magnetic cushion, as it glided out of the fog into view. The front panel had been replaced, but not painted. The old Oshima city seal etched into the side. A spotlight mounted to the passenger door swept along the storefronts and over the two of them on the sidewalk.


"Erin Adler." Vigil paused, her voice low and directly in her ear. "Rin. You will do exactly as I say, or these officers will shoot you. You will not die immediately. However, this produces an undesirable outcome. Please remain quiet."

Vigil reached down and grabbed Rin, firmly placing her hands behind her back.

"Okay. Vigil, that kind of hurts."

"Remain quiet, Rin." Vigil's dual tones were razor-sharp.

The police cruiser came to an idle at the side of the road. The spotlight focused directly on them. Rin turned her head from the glare. Two figures emerged from the car. "Hey!" one of the officers yelled. "Where's your partner, canner?"

Vigil remained silent. 

The two figures were backlit by the spotlight as they walked forward. Rin could see their untucked and disheveled standard-issue Oshima police uniforms. 

"I'm talkin' to you, canner," the officer yelled, lighting a cigarette and blowing the smoke into the fog. "You know, after today, I don't like canners who don't do what they're told."

His partner clicked a tactical flashlight in Rin's face. "Holy shit! Sully. Look at this. Ain't this the bitch the Madame showed us a picture of last week?"

Sully stepped out from the cruiser's glare. "I'll be damned. Brask, you and me just made our monthly payouts." He closed the distance, crowding her. "Someone's been lookin' for you." 
He took a drag of his cigarette, his voice stern and rough. He turned his attention to the synthetic. "Why've you got this girl?"

Vigil maintained her silence. Rin felt the synthetic's grip shift deliberately against her wrists. Vigil wasn't frozen; she was stalling.

"Well, we'll take it from here, canner. You're done. Get the hell outta here." Sully's voice was harsh.

"Hey, uh, Sully, why's she wearin' a D.O.C. jumper?" Brask's voice pitched in confusion.

Sully inspected Rin's orange suit. "Where you comin' from, girl?" 

He took out his handgun. Rin's eyes dropped to the weapon. Sully had heavy, meaty hands, with dirt packed deep beneath his fingernails. The badge on his chest meant nothing to her. She recognized the hands of a thief.

"I told you, canner," Sully said. "You're done here. Go back to the—"

"—precinct," Vigil's dual tones spoke in perfect, simultaneous unison with him. 

Sully stopped. His brow furrowed in annoyance. He took a step closer, raising the handgun. "Listen to me. You are going to hand over the—"

"—girl, and you are going to walk away," they said together, syllable for syllable. 

The cigarette trembled in Sully's lips as he shoved the barrel of the gun firmly against Vigil's chest. "I'll put a hole in your—"

Brask stiffened, his eyes wide. "Sully," the confusion in his voice turned into pure panic. "Sully, put the gun down." 

"What? I'm not askin' this machine twice—" Sully pushed the muzzle harder into Vigil's black bomber-jacket.

"I said put the gun down!" Brask's hand shook so badly the flashlight beam stuttered across Vigil's unblinking eyes. 

The sharp, mechanical double-clack of a Renewal-issue assault shotgun chambering a round cut through the rain. 

"Listen to your partner," a familiar voice said from the dark behind Brask. "I'm gonna get you to drop that gun. It's for your own good, really."

Rin felt Vigil's hands finally release her wrists. The realization hit her, the machine had been tracking the timeline, stalling the cops.

Rin looked past Sully. Before she even registered the face, she saw the worn, familiar sleeves of her own leather jacket. Harper was standing behind Brask, the rain matting her hair flat against her face. She jabbed the black barrel of the assault shotgun hard against his back, her head tilted forward with a fierce concentration in her eyes as she looked over his shoulder. 

Sully froze as he caught the glowing text beneath Vigil's left eye. "What the—" His cocky posture vanished. "She's Renewal." He stared at the serial number. He was suddenly a man caught between a military-grade shotgun in his partner's back, and a corporate killer.

Vigil reached out, her mechanical speed tearing the gun from Sully's grip before he could even flinch. In one swift motion, Vigil slid the magazine free from the handle, pushed the slide back to pop the bullet from the chamber, and handed the empty gun back to the officer.



"Get in the car," Harper said, stepping around Brask, the stock of the shotgun firmly against her shoulder. "And get lost." 

Sully kept his eyes locked on the glowing serial number. He backed away toward the idling mag-cruiser, pulling Brask with him. 

"The Madame is gonna pay a fortune for this," Sully growled as they climbed into the vehicle. "These two walking with a rogue `RA`." 

The cruiser's doors slammed shut. The magnetic drive whined, and the vehicle bolted down the street, vanishing into the fog. 

Rin exhaled a shaky breath. She ran toward Harper, wrapping her arms around her in a tight, desperate hug. 

"I'm so glad to see you," Rin breathed into the worn leather of her own jacket. 

Harper dropped the gun, and hugged her back, her grip firm. When they pulled apart, Rin managed a weak, exhausted smile. "The jacket looks better on you."

Harper smiled. She pulled the Madame's mag-train ticket from the jacket pocket.

Rin looked at the ticket then up at Harper's face. "You could've left," she said.

Harper's smile widened. She looked past Rin, her eyes scanning the flooded street. 

The sharp crack of a rifle echoed in the distance.

"It's gettin' mean out here," Harper said, her voice tight. "C'mon. Let's make ourselves scarce."
