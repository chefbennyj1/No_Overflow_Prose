Rin's breath smoked under the streetlights. The cold came straight through her tank top. Snow landed on her bare arms and didn't melt.

She couldn't stop looking up.

Eighty stories above her, the white shape hung over the point of the tower with the doctor in its hand. It was shaped like a woman. Even from down here it was too bright to hold her eyes on. She had first seen its name on Harper's screens, the night all of this started. Now she knew one more thing about it. It had broken Vigil once.

She looked for Vigil instead.

Vigil sat in the driver's seat with the door hanging open, her face turned up through the windshield. Snow was collecting on her sleeve where her arm rested on the frame. Rin had watched her strip a gun out of a cop's hand before he could blink. Now she wasn't moving at all.

"Can it see us?" Harper asked.

Vigil didn't answer.

Elena had started to shake. Iris got her back onto the stretcher and pulled the blanket up to her chin, and Harper climbed in after them. Rin went around to the cab.

When she got in, Vigil started to drive.

***

They went slowly. Nothing else moved in the clearing. The snow lay smooth and orange under the lamps, and the only tracks in it were their own.

Near the fog line, a white mag-car sat at the curb, still running on its cushion, blending into the ever growing white of the snowy street. Both doors hung open. Snow had drifted in across the seats and piled on the dashboard. Whatever footprints had led away from it were filled in.

Rin turned in her seat and watched it until it was lost in the snow. Whoever had been driving had left and never come back to close the doors.

A block on, the street was trampled.

The snow had been churned to gray slush in a band as wide as the road. Bare footprints, hundreds of them, pressed one inside another. Blue streaks ran through the slush where something had been dragged. Plastic sheets tossed in the wind, wrapped loosely around street signs, and lay twisted in the gutter, half buried.

One way, the trudged snow ran off into the fog, the other way, it ran straight to the foot of the tower.

Vigil followed it.

Rin knew where it came from before they got there. The hangar. She thought back to the rows, full of synths who had stood waiting. She looked back at Harper, who's pale complexion and wide eyes said it all. She knew exactly what the trials of blue liquid on the road was, and where it came from.

"Jesus, Rin. Is this what I—" Harper began whispered under her breath.

"Yes, Harper." Vigil interrupted. "This is expected. Erin Adler's theft always results in Elena Marlowe's synth army marching on the lab. However, three variables change this timeline, and make the outcome unreadable. Elena Marlowe is alive, Detective Victor Graves exists in this timeline, and Doctor Blackwell is not dead."

At the end of the trampled band, the tower's lowest doors faced the street. They had been glass. Now the frames stood empty, and the glass lay across the snow in a wide fan, every piece of it on the outside.

Rin read it the way she would have read a lock. Nobody had broken in. Something had pushed its way out.

Vigil stopped the ambulance at the edge of the glass.

***

They went in on foot. Harper took the lead with the shotgun. Iris came behind her with Elena on her arm, and Vigil walked at the back.

The bottom of the atrium was as big as a train station. Forty stories of glass went up from it, walkway stacked over walkway, to a square of sky so far up it was the size of a thumbnail. The sun hadn't reached down this far. What light there was came down the shaft thin and gray and lay on the frost.

Everything was frosted. The marble, the rails, the steel of the checkpoint. The glass crunched under their boots, then the frost did.

The army had come through here. Plastic sheets hung from the rails of the lowest walkway where bodies had brushed past and left them. IV stands lay on their sides across the floor, dozens of them, wheels in the air. Feeding fluid had run out of the cut tubes and frozen into the marble in long blue streaks, all of them pointing at the doors. Snapped wrist tags lay everywhere, stamped into the frost.

At the Renewal post, a riot shield leaned against the desk. A helmet sat upside down on the floor beside it, its red seam dark. Nobody was there.

Iris stopped beside Rin. "There was a fountain," she said. "Right there. Well, not right there, it was more toward the— It doesn't matter." She pushed her glasses up.

Where she had pointed, a basin of black stone stood full of ice. A plastic sheet was frozen into the top of it.

Near the turnstiles, one of them hadn't made it out.

It lay on its back on the marble with its arms at its sides. A plastic sheet was tangled around its legs, and one knee bent the wrong way. The serial under its eye still glowed. Its eyes were open, turned up at the light coming down the shaft.

The face was narrow, with blunt bangs cut straight across.

Rin knew that face. Vigil was wearing it.

Elena let go of Iris and lowered herself to her knees beside it. Her synthetic hand hovered over the face and didn't touch it.

"Seven," she said. "I only ever drew seven."

"It's still on," Harper said.

Vigil came past them and knelt on the other side of the body. She pressed her thumb to the skin under its eye and held it there. The serial dimmed and went dark. The eyes stayed open.

Then the sound came down the shaft.

It came from high above, from the top of the atrium, where the plaza was. A dry crackle, flattened by the distance and the glass. Then another, rolling down from walkway to walkway until it reached the floor.

Rin had followed the tracks here. She knew where the other end of them went.

Iris was counting under her breath. Rin heard her reach four. Five.

Another crackle came down the glass. Iris stopped counting.

Harper's hand lay flat on the frosted rail beside her. It was nowhere near the scar.

Rin had grown up listening to gunfire through the walls. She knew what a fight sounded like. This was one sound, over and over.

"They're not shooting back," she said.

"They were not told to," Vigil said.

Rin turned. Vigil was still kneeling by the body, her eyes on its face, the serial glowing under her own left eye. Rin couldn't tell whether that was an answer, or something Vigil remembered.

Vigil stood. "We cannot stay here."

***

The turnstiles were locked. A red light burned on the panel at the end of the row.

Rin knelt in front of it. Her fingers were stiff from the cold. No SBC, no tools. She worked a thumbnail under the edge of the cover plate and pried until it popped loose. Behind it, a bundle of wires ran into the reader.

She found the two she wanted and stripped the first one with her teeth.

The light turned green.

The bars folded back. All of them, down the line, one after another.

Rin stayed on her knees with the wire between her teeth. She hadn't crossed anything.

"Well," Harper said. "Look at you."

Rin spat the wire out and stood.

Past the turnstiles, a steel shutter closed off the lift lobby. Iris let go of Elena long enough to step up to the keypad beside it. She pushed her glasses up and keyed the first number, a four.

The shutter rolled up into the ceiling.

Iris stood with her finger in the air.

Rin turned to Vigil. "Is that you?"

"No."

Vigil's eyes went up the shaft, past forty floors of glass, toward the top of the tower.

Rin had known before she asked.

"Why is she letting us in?"

"I cannot see why," Vigil said.

***

The executive lift stood open at the back of the lobby. Inside were steel walls and a single column of buttons.

Elena stepped in first. She reached for the lowest button with her right hand and pressed her thumb to it.

Nothing.

She pressed again. The button stayed dark.

Elena turned the hand over and looked at the carbon-fiber thumb. Then she lifted her left hand and pressed with that.

The button lit. The doors closed, and they went down.

The floor counter ran down in red. B2. B3. B4. Then B6, and B4 again, and B5. Rin's stomach told her they were still falling.

Iris didn't say anything. Her lips were moving, counting.

The counter passed B40 and kept going. Then it went blank.

***

The doors opened, and the cold came in after them. It stung Rin's eyes and smelled of metal.

The lab was dark except for the pillars. They rose out of a pit in the middle of the floor, white and pointed, standing in pools of coolant. Text crawled down the screens on their sides and lit the mist lying on the water. A steel railing ran around the pit, and the floor around it was all grating.

Synthetics in lab coats stood along the walls where they had been left. None of them turned.

Near the railing sat a workstation with one chair in front of it. A ring of dead emitters circled the floor beside it, scorched black. The ashtray on the desk was full.

Elena stood in the doorway and looked at the chair.

Then she went to her terminal. Iris went with her and sat down at the station beside it, and her hands were already moving across the screen.

Vigil walked to the railing and looked down into the pit.

"When I come up," she said, "the Madame will turn the army on me."

"Then she'll have them," Rin said.

"Only if they still answer to her."

Rin tucked the lock of hair behind her ear.

"I sold her the keys," she said.

"Then you know how to take them back," Harper said.

She unzipped the jacket. The train ticket was in the inside pocket where she had put it. Under it was something wrapped in a rag. She unwrapped it and held it out.

Rin's SBC. The wire-wrapped board, the cracked green screen, the cable still coiled around it the way she had left it.

"It was in the jacket," Harper said. "Never wiped it."

Rin took it. It was warm from riding against Harper's ribs. She switched it on, and the screen lit green. Everything she had pulled out of the Company that first night was still on it.

Vigil knelt on the grating in front of her and turned her head. The port was behind her ear.

Rin uncoiled the cable. Her hands had stopped shaking.

"Hey." Harper caught her wrist, the same as she had in the hangar. She didn't say anything else. Then she let go.

Rin pushed the cable into the port and ran the handshake.

The screen filled. Serial numbers poured down it, too fast to read, and every line ended in the same key. Rin knew it. She had handed it over in the rain.

"RA units talk to Renewal," she said. "Renewal talks to storage. Storage talks to—"

"—everything with a tag on its wrist," Vigil said.

Rin keyed down through her own access, into the authority she had never had a use for. A single line asked her where to move it.

Harper leaned over her shoulder and read the screen. "Transfer authority," she said. "Told you."

Rin pointed it at the board in her hand and pressed the key.

The serials kept pouring down. At the end of each line, the key changed. Hundreds of lines. Then thousands.

Rin's thumb found the stop command and rested there.

"If they stop, she'll know," she said.

"Then they keep walking," Vigil said.

Rin looked up at the ceiling. Somewhere above it, the rows were still stepping over each other into the guns.

She took her thumb off the key.

"When I come up," Vigil said, "they have to answer."

"Answer who?"

"You."

The last line scrolled through. The SBC chirped. Rin pulled the cable.

Vigil stood. She looked at Harper, and then at Rin.

"I found you in this place, at this moment," she said, in both of her voices.

Rin knew the words. They were the first thing the synthetic had ever said to her, in the hangar, with the plastic sheet still hanging off its face.

"Then find me after," Rin said.

Vigil didn't answer. She put one hand on the railing and dropped over it into the pit.

The coolant took her to the knees. She waded between the pillars, into the mist, until she stood at the center, and turned to face Elena's station.

"Ready," Elena said.

Vigil closed her eyes.

The serial under her left eye went dim, then dark. The body stood in the mist with nothing inside it. Then its knees folded, and it went down into the coolant.

Harper's hand found Rin's.

***

"Iris," Elena said.

"The boundary's stable. Well, not stable. It's inside tolerance." Iris's fingers flew. "Going."

The text on the pillars stopped scrolling. Every screen went black at once.

In the mist above the fallen body, something began to form.

It came together from the edges in. Shoulders. The line of a back. It was darker than anything in the room, and fine traces of blue and white light crawled across it like current across a board.

It hung there half made. Rin could see the pillars through its legs.

Iris's voice went up. "It isn't holding."

"Keep going."

"Doctor, the clock—" Iris turned her screen. The time in its corner jumped forward and back, faster than Rin could read it.

"Keep going."

The pillar screens lit again.

They didn't show text. Small triangles marched in from the edges of every screen, row after row, each row copying the last, nesting inside each other in perfect order. Toward the center, the pattern came apart. The triangles broke into noise there, and the noise grew.

Iris stopped typing. "That's not ours."

The frost came next. It crept across the grating from the walls in the same shapes, clean and repeating, closing in on the pit. Rin stepped back from it. Where it reached the railing, the steel split with a crack like a gunshot.

In the center of the pit, the coolant boiled and froze at once. Light came up out of it from underneath, from much farther down than the pit went.

The shape above the water held for one more breath. Then it tore apart.

"Elena!" Iris shouted.

The floor tilted. Overhead, a long groan ran through the whole building. Dust sifted down out of the dark onto Rin's face.

She looked up. A crack opened in the ceiling, straight across the lab.

Harper hit her from the side. They went down on the grating together, Harper on top, her arms locked around Rin's head, her whole weight pressing her flat.

"Erin—"

Then the ceiling came down.
