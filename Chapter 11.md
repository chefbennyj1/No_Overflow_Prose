On any given day, the ambient temperature of the Dead Circuits was a humid, trapped heat. However, standing at the base of the Takeda-Holden tower, the concrete beneath Harper's boots felt like a glacier.

The smog didn't touch the tower. The dense fog that choked the rest of the upper city broke around it, creating a strange cylinder of clear air rising  upward. 

Harper tipped her head back. She couldn't see the actual top of the building from street level. Far above, the underbellies of New Veridia's pedestrian sky-bridges and suspended transit walkways crisscrossed the gap between the skyscrapers. She could even see the underside of the sprawling corporate plaza built around the tower's midsection—a heavy shelf of steel and concrete casting its shadow over the lower grid. The forced perspective merged the tower's sheer walls together, vanishing into a dull point in the night sky. The peaks of the surrounding skyscrapers lost in the smog.

She stared up through the clearing. For the first time in years, she saw stars.

They were faint, competing with the polluted light of the city, but they were there. Small pinholes of light in the black.

"You're staring," Rin said. 

Harper broke her gaze from the sky and looked back down at the street. She turned her attention to Rin, who stood just steps away, her jacket collar turned up against the localized cold. 

"We're never gettin' through that lobby," Harper said, her voice cynical. The main concourse of the building was a fortress, and she knew it. Turnstiles, scanners, and heavily armed Renewal agents flanked every entrance.

"—Don't need the lobby," Rin replied shaking her head. She pulled out her wire-wrapped SBC. The small green crystal display listed a basic string of text instructions Vigil had dropped onto her drive. "Company buildings always have leftover deep storage in the sub-levels. Left over from before the mergers. Sealed up and unused."

Harper leaned over to read the glowing green text. "Takeda-Holden transit terminal, sub-level four, door 4002. What's even down there?"

"Don't know," Rin said, turning the SBC off. "But it's what it wants."

Harper smiled, though the anxiety didn't leave her eyes. "Always a plan," she muttered. "Let's go."

They descended the concrete steps into the Takeda-Holden Transit Terminal. 

The station's interchanges fell four tiers below them, surrounded by arches of polished white stone and chrome. They rode the steep escalators past the upper commuter hubs, descending deeper into the earth until they hit sub-level four. Corporate executives, Renewal office workers, and high-end synthetics packed the platforms. The floors were black marble, extending like spilled ink across the terminal.

As they reached the first platform, the temperature plummeted further. 
Frictionless magnetic trains surged through the tunnels, pulling the super-cooled air deep into New Veridia before it settling back in. Harper watched her breath plume in the sterile overhead lighting. The dry, unnatural air crawled along her skin with a prickle of static electricity. The elite commuters didn't seem to notice, but it stiffened Harper's joints.

The herd of corporate suits moved quickly across the platforms, racing to get out of Oshima as fast as possible. Some waited for trains, while others moved toward the tower's executive lifts, speeding up to the pedestrian sky-bridges hundreds of stories above them.

They wore the grime of the Dead Circuits. Harper's brown suede jacket was worn. Rin's heavy boots scuffed against the marble. The wealthy commuters actively parted around them, casting disgusted glances over their hand-held devices at the two girls dirtying the transit hub.

They both stuck out. Badly. And worse, neither of them carried the New Veridia IDs required to legally stand on the platform.

Harper's eyes darted toward the far end. "Where is it?"

"End of the line," Rin said, pulling her SBC from her pocket. She veered away from the tracks, cutting toward a secluded corridor of rusted green maintenance doors running along the side of the train tube. 

They found it hidden in the freezing shadows. Faded white stenciling on the rusted, green steel read: **4002**. 

Rin didn't hesitate. A blank magnetic card dangled from the SBC by a thin ribbon cable. She swiped it through the door's reader. The lock flashed a harsh red. 

"Keep watch," Rin muttered. The bare circuit board in her hand flickered with raw data, running a brute-force decryption against the doors card reader. 

Harper stepped back, scanning the platform. Corporate executives flowed past. 

The crowd parted as two Renewal agents made their way through the terminal platform, patrolling the marble floors. They turned the corner, their opaque visors sweeping the corridor. 

"Rin," Harper breathed. "We have a problem."

The lead agent's visor locked onto them. He raised a heavy, riot-control baton, his voice booming over the noise of the terminal. "Hey! Step away from the door!"

"I need ten seconds," Rin said, watching the flow of numbers move down the green display of the SBC.

"You don't have ten!" Harper gasped as the agent broke into a heavy sprint down the platform. The crowd scattered, making way for the charging agent. 

A sharp beep chirped from the SBC, the small green crystal display reading a series of hashed numbers and letters. 

The agent closed the distance, raising the baton to strike. Harper stepped squarely in front of Rin, bracing herself to take the blow. 

Rin dragged the card upward through the reader a second time. The magnetic lock disengaged with a heavy, mechanical clack. Rin shoved her shoulder against the steel door, forcing it open. She grabbed Harper by the jacket collar pulling her firmly backward into the dark just as the baton swung through the empty air.

The heavy door slammed shut, severing the bright lights of the transit terminal. They stood in the pitch-black, freezing shadows. A second later, a heavy fist hammered against the outside of the steel frame.
