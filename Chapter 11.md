His hands clasped behind his back, Doctor Silas Blackwell stood on the mezzanine overlooking the Mnemosyne Engine. The main floor of the lab was quiet, the cooling fans spinning steadily. Below him, Elena Marlowe was seated at her terminal, dark shadows under her eyes, leaning into her palm. 

"The alignment is holding," Elena said, her voice echoing faintly. "For now."

Blackwell didn't reply. He turned away from the railing and walked into his private office. He stopped at his wooden desk.

On the bookshelf, wedged between two volumes, was a black portfolio containing his original thesis paper on the Parity Effect. He always kept the spine flush with the leather books beside it. Now it jutted out of place.

He stepped closer, pulling the portfolio from the shelf. He turned it over in his hands. The pristine matte finish bore the faint, oily smudge of a fingerprint near the edge.

Someone had been in the office. 

Blackwell set the portfolio back on the shelf, aligning it into its place. He turned and walked back to the doorway. 

"Elena," Blackwell called out, his voice level. "Has anyone else been on the floor off-schedule this week?"

Elena didn't look up. "Just Iris. She was here early on Tuesday before my shift."

Blackwell didn't react. He pulled a crumpled package of cigarettes from his pocket and closed his eyes. He drew a loose cigarette from the package and tapped it against the box. Monday morning. Iris was sitting at her terminal on the mezzanine. The exact rhythm of her stylus against her work station registered in his memory. 

He tapped the cigarette again. 

The exact moment the shadow process had appeared in the readout, the synthetics scrubbed the log.

He went to tap the cigarette again and stopped. Iris's demeanor. Her stylus had stopped its rhythm. There was a subtle stagger in her breath. An awkward shift in her eyes as she looked away. 

Iris had seen the synthetics scrub the logs. 

*She knows.*

He opened his eyes. His pulse had slowed. His footsteps rang out across the lab as he walked out of the office and down the metal stairs.

Elena looked up from her terminal as he passed. "Doctor? Is something wrong?"

Blackwell didn't break stride, marching straight toward the hydraulic doors. 

"Something important has come up," Blackwell said, his voice cold and absolute. "Continue to monitor the boundary until I return."

He stepped through the doors, the steel sealing shut behind him.
