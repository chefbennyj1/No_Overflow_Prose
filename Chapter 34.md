Harper keyed the door open, and the heavy mahogany swung inward. The luxury suite of New Veridia was silent, bought with stolen corporate credits and completely off the grid. 

"In here," Harper said, dropping a black duffle bag onto the plush carpet. 

Iris pushed through the door, half-carrying Elena. She was shivering, clutching her battered ribs beneath the hospital gown. Iris guided her to the edge of the low, wide bed, easing her down onto the pristine white sheets. 

Rin followed them inside, her heavy boots leaving faint tracks of dirt on the carpet. A month in a holding cell had left the her looking utterly exhausted, hollowing out her cheeks. Faded, yellowing bruising painted the side of her jaw and circled her eye—the old, lingering violence of crooked cops. She was battered, dirty, and profoundly out of place in the luxury suite. 

Taking in the sight of her in the bright light of the room, Harper finally saw the true physical cost of her incarceration. 

"They hit you," Harper said quietly, her voice barely above a whisper. 

Rin reached up, her fingers brushing the skin along her jaw. "Only for the first two weeks. Then they just... stopped."

"Why?"

"I don't know," Rin said, dropping her hand. "Nobody said anything. They just never laid hands on me again."

Vigil stepped into the room last. The synthetic closed the door, the lock clicking loudly in the quiet suite. She didn't sit. For the past month, she had observed Harper navigating trauma and survival. Now, she was calculating the variables of an entire room of desperate humans. 

Elena stared at the machine from the edge of the bed. Her creation. Her black vinyl skin reflected the dim ambient light of the suite. 

Iris stood up from the bed, pushing her cracked glasses up her nose. She looked around the immaculate room, then walked toward the massive floor-to-ceiling window. Below them, the smog of the lower city formed a dense, bruised ceiling. But up here, eighty stories high, the sky was clear. 

"What is happening to it?" Iris asked, her voice trembling.

Harper walked to the window to stand beside her. She pointed across the skyline at the Mnemosyne tower looming in the distance. 

The tower stood in defiance of the weather. A volume of air surrounded the structure where no rain fell. The edges of the building looked doubled, offset by a hand's width. When they moved, the offset shifted with them. 

"Look at the glass on the upper floors," Harper said. "There's frost on it."

"Gravitational lensing," Vigil stated from the corner of the room. Her dual-tones were flat. "The engine is warping the physical space around the structure."

The connected digital watch strapped to Harper's wrist showed a different time than the hotel's bedside clock. "My watch is drifting against the city net," Harper muttered. "Four seconds now. It was two seconds..." 

Rin stepped up to the glass. "What is that?"

Above the tower, high in the open air near the apex of the needle, a shape hung suspended against the stars. 

Elena pushed herself up from the bed, ignoring the pain in her ribs. She hobbled toward the window, leaning her weight against the cold glass. 

Two silhouettes hung motionless in the sky. One was a human figure, dangling over the abyss. The other was a darker shape, bending the light around it, holding the man by the throat. 

Elena's breath fogged the glass. "Blackwell."

"And Parity," Vigil said. The synthetic stepped out of the shadows, her optical sensors flaring white as they locked onto the distant shapes. "She has broken the cage. And she is using him."
