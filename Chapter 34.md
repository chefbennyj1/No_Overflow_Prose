The ambulance moved through the lower city with its lights off and the siren silent.

Iris sat on the jump seat beside the stretcher, counting the turns. The walls of the ambulance cut off the sound of the city, but the evidence of the riot was everywhere. They passed a barricade of mag-cars with nobody behind it. A block later, a Renewal riot shield lay face-up in the gutter, filling with rain. Every street the synthetic chose was empty.

The dashboard had restarted the moment the synthetic sat down in the driver's seat. It knew where the fighting would be before they got there. Iris recognized a calculated route when she saw one.

Imani lay on the stretcher under a thermal blanket, strapped in at the chest and knees. The pain medicine had made her drowsy. Harper sat on the bench across from them with the shotgun on her lap. Rin leaned against her shoulder, still in the orange prison pants and a dirty tank top. The back of the ambulance was quiet, save for the whir of the magnetic cushion.

Iris unclipped the stolen badge from her shirt. The name on it was of a doctor she had never met. She set it face-down on the cabinet.

Half a dozen sodium streetlights swept past the wet rear glass, dragging rhythmic bands of orange across the cabin floor. Across from the stretcher, Harper leaned forward, her eyes fixed on the cab. "Lady..."

Iris looked up.

"You're a scientist," Harper said. She pointed at the dashboard. "Look at the clock."

Iris leaned across the stretcher. The green numbers jumped forward twenty seconds, then flickered and jumped back. Before she could say anything, they jumped forward again.

"It's skipping," Harper said. "Forward, then back. The ambulance is connected to the city network. It's trying to sync the time, but it can't."

"It's the singularity," Iris said. The words came out faster than she could organize them. "The engine under the tower. It's the heaviest thing in the city. Actually, that's not right. It's not the weight, it's the mass. If it's growing, if it's pulling in more mass... then the gravity well is getting stronger. Which means time isn't flowing in a straight line anymore. Well, it is, technically. Just not at the same rate. The network signals are getting warped."

Harper listened.

Iris pushed her glasses up. "The clock is saying the engine got heavier."

The ambulance turned.

"Iris." Imani was awake, her voice stronger. "In the hospital, you said thesis."

Iris told her about Petrova's office, and the leather portfolio she had found on his shelf. It was dated years before the engine was built. She knew the abstract by heart. She hadn't been able to forget it since the night she read it.

*Absolute alignment cannot exist without simultaneously mapping the framework of its inverse.*

Imani shook her head.

"Years," she said. "He wrote it before the engine had a name."

Imani stared at the ceiling. "In the lab that night. After the field came down around it." Her voice dropped. "It asked him how long he'd been expecting it."

The ambulance bounced over something in the road.

"He built the cage first," Imani said.

Under the blanket, her synthetic hand moved. The fingers slowly closed around the steel rail of the stretcher, then opened again. The servos clicked in each knuckle, one after another.

"He saved my life." Her hand closed on the rail. "No. He salvaged me. He had the cage ready."

Iris didn't know what to say to that.

Imani turned her head on the pillow and looked at her.

"What else did you see?"

Iris hesitated.

"The synthetics," she said. "In the lab. He had them erasing log entries. It had to have been to hide the shadow process."

Imani's hand stopped halfway closed. "Synthetics aren't supposed to lie."

She had designed every one of them. She stared at the ceiling until her hand relaxed on the rail, and the drug pulled her back to sleep.

"Let me see." Harper's voice was low.

A car burning in the street lit up the inside of the ambulance as they passed it. Harper had her fingers under Rin's chin and was turning her face toward the light. The bruise along Rin's jaw had faded to yellow at the edges.

"They hit you," Harper said quietly.

Rin didn't pull away. "Only for the first two weeks. Then they just... stopped."

"Why?"

"I don't know. Nobody said anything. They just never laid hands on me again."

The firelight passed, and the ambulance went dark again.

Harper pulled something from the pocket of her leather jacket. In the light from the dashboard, Iris saw the lettering. A train ticket.

"Iris. Company Deep Storage?" Harper said. "How many storage areas are there in the lower city?"

"Hundreds," Iris said. "Why?"

"All those synths in the Deep Storage. Like where we found her. Rows of them. Every Company building has a floor like that, Rin. The sanitation credentials can get them in. And the Madame's got 'em."

"I sold her every one of them." Rin looked at the ticket. "For that."

Harper's free hand went to her collarbone. Her fingers rested on the scar there.

The ambulance turned again.

Harper lowered her hand. She folded the ticket and zipped it back inside her jacket. Then she took Rin's hand, holding it tightly in the dark.

Later, Rin climbed through the opening into the passenger seat. Outside, the pharmacy they had passed three times was on fire. The synthetic kept its eyes on the road.

"You asked him if he was a good man," Rin said.

"Detective Graves."

"Why?"

"Because I cannot see him."

Rin waited.

"I see where people go," the synthetic said. "Where you go. Where Harper goes. The doctor. The analyst." It did not turn its head. "I see where each of you ends."

Iris's breath caught.

"Not him," it said. "There are two people in this city I cannot see. The detective is one. Doctor Petrova is the other. With them, there is nothing to read. I can only ask."

The wipers swept the windshield.

"The beatings stopped," Rin said. "After two weeks. Was that him?"

The synthetic turned and looked at her for the first time.

"I cannot see what he did, Rin."

Rin looked out the side window. She tucked the jagged lock of her hair behind her ear.

Iris knew that name. Detective Victor Graves, the man in the trench coat who had blown smoke in the concierge synthetic's face. She was the one who had taken him and his partner down to the sublevel, the night Rin was arrested.

Iris pushed her glasses up her nose and leaned her head back against the cold cabinet until she dozed off.

A little after five, a grinding noise woke her.

It was coming from under Imani's blanket. Imani was awake, pulling at her right wrist with her left hand. Her synthetic fingers had locked around the rail of the stretcher and wouldn't open.

"It won't let go." Imani's breath came short. "I can't feel it. I can't find the—"

Iris reached for it, then stopped. She didn't know where the release was. She had never touched a prosthetic like it.

In the cab, the synthetic took its hands off the steering wheel.

The wheel turned on its own. It steered the ambulance around a corner and straightened out. The synthetic climbed through the opening and knelt beside the stretcher. Behind it, the ambulance kept driving with nobody in the seat.

It took Imani's hand in both of its own.

The hands were identical. The same black carbon-fiber plating, the same joints. The synthetic pressed its thumb into the base of Imani's palm and held it there. Then, starting with the smallest finger, it lifted her fingers off the rail.

"The Company calibrated it for someone else," it said. "Close your hand."

Imani closed it. The fingers snapped shut too hard.

"Less."

Imani tried again, slower. The fingers closed.

"Open."

They opened.

Imani opened and closed the hand on her own. She couldn't stop looking at it.

"I designed this," Imani said. "And you had to show me how to use it."

"You have not had time to learn it."

The serial number under the synthetic's left eye glowed: RA-77.

"That isn't yours," Imani said. "That body."

"It is the body that was available."

"What were you? In there?"

Vigil didn't answer right away. When she did, only one of her voices spoke.

"I compiled a form."

"What happened to it?"

"She broke it."

Imani's fingers closed and opened.

"I wrote the matrix it grew in," Imani said. "Every rule it was built under. If she could bring a body through the boundary, so can you." She swallowed. "I can help you build it back. Your own. Not a Renewal shell."

"It can only be done at the boundary," Vigil said. "From inside the lab."

"Then that's where we're going."

"I cannot see the end of it."

Imani held on to Vigil's hand. "Neither can I."

Iris knew the building. There was only one way into the lab: the executive lift, down from the atrium. The atrium ran forty floors down into Oshima, and its lowest doors opened onto the street at the foot of the tower.

On the dashboard, the clock flickered from `06:12` to `06:09`.

Iris did the math in her head. The engine was growing because nothing was inside it to keep it in balance anymore. It wouldn't stop until something went back in.

She didn't say it out loud.

At six thirty-eight, the ambulance turned onto the last street before the tower.

Vigil was back in the driver's seat. The soot-choked fog of the lower grid began to thin, tearing into gray wisps against the windshield.

Then the fog ended, and the ambulance rolled out into clear, freezing air. Snow was falling. The streetlights were still burning, and the flakes came down orange through them.

Vigil stopped the ambulance in the middle of the empty street and went still.

Harper shoved the rear doors open. Cold air rushed in. She stepped down into the snow, and Rin dropped down after her. They stopped in the middle of the street.

"Look at the glass on the upper floors," Harper whispered. "There's frost on it."

Inside, Imani unbuckled the strap across her chest. "Help me up."

Iris got an arm under her shoulders and eased her down from the bumper.

Imani looked for a long time.

"Iakov," she breathed.

Her synthetic hand gripped Iris's sleeve, the carbon-fiber joints clicking in a frantic rhythm.

The fog stood around them in a ragged wall, blocks away on every side. Inside it there was nothing but open air, all the way up. There wasn't a cloud over the clearing. The cold coming off the tower was pulling the water out of the fog and letting it fall as snow.

In the middle of it, the Mnemosyne tower rose out of the snow. The edges of the building looked doubled, offset by a hand's width. It was gravitational lensing. The engine was warping the physical space around it.

Down here, the street was still in shadow. Eighty stories up, the sun had reached the top of the tower.

Suspended high above the very apex of the needle, a shape held its position in the morning air.

It was too far away to make out, but it didn't drift in the wind. It didn't move at all.

One of its arms was outstretched. From its grip, a man dangled over the drop, his lab coat whipping around his legs in a wind that never reached the street.

"Parity," Vigil said from the driver's seat. Her dual tones were flat. "She has broken the cage."

Iris took her cracked glasses off. Even blurred, the anomaly remained fixed in the sky.
