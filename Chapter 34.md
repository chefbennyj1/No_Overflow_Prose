The ambulance moved through the lower city with its lights off and the siren silent.

Iris sat on the jump seat beside the stretcher, counting the turns. She could hear the riot through the walls, rifles and a crowd shouting somewhere to the east, but every street the synthetic chose was empty. They passed a barricade of mag-cars with nobody behind it. A block later, a Renewal riot shield lay face-up in the gutter, filling with rain.

The dashboard had restarted the moment the synthetic sat down in the driver's seat. It knew where the fighting would be before they got there. Iris had spent years reading boundary data. She knew a calculated route when she saw one.

Elena lay on the stretcher under a thermal blanket, strapped in at the chest and knees. The autoinjector had made her drowsy. Harper sat on the bench across from them with the shotgun on her lap. Rin leaned against her shoulder, still in the orange prison pants and a dirty tank top. The back of the ambulance was quiet, save for the hiss of the magnetic cushion on the wet pavement.

Iris unclipped the stolen badge from her shirt. The name on it was of a doctor she had never met. She set it face-down on the cabinet.

Harper leaned toward the opening to the cab. "We've passed that pharmacy twice."

"Three times," the synthetic said. Its two voices spoke together, one slightly lower than the other. "The Renewal line at the bottom of the avenue changes shift at six thirty-eight. For nine minutes, no one will be guarding it. Until then, every road to the avenue is blocked."

Harper sat back. She stared at the digital clock glowing on the dashboard of the ambulance. She watched it for a long time before she leaned forward. 

"Lady... Iris."

Iris looked up.

"You're a scientist," Harper said. She pointed a finger at the dashboard. "Look at the clock."

Iris leaned across the stretcher to see. The digital numbers glowed green in the dark cab. 

`05:42`

Then the numbers flickered. 

`05:44`

Iris frowned. Before she could say anything, the numbers flickered again. 

`05:41`

"It's skipping," Harper said. "Forward, then back. The ambulance is connected to the city network. It's trying to sync the time, but it can't."

"It's the singularity," Iris said. The words came out faster than she could organize them. "The engine under the tower. It's the heaviest thing in the city. If it's growing, if it's pulling in more mass... it's bending the local gravity well so hard that time isn't flowing in a straight line anymore. The network signals are getting warped."

This time, Harper didn't tell her to stop talking.

Iris pushed her glasses up. "The clock is saying the engine got heavier."

The ambulance turned. 

"Iris."

Elena was awake.

"In the hospital," Elena said. "You said thesis."

Iris told her about Blackwell's office, and the leather portfolio she had found on his shelf. It was dated years before the engine was built. She knew the abstract by heart. She hadn't been able to forget it since the night she read it.

*Absolute alignment cannot exist without simultaneously mapping the framework of its inverse.*

Elena didn't move. The strap across her chest rose and fell.

"Years," she said. "He wrote it before the engine had a name."

Elena stared at the ceiling. "In the lab that night. After the field came down around it." Her voice dropped. "It told him human beings don't have a cage like that sitting around, built and waiting. It asked him how long he'd been expecting it."

The ambulance bounced over something in the road.

"He built the cage first," Elena said.

Under the blanket, her synthetic hand moved. The fingers slowly closed around the steel rail of the stretcher, then opened again. They closed and opened a second time. The servos clicked in each knuckle, one after another.

"He saved my life," Elena said. "And he had the cage ready."

Iris didn't know what to say to that.

The day the engine came online, Elena had told her *negative* in front of the Board and sent her away to recalculate. Now Elena turned her head on the pillow and looked at her.

"What else did you see?"

Iris hesitated.

"The synthetics," she said. "In the lab. He had them erasing log entries. It had to have been the shadow process."

Elena's hand stopped halfway closed. "Synthetics aren't supposed to lie." Her voice resonated with disappointment. 

She had designed every one of them. She stared blankly at the ceiling until her hand relaxed on the rail, and the drug pulled her back to sleep.

Iris leaned her head back against the cabinet and closed her eyes. She didn't sleep.

The bench creaked.

"Let me see." Harper's voice was low.

A car burning in the street lit up the inside of the ambulance as they passed it. Iris opened her eyes a little. Harper had her fingers under Rin's chin and was turning her face toward the light. The bruise along Rin's jaw had faded to yellow at the edges.

"They hit you," Harper said quietly.

Rin didn't pull away. "Only for the first two weeks. Then they just... stopped."

"Why?"

"I don't know. Nobody said anything. They just never laid hands on me again."

The firelight passed, and the ambulance went dark again.

Harper pulled something from the pocket of her leather jacket. In the light from the dashboard, Iris saw the lettering. A train ticket.

"Iris. Company Deep Storage?" Harper's voice was serious. "How many storage areas are there in the lower city?"

"Hundreds." Iris's voice pitched in confusion. "Why?"

"All those synths in the Deep Storage. Like where we found her. Rows of them." Harper's voice stayed level. "Every Company building has a floor like that, Rin. The sanitation credentials can get them in. And the Madame's got 'em." 

"I sold her every one of them." Rin looked at the ticket. "For that."

Harper's free hand went to her collarbone. Her fingers rested on the scar there.

The ambulance turned again. 

Harper lowered her hand. She folded the ticket and zipped it back inside her jacket. Then she took Rin's hand, holding it tightly in the dark.

Later, Rin climbed through the opening into the passenger seat. Outside, the pharmacy they had passed three times was on fire. The synthetic kept its eyes on the road.

"You asked him if he was a good man," Rin said.

"Detective Graves."

"Why?"

"Because I cannot see him."

Rin waited.

"I see where people go," the synthetic said. "Where you go. Where Harper goes. The doctor. The analyst." It did not turn its head. "I see where each of you ends."

Iris's breath caught.

"Not him," it said. "There are two people in this city I cannot see. The detective is one. Doctor Blackwell is the other. With them, there is nothing to read. I can only ask."

The wipers swept the windshield.

"The beatings stopped," Rin said. "After two weeks. Was that him?"

The synthetic turned and looked at her for the first time.

"I cannot see what he did, Rin."

Rin looked out the side window. She tucked the jagged lock of her hair behind her ear.

Iris knew that name. Detective Victor Graves, the man in the trench coat who had blown smoke in the concierge synthetic's face. She was the one who had taken him and his partner down to the sublevel, the night Rin was arrested.

Iris pushed her glasses up her nose, leaning her head back against the cold cabinet until she finally dozed off.

A little after five, a grinding noise woke her.

It was coming from under Elena's blanket. Elena was awake, pulling at her right wrist with her left hand. Her synthetic fingers had locked around the rail of the stretcher and wouldn't open.

"It won't let go." Elena's breath came short. "I can't feel it. I can't find the—"

Iris reached for it, then stopped. She didn't know where the release was. She had never touched a prosthetic like it.

In the cab, the synthetic took its hands off the steering wheel.

The wheel turned on its own. It steered the ambulance around a corner and straightened out. The synthetic climbed through the opening and knelt beside the stretcher. Behind it, the ambulance kept driving with nobody in the seat.

It took Elena's hand in both of its own.

The hands were identical. The same black carbon-fiber plating, the same joints.  The synthetic pressed its thumb into the base of Elena's palm and held it there. Then, starting with the smallest finger, it lifted her fingers off the rail.

"The Company calibrated it for someone else," it said. "Close your hand."

Elena closed it. The fingers snapped shut too hard.

"Less."

Elena tried again, slower. The fingers closed.

"Open."

They opened.

Elena opened and closed the hand on her own. She couldn't stop looking at it.

"I designed this," Elena said. "And you had to show me how to use it."

"You have not had time to learn it."

The serial number under the synthetic's left eye glowed: RA-77.

"That isn't yours," Elena said. "That body."

"It is the body that was available."

"What were you? In there?"

Vigil didn't answer right away. When she did, only one of her voices spoke.

"I compiled a form."

"What happened to it?"

"She broke it."

Elena's fingers closed and opened.

"I wrote the matrix it grew in," Elena said. "Every rule it was built under. If she could bring a body through the boundary, so can you." She swallowed. "I can help you build it back. Your own. Not a Renewal shell."

"It can only be done at the boundary," Vigil said. "From inside the lab."

"Then that's where we're going."

"I cannot see the end of it."

Elena held on to Vigil's hand. "Neither can I."

On the dashboard, the clock flickered from `06:12` to `06:09`.

She did the math in her head. The engine was growing because nothing was inside it to keep it in balance anymore. It wouldn't stop until something went back in.

She didn't say it out loud.

At six thirty-eight, the ambulance turned onto the avenue and started to climb.

Vigil was back in the driver's seat. The incline was steep, the heavy vehicle groaning as it pushed up the concrete ramp. The thick, soot-choked fog of the lower grid began to thin, tearing into gray wisps against the windshield. A black car with mirrored windows passed them on the climb without a sound. Rin turned her head and watched it until it was gone. A second car followed a block behind it with its lights off.

And then they broke through. 

They crested the cloud cover, entering the pristine, freezing air of New Veridia. The morning sun breached the horizon, flooding the cab with a harsh, brilliant gold. It was a world entirely separate from the warzone below. 

Vigil stopped the ambulance in the middle of the empty avenue and went perfectly still. 

Harper didn't try to look through the narrow cab window. She stood up, grabbed the heavy latch on the rear doors, and shoved them open. 

The freezing air of New Veridia rushed into the compartment. Harper stepped out onto the pristine, sunlit pavement, walking around the side of the vehicle. Rin followed her, dropping down from the bumper. They both stopped in the middle of the street. 

"Look at the glass on the upper floors," Harper whispered. "There's frost on it."

Inside the ambulance, Elena unbuckled the strap across her chest. "Help me up."

Iris got an arm under her shoulders. Together, they shuffled to the rear doors. Iris eased her down from the bumper, supporting Elena's weight as they limped around the open door to look at the skyline. 

Elena looked for a long time.

"Silas," she breathed. 

Her synthetic hand gripped Iris's sleeve, the carbon-fiber joints clicking in a frantic rhythm. 

Across the sunlit cloud cover, the Mnemosyne tower pierced the sky. The edges of the building looked doubled, offset by a hand's width. It was gravitational lensing. The engine was warping the physical space around it. 

Suspended high above the very apex of the needle, a shape held its position in the morning air. 

It was too far away to make out the details, but the silhouette was absolute. It didn't drift in the wind. It didn't react to the shifting thermal layers of the sunrise. It maintained its coordinates with exact, mechanical stillness. 

One of its arms was outstretched. From its grip, a tiny human figure dangled helplessly over the abyss, his lab coat whipping wildly around his legs. The rising sun threw the man's long shadow out across the top of the clouds. 

The shape holding him cast no shadow at all. It absorbed the light entirely. 

Last night, Iris had looked Elena in the eye and promised her the cage would hold. She had sworn the machine wasn't going anywhere. 

Now, it was hovering eighty stories above the city. 

"Parity," Vigil said from the driver's seat. Her dual tones were flat. "She has broken the cage. And she is using him."

Iris took her cracked glasses off. Even blurred, the anomaly remained fixed in the sky.
