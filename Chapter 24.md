The single fluorescent tube overhead flickered, popping with a sharp electrical snap. Victor Graves sat still, staring across the metal table. He had been patient. He wanted the head of the Filament, and Erin Adler was the bait. If she wanted to avoid a concrete cell, she would make a deal.

The steel door to the interrogation room unlocked with a whirring mechanical clack. A synthetic guard pushed it inward. Harlan stepped through, carrying two steaming foam cups. The synthetic followed him inside, taking up a rigid position against the wall by the door. 

Harlan set a cup in front of Victor, leaning close. "Did you ask her?" he kept his voice low.

Victor took the coffee. The heat burned straight through the cheap cup. "Harlan, it's not like you to entertain this garbage." He leaned closer, dropping his pitch to a harsh rasp. "If you humor this—" Victor got up form his chair to make room for him to sit.

Harlan ignored the warning. He sat at the end of the table and stared directly at the girl. "Why were you in Deep Storage at the Takeda-Holden Corporate building?"

Rin stopped fighting the cuffs. Her eyes darted over Harlan's shoulder, lingering on the analog clock mounted on the concrete wall. 

"Don't worry," Victor said, catching her gaze. "We'll get you back to your cell soon."

Rin's shoulders dropped. She tucked the jagged sidelock of her hime-cut behind her ear. "Takeda-Holden created a singularity computer," she said flatly. She raised her right hand, her handcuffed wrist pulling tight against the chain. She tapped her index finger against the metal table.  

"They trained a super-intelligence." Her finger met the table again. "It breached the system." Another tap on the table.

Harlan didn't blink. The muscles in his jaw tightened. Victor leaned in "Come on Harlan. We want the Filament."

"The corporation built a black hole in the middle of the city, and they don't have control over it," Rin finished, shaking her head. The metal chair scraped loudly against the concrete floor as she leaned back, tapping her finger again against the table.



Victor stared at her hand. His eyes tracked the rise and fall of her index finger. It wasn't a nervous twitch. The rhythm was flawless. Precise. Exact seconds tapped out against the aluminum. 

*Tap.* 

The realization hit him like ice water in his veins. It wasn't a stall. It was a countdown. 

Time seemed to drag to a crawl as Victor slowly turned his head toward the door. 

The synthetic stood perfectly still against the wall. Victor's eyes fell to the stamp beneath its left eye. It didn't read `PD`. Oshima PD didn't employ `RA` units. 

`RA-77`. 

A Takeda-Holden Corporate killer. Inside his precinct. 

The cheap foam cup slipped from Victor's fingers. It hit the floor, bursting open and spilling scalding black coffee across the tiles. 

"Harlan!" Victor yelled, reaching for the revolver under his coat.

The synthetic didn't reach for a weapon. It simply looked at him, and its eyes ignited. 

The room didn't go dark. It went white. A blinding, incandescent flare of pure light erupted from the synthetic's eyes, searing with the intensity of a dying star. 

For Victor, it was absolute agony. His albinotic eyes, already sensitive to the cheap fluorescent bulbs, were completely overwhelmed. The world washed out in a violent, burning white. He cried out, throwing his arm over his face, completely blinded. 

The sharp snap of breaking metal echoed over the ringing in his ears. 

When the piercing light finally died down to a dull, throbbing afterimage, Victor blinked through the tearing pain. 

The interrogation room was empty. The cuffs lay snapped on the metal table. 

Null was gone.
