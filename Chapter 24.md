The single fluorescent tube overhead flickered, popping with a sharp electrical snap. Victor Graves sat still, staring across the metal table. He had been patient. He wanted the head of the Filament, and Erin Adler was the bait. If she wanted to avoid a concrete cell, she would make a deal.

The steel door to the interrogation room unlocked with a whirring mechanical clack. A synthetic wearing an Oshima police-issue black bomber jacket pushed the door inward. Harlan stepped through, carrying two steaming foam cups. The synthetic followed him inside, taking up a rigid position against the wall by the door, its eight-point cap worn low over the subtle glow of its eyes.

Harlan set a cup in front of Victor, leaning close. "Did you ask her?" he kept his voice low.

Victor took the coffee. The heat burned straight through the cheap cup. "Harlan, it's not like you to entertain this garbage." He leaned closer, dropping his pitch to a harsh rasp. "If you humor this—" Victor got up from his chair to make room for him to sit.

Harlan ignored the warning. He sat at the table and stared directly at the girl. "Why were you in Deep Storage at the Takeda-Holden Corporate building?"

Rin didn't answer.

Victor watched the fight go out of her hands. The chain slackened against the center ring. Her eyes went past Harlan's shoulder and stayed there, fixed on something at the back of the room.

He followed the line of it to the door, where the guard stood against the wall with its hands loose at its sides.

"Door's locked, kid," he said. "And you'll be back in your cell inside the hour."

Something moved through her face that Victor had spent a month trying to put there.

He leaned in and dropped his voice. "Erin. Talk to me."

She talked.

"Takeda-Holden built a singularity computer." Level, and fast, and more words than she had given him since the hangar. "They trained an intelligence inside it. It got out."

Harlan's jaw set. He didn't move.

It landed in Victor's chest like a win. Four weeks of the folder and the pacing and the same six questions, and here it came, over cheap coffee.

"Keep going."

"They built a black hole in the middle of the city." Her eyes came back to Harlan. Then went past him again. "And they don't have control over it."

Victor turned his head to catch Harlan's face and caught the mirror instead. The far wall handed him the room in reverse. Himself, bent over the table. Harlan at the end of it. The girl in orange between them. His focus drawn by the door, the guard — its head angled down toward her. Its mouth was moving.

No sound came out. The lips shaped the words anyway, slow, careful, a thing working out how to use a face.

And they don't have control over it.

"—and they don't have control over it," Rin said.

Victor turned around.

The guard hadn't shifted since it took the wall. Shoulders squared, hands loose, weight held with a patience no living body manages. It was looking at the girl. Only at the girl. It had been looking at her since it walked in behind his partner's coffee.

His eyes went to the stamp beneath its left eye.

Not  a `PD` serial designation. The serial glowed `RA-77`.

Oshima PD didn't run Renewal units. Oshima PD couldn't afford one.

He felt the hot cup leave his fingers. It hit the tile and split, and black coffee ran out across the grout in a spreading sheet.

Four weeks. A fake charge, a name that wasn't hers on the booking sheet, a cell in the back of a precinct too busy with riots to ask questions. Four weeks of standing between this kid and the Company — and the Company had walked right through the door.

They had found her. They'd sent one of their own to close the file.

Victor put himself between the wall and the table, his back to the girl, and pulled the revolver from under his coat. The rails woke against his palm, a charge crawling up into his wrist.

"Harlan. Get down."

The lights overhead popped and quit. The precinct went with it. Every light on the floor, the intake board, the terminals, gone. Out in the corridor shouting, chairs falling over, boots hammering toward the stairwell in the dark.

Inside the ten-by-ten interrogation room, there was no light. The red indicator on his revolver, and the dim glow of the `RA` unit's eyes and serial in the room.

"Harlan, that isn't a house unit!"

The guard stepped off the wall. Victor had seen that walk before, on a fifteenth-floor landing, on a squad that climbed the whole way up without taking a breath. Nothing in the hips. Nothing in the shoulders. Movement taking the shortest distance available to it.

Victor trained his revolver between the synthetic's brow. "Stop right there, canner."

It turned its head toward him without hurrying. The synthetic's expression wasn't generic, there was a soulish feature hidden in the eyes. It had different eyes. It quickly reached out and grabbed the barrel of the revolver, causing the magnetic rails to spark and overheat in his hand. 

"Ah, shit!" He dropped the weapon to the floor.

He could feel the synthetic's gaze scanning him. Then it spoke, "You are an 'Obscurity' in the timeline, Victor Graves. You stand outside readable futures. You are an ambiguity. Tell me, are you a good man, Victor Graves?" The synthetic's tone was different from what Victor had heard millions of times before. Victor couldn't answer, his words gone.

Its eyes lit up a brilliant light with flares of blue. The flash of light went through his eyelids. So bright it took the table, the girl and his partner and turned it all into a blinding white. 

He went down on the wet tile with both hands over his eyes, and the white came down with him.

He could hear the sound of the synthetic changing position. The chains drawing tight against the ring on the table. Then the violent crack of the metal ring breaking into pieces.

Victor reached out, grasping into the brightness. His hand closed on a wrist. Warm, a pulse going hard under his fingers, the sleeve of a jumpsuit a size too big bunching into his fist.

He had her. He couldn't see but he had her, and he held on with everything he had left in him.

She didn't pull. She leaned down instead, close enough that he felt the breath of it against his ear, and she wasn't afraid.

"I told you," Rin said. "They don't have control."

She rolled her wrist against his thumb — the one direction a grip won't hold — and she was out of his hand.

Victor's eyes adjusted to the room. Harlan was already at the door. Out in the corridor, the shouting changed. Confusion. Frustration. Victor reached the door too. In the bullpen, the intake synthetic unit, the pair of synths on the holding gate, the clerk in evidence. Every machine on the floor came off standby inside the same moment.

A man yelled at one to move.

It didn't move. That was the part that made the unruly situation even stranger. Fifty officers in the dark, and not one machine had laid a hand on anybody. They had walked to the places where they would be most in the way, and stopped.

Victor watched his 'confession' moving through the crowd. The precinct synths were making a lane.

Four weeks he had sat across from her. Four weeks of working her down, waiting on the day she broke and handed him the Madame. He'd watched it happen an hour ago.  She had never been breaking. She'd been waiting on her ride, and he had been the one keeping her warm until it came.
