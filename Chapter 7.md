Victor walked down the peeling  seafoam hallway of the long-term care facility. The blue and yellow checkered linoleum under foot. The fluorescent lights overhead buzzed with a dying electrical hum. He stopped at the front desk, dragging a chained pen across the visitor log. He looked up at the nurse behind the desk, "Victor Graves, for Arthur Graves". 

He continued down the corridor until he reached a degraded wooden door painted green. An arrangement of fake blue flowers sat collecting dust in a cheap vase beside the frame. Victor adjusted the collar of his trench coat and pushed the door open. 

The old man sitting in the faded, patched corduroy, armchair didn't look at him. His white hair was parted perfectly to the side, while clouded eyes darted between the cracked window and the doorway, trailing unseen threats. 

"Close it," Arthur whispered, his voice dry and trembling. He gripped the armrests of his chair. "You gotta keep an eye —" He pointed his wrinkled, tremoring finger toward the window. "There is an expectation here, Victor.  The Madame don't just trust anyone ya know." 

Victor sighed, stepping further into the room. "Dad, you're not working anymore." 

Arthur’s head snapped toward him, his expression twisting into sudden, fierce anger. "You don't know what you're sayin'! You want to get us both gutted? Watch the damned window!" 

Victor held his hands up, instantly retreating. He didn't want to fight the ghost of his father. He pulled a plastic chair across from him and sat down directly in front of the glass. "Alright, Pop. I'll watch the window." 

Victor stared out at the smog-choked skyline of the lower city. The Filament hadn't killed his father in a blaze of glory. They had wrung every drop of usefulness out of him until his mind broke, and then discarded the hollowed-out shell in a cheap room. 

A minutes later, Victor stood up and walked back out into the hall. Harlan was waiting by the degraded wooden door, his eyes resting on the fake blue flowers. Harlan didn't ask if the visit went well. He already knew. 

"Is she's not worth it, Vic?" Harlan said, keeping his voice low as they walked back down the peeling seafoam corridor. "You know, we can't save everyone the Filament touches."

Victor tightened his jaw. "They hollowed him out. —Not going to let it happen to anyone else."

***

Victor rolled his thumb along the striker of his lighter, sparking a flame against the end of his cigarette. He exhaled, adding to the low-hanging smoke of the busy bullpen. The precinct was loud. Exhausted beat cops in wet bomber jackets pushed past his desk, shaking off the freezing rain. They were trailed by a precinct synthetic, the blue `PD-09` serial stamp glowing faintly beneath its unblinking eye. Up front, a crowd shouted over the intake counter—desperate people demanding answers for the Renewal sweeps in lower grids.

"It doesn't fit." His words barely cut through the noise.

Harlan leaned forward, resting his elbows on the metal desk. "I told you to buy your jacket off the rack, not the bin," he said with a slight smirk.

Victor leaned back in his chair, ignoring the joke. He crossed his arms, letting the smoke spiral. "Null's M.O." he reached forward and crushed the half-smoked cigarette into the overflowing ashtray. 

Leaning his chin against his hand, Victor stared at the cluttered desk. "Null usually doesn't stick around." 

Harlan’s smirk vanished. "Null 'stuck around'," he paused, his expression hardening and with a serious tone, "—to shoot tear gas at my face. Remember?"

Victor shook his head at the complaint. "Null tripped a silent alarm because she was digging too deep." He pulled a thick folder onto the desk. "I got the terminal logs from the office Null hacked. Doctor Silas Blackwell." 

A sudden commotion erupted in the room as officers dragged a group of struggling demonstrators across the floor. Victor kept his focus on the papers. 

"It’s heavily redacted," Victor said, his eyes scanning the thick black lines. "Except for this: *Mnemosyne*." He waited to see if he had Harlan’s attention. His partner remained silent. "The log has a second name in the metadata," Victor continued. "Doctor Elena Marlowe."

Harlan raised an eyebrow. "Marlowe? The cybernetics prodigy?" 

Victor knew the name. He knew her contributions had revolutionized the current generation of synthetics before suddenly vanishing from the headlines. She was the reason the city was crawling with them—the reason you couldn't walk down a street without some unblinking canner looking you over.

"Vic, she hasn't been seen public-side in five years," Harlan said.

"Because the Company buried her down there to build... whatever this thing is," Victor replied.

Harlan shifted in his seat, his posture relaxing. "Well, there's our answer. Takeda-Holden private data... That's a Renewal Agent's job. Let them deal with the headache." 

A second later, Harlan tensed.

Victor slammed his fist against the desk, the sound cutting through the noise of the room. "No. Null is mine. This is how we get the Filament. Whatever this *Mnemo—thing* is, Null stole the keys."

The bullpen fell silent at the outburst. Victor ignored the stares, letting his temper cool as his focus narrowed. 

"Time we paid Doctor Blackwell a visit."
