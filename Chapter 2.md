  Rain lashed against the grit of the Dead Circuits. The lower city was a broken memory of what used to be, buried in the shadows of the "Towers", the daunting arcologies of the city. The brutalist structures of the upper grid disappeared into the thick fog, their windowless foundations looming overhead. 
  
  The atmosphere was bitter, and concentrated with exhaust. Despite the rain, the morning sun struggled to pierce the smog—sudden, random columns of tarnished light that moved across the wet streets. Under the convective sun-shower, droves of people moved like a slow, dark river beneath a canopy of black umbrellas. Every human face beneath them disappeared behind overused surgical masks, no longer sterile, and stained from filtering the air. Only the synthetics walked barefaced. The freezing rain beaded off their vinyl skin, while the luminescent serial codes stamped beneath their eyes cut through the fog.

   Battered Takeda-Holden surveillance cameras clung to the steel poles of the intersections, their casings dented from thrown rocks, lenses tracking the crowds.

  Rising from the center of the old blocks was a Company office building. A temporary, security barricade had been erected on the ground floor to protect the building's lower half from the possibility of uneasy gatherings. The monolith structure was a pristine slab of mirrored, tinted glass towering over the broken city. A holographic billboard clung to its facade, bleeding blue static into the smog. It advertised: *Takeda-Holden*.
  
  Fifteen floors up, isolated from the decay and past the fried security locks of the maintenance stairwell, Rin stood in the company hallway, pulling wires from an office's magnetic lock.
  
The corridor was otherwise silent. Rin had done her research; she had found the perfect floor. For whatever reason, the executive suites on floor 15 had been unattended for weeks. 

The cool air conditioning filled her lungs, and the sharp scent of chemical cleaner sat high in her nose. At the far end of the hall, the polished doors of the elevator stood jammed open, propped by a heavy steel wrench, wedged into the floor track to keep the car from being called away.

A sudden flash of blue light reflected off the hallway walls. Rin jerked backward, dodging the hidden retinal scanner. Her eyes widened as the sweeping laser beam searched for its missing data. 

She held her breath until the beam faded. A second later, she crossed the two stripped wires to bypass the scan and pulled a crude, wire-wrapped single-board computer from her jacket. She held the SBC against the lock's near-field communication display. Its green crystal screen flickered to life, scrolling rapidly, stopping on a hash of pixelated letters, and numbers.

The magnetic lock disengaged with a hollow snap. She ran her fingers the length of the letters engraved on the smooth plaque attached to the center of the door, *Dr. Silas L. Blackwell, Ph.D Sc.D*.

Tucking the jagged sidelock of her black hime-cut bob behind her ear, she slipped through the doorway, stepping into the muted silence of an executive suite. Water dripped from her oversized leather jacket onto the floor around her loosely tied combat boots. She navigated the darkness, making her way towards a polished desk in the far corner. 

As she crossed the center of the office, the window overlooking the lower city horizon flared to life as a surge of blue pixels flowed from one end of the glass to the other. The office was immersed in a blue and white light as the pixels converged into streaming columns of information. She approached the terminal sitting on the desk.

She didn't admire the view. The data streaming across the holographic glass represented sheer excess. Rin jammed the connection wire of her SBC into the main console. 

*Just need enough credits to put someone on a train out of the Dead Circuits,* she thought.

The terminal awoke, projecting a Takeda-Holden Corporation login screen. A minimal, white screen with black text.

*Company employee: RD001, S. Blackwell* .

Lines of access data spooled across the screen of the SBC, mirroring the forced terminal window on the workstation's display. The malicious code initiated a brute-force assault. The program she named after her own alias: *Null*. She waited impatiently for the firewall to break. 

Down the hall, the elevator doors tried to close on the steel wrench for the fifth time. *Clunk-ding.* The metal doors reopened.

The SBC chirped, and the login on the terminal screen flashed simultaneously, granting access to the executive files. 

*Gotcha.* Her focus snapped to the panel.

She dived straight into the personnel directories. Her target was generic janitorial and sanitation credentials—the most worthless basic access to the Company's Deep Storage facilities that were buried under the city. It was the price demanded by "The Filament," the cartel who ran the Dead Circuits. She had run jobs for them before. Never this far up into Company territory. 

The file architecture was dense, built to deliberately slow data extraction.

"C'mon!" she hissed in frustration. The download was crawling. 

As the authorizations copied to her drive, a folder in Blackwell's directory caught her eye: *Main Fusion Logs*. She opened the folder; it was full of power consumption logs. Each file incrementing in size. The numbers revealed a catastrophic, sustained hemorrhage of the city's fusion reactor that had been running continuously for weeks.

 She found and opened an operations file:*Mnemo_1.opt*. The screen flooded with classified schematics for a program called *Project Mnemosyne*. At the center of the complex data was a wireframe of a laboratory tower. Rin stopped, her eyes darting from the small screen to the window behind her. 

The blue holographic data on the glass was a scaled projection of the exact same tower schematic.

Rin moved away from the desk. She stepped back until the wireframe on the glass lined up with the skyline behind it. The projection perfectly eclipsed a building in the distance. Whatever Blackwell was monitoring, it was in that tower, and it had been tearing at the city's power grid for the better part of a month.

Before she could dig deeper, the authorization download completed. Rin rooted in her jacket pocket and pulled out a red thumb drive. Down the hall, the elevator jammed against the wrench once more, chiming as the doors recoiled. Rin froze with the drive in her palm, scanning the indecipherable symbols and diagrams, contemplating the repercussions of taking more than she had originally come for. 

She connected the red drive to the terminal. Her fingers instinctively hooked the loose strand of hair back behind her ear before she hammered the key to start the copy.

The fluorescent lights of the maintenance stairwell flickered, casting active shadows against the concrete. 

"Fifteen floors up with no elevator, Victor?" Harlan gasped, loosening the tie of his grey suit. He leaned against the metal railing to catch his breath. "You sure it's Null this time?"

Victor didn't slow his ascent. His black coat whipped at his ankles with each quick step. He kept climbing. At the landing he turned and wiped the mess of white hair from his eyeline. He dismissed the familiar sting of the fluorescent lighting searing into his Albinotic eyes. 

"Yeah," Victor said, his voice steady. "Not losin' this lead, Harlan. Best we got to the Filament. Null won't get past me again."

"If we don't have a heart attack first," Harlan said, pushing off the railing to keep up. 

"We gotta get to Null before Corporate Renewal," Victor said, his gaze fixed on the landing above. "If Takeda-Holden finds them first, months of work will have been for nothing." Victor turned back, looking at Harlan on the landing below him. "And we want those mafia bastards."

Victor reached the steel fire-door of the fifteenth-floor landing. The security lock was fried, a scorched mess of exposed wires. He didn't wait. Shoving the fire door open, he swept his weapon into the sterile hallway—an augmented revolver resembling a relic snatched from another century. The gun's retrofitted magnetic rails vibrated with a low charge, and a red indicator light blinked, signifying its readiness to fire. The rails woke against his palm, a charge crawling up into his wrist. 

Victor stepped onto the landing in silence. At the opposite end of the hall, an executive suite door stood ajar, blue light bleeding through the gap. Behind him, the jammed elevator doors tried to close against a steel wrench. *Clunk-ding.* Victor noted the noise, his attention drawn quickly to the opened door. Through the opening, in the suite, he could see a section of the office's picture window; the glowing blue wireframe projections.

Harlan burst through the stairwell door a second later, his weapon raised. "Jesus, Victor! You're being reckless—"

"Quiet," Victor murmured, raising his index finger towards Harlan, then pointing to the open door. Following the signal, Harlan snapped his focus to the end of the hall. The two detectives advanced down the corridor, closing the distance to the executive suite.

***

Inside the office, a low tone from the workstation terminal signaled the completed data transfer. 

Rin perked up, the sound of muffled voices from the hallway cutting through the muted silence of the office. Keeping her eyes on the door, she reached down, feeling for the red thumb drive. She pulled it from the console, her fingers closing tightly around the warm metal.

She pocketed the drive, her gaze shifting back to the window, then again to the ajar executive doors. 

Her fingers curled around a cylinder in the pocket of her leather jacket. She pulled it free into the blue light of the office. It was a high-yield CS gas canister, matte color with a faded yellow band painted around it. She held it in her palm. It had taken seven people to drag a hulking Renewal agent to the ground long enough for the canister to snap loose from its belt—a bloody memory she tried to forget. Rin ran her thumb over the rusted safety pin, staring at the small explosive as she calculated what she was about to do.

With her free hand, she pulled a low-profile rebreather mask from her collar. It was specialized, far more advanced than the stained surgical masks worn by the crowds in the Dead Circuits below. She pulled the thick straps over her dark hair, the rubber sealing tightly around her nose and mouth with a faint hiss of pressurized air. 

Rin hooked her finger through the grenade's metal ring.

***

Out in the hall, Victor tightened his grip on his revolver, stepping toward the office door. Harlan sidestepped beside him, carefully pulling back the slide of his pistol. The metal clicked, and he guided the slide softly forward to chamber a round, then gave Victor a tight nod.

But before they could breach the office, the familiar sound echoed through the hallway. *Clunk-ding*.

Only this time, the metallic ring was hollow and slightly out of tune.

A canister with a faded yellow band had rolled to a stop against the toe of his standard-issue dress shoes, having bounced off the metal doorframe of the office.

He felt the abrupt, hardened force of Harlan grabbing his coat with both hands, twisting him backward as they fell against the wall. 

The canister erupted with a violent hiss. Thick, choking white smoke instantly flooded the narrow hallway, creating a dense wall of fog between the detectives and the office. 

Victor propped himself up to one knee, coughing as the acrid chemical burned his sensitive eyes. He raised his weapon, squinting into the blinding cloud. 

Through the haze, a lithe, feminine silhouette emerged. Backlit by the blue light of the office, refracting through the fog and stretching into long rays around her. 

Before Victor could blink through the pain and adjust his aim, she was moving. She didn't engage. Slipping past them, sprinting down the corridor. 

"She's running!" Harlan choked out, stumbling blindly against the hallway wall. 

Victor surged to his feet, shaking off the burning in his eyes, but she was too fast. He watched through the clearing smoke as she reached the end of the hall, sliding seamlessly into the elevator car. She kicked the heavy wrench from the floor track with her boot, freeing the doors from their open state. 

Victor reached the elevator a second later, his stare centered through the final sliver of the closing doors. 

Victor and the girl's eyes met for the first time through the closing gap.

He set his gaze on her, unyielding. Locked onto her. He ignored the tearing pain of the smoke—this was no longer only about the Filament.

The doors sealed shut. Null was gone. 

Victor and Harlan stumbled backward, breaking through the fire door and collapsing into the stairwell, heaving desperate breaths of cleaner air. There was no way to get to the bottom floor in time to catch the elevator as it arrived.

Harlan leaned heavily against the concrete wall, his lungs burning as he gasped for air. "God... damn it!" he wheezed. "Did you... did you get a good look at her?"

Harlan choked on another dry, burning gasp. Victor didn't look away from the fire door. In his mind, the split-second of eye contact looped again: the sharp eyes, the feminine silhouette backlit by the office light. 

"I did," Victor said, catching his breath.

Before Harlan could say another word, the synchronized tread of tactical boots echoed up the stairwell. A harsh red hue bounced off the concrete walls below them, bleeding upward before the squad even appeared.

A squad of Takeda-Holden Renewal Agents marched onto the landing. They moved with mechanical precision, clad in sleek, form-fitting black tactical armor. 

Harlan grabbed Victor’s arm, his voice dropping to a harsh whisper. "Victor... they just climbed fifteen flights. They aren't even breathing."

Their humanity was obscured by glossy, faceless helmets. A single, vertical slice of heated crimson cut down the center of the black glass, still flaring from their climb up the stairwell. 

The lead agent didn't speak. He stepped directly into Victor's personal space, towering over him. The red seam down the center of his face pulsed as it scanned Harlan's upheld Oshima police badge. The agent then snapped a deliberately bright tactical light onto Victor's face, burning directly into his sensitive eyes.

Victor was still holding his revolver. The entire squad shifted towards him in response. A silent, lethal threat.

Victor made a point of slowly dropping his gun into his holster and raising his hands, so as not to instigate them. He winced, blinking through the blinding light. "Oh, look," he said, masking a spike of adrenaline. "The cavalry finally showed up. Just in time, as always."

He didn't argue further. He stepped aside, letting the silent, black-armored squad push past them to secure the executive suite.

"We're done here, Harlan," Victor said, turning to head down the stairs. 

Harlan pushed himself off the wall, wiping a streak of sweat and soot from the dark skin of his forehead. "What's so important that Null would risk so much to access?"

"'What' indeed, Harlan," Victor started the long descent back down to the Dead Circuits. He paused on the first step, his eyes narrowing. "But the name on the office she just hit... said Doctor Silas Blackwell."

Harlan frowned, staring after him into the stairwell. "Blackwell?"
