  Rain lashed against the grit of the Dead Circuits. The lower city was a broken memory of what used to be, buried in the shadows of the "Towers"—the massive arcologies of the city. Looking up from the flooded streets invited vertigo. The brutalist structures of the upper grid disappeared into the thick fog, their windowless foundations looming overhead like the legs of iron giants. The atmosphere was bitter, heavy and thick with exhaust. Droves of people moved like a slow, dark river beneath a canopy of black umbrellas. Every face beneath them disappeared behind overused surgical masks, no longer sterile, and stained black from filtering the air. 

  Rising from the center of the old buildings was a Company data hub. Unlike the rusted slums surrounding it, the monolith structure was a pristine slab of mirrored, black glass. A holographic billboard clung to its perfect facade, bleeding blue static into the smog. It advertised: *Saito Takeda*. 
  
  Fifteen floors up, isolated from the decay and past the fried security locks of the maintenance stairwell, a lone figure stood in the sterile hallway, pulling wires from an office's magnetic lock. 

The corridor was otherwise silent, smelling of oxidizing disinfectants and recycled air. At the far end of the hall, the polished doors of the elevator stood jammed open, propped by a heavy steel wrench, wedged into the floor track to keep the car from being called away. 

  A sudden flash of blue light reflected off the narrow hallway walls. Rin jerked backward, only just dodging the hidden retinal scanner. Her eyes widened as she watched the sweeping laser beam search for its missing retinal data. She held her breath until the beam faded. A second later, she crossed the two stripped wires to bypass the scanner and swiped her forged personnel card, illuminating the lock's near-field communication display.

The magnetic lock disengaged with a hollow thud. 

She slipped through the threshold, stepping into the muted silence of the executive suite. She was young, barely in her twenties, with pale skin that starkly contrasted her sharp, asymmetrical dark hair. Her features were delicate but hardened by the grit of the lower city. A messy fringe of bangs hung across her piercing blue eyes, which were alert and constantly calculating her surroundings. Water dripped from her oversized, heavy leather jacket, pooling on the polished floor around her loosely tied combat boots. She navigated the darkness, making her way towards a sleek desk in the far corner. 

As she crossed the center of the office, the room's massive window flared to life, a wave of blue pixels cascading across the reinforced glass. The sudden illumination bathed the office in a blue and white caustic luminescence, as the pixels coalesced into streaming columns of telemetry data. She approached the terminal sitting on the polished black glass inlay of the wooden desk, ignoring the holographic overlay of the Dead Circuits sprawling fifteen floors below.

She didn't admire the view. The telemetry data streaming across the glass represented sheer excess—idle processing power that could keep the Dead Circuits heated through the winter. Rin pulled a black single-board controller from her jacket, jamming its connection wire into the main console. 

The terminal awoke, projecting a secure Saito Takeda Corporation login screen in stark text. 

Her fingers crawled across the mechanical keyboard. *Hello Company employee: RD001, S. Blackwell.*

Lines of access data spooled across the tiny screen of the SBC in her hand, mirroring the forced terminal window on the workstation's display. Malicious code compiled the trojan, initiating a brute-force assault, its digital signature masked behind her alias: *Null*. The encryption fought back, a rotating cipher that chewed through her algorithms. She typed furiously, manually guiding the breach until the firewall finally splintered, granting access to the executive files. 

*Gotcha.* Her eyes locked on the sprawling data.

She bypassed the personal directories, diving straight into the infrastructure sub-networks. Her target was the municipal routing keys—the price 'The Filament' demanded to keep the lower grids online. But the network architecture was dense, built like a labyrinth to deliberately slow data extraction. 

"C'mon!" she hissed in frustration. The download was crawling. 

As the routing keys slowly copied to her drive, a hidden sub-process in Blackwell's directory caught her eye. It was a power consumption log. The numbers didn't make sense. It wasn't a random spike; it was a sustained hemorrhage of the city's fusion reactor that had been running continuously for weeks. 

She opened the file path. The screen flooded with classified schematics for something called a *Mnemosyne Engine*. At the center of the complex data was a wireframe of a massive laboratory tower. Rin stopped, her eyes darting from the small screen to the massive window behind her. 

The blue telemetry data on the glass wasn't just random diagnostics. It was a scaled holographic projection of the exact same tower schematic.  

Rin stepped backward in the room, moving away from the desk until the glowing wireframe perfectly eclipsed a structure looming in the distant city skyline. It was monitoring an anomaly; localized entirely within that specific building— that had been tearing at the city's power grid for the better part of a month.

Before she could dig deeper, the power routing keys download completed. Rin rooted in her jacket pocket and pulled out a red thumb drive. She stopped for a moment, scanning the indecipherable symbols and diagrams, contemplating the repercussions of taking more than she had originally come for. 

*This will take time.*

She connected the red drive to the terminal. Her fingers instinctively found the jagged sidelock of dark hair that always managed to fall into her face when she worked. She hooked it tightly behind her ear—a familiar, nervous tick that betrayed the calm, professional facade she tried to maintain. Blowing a damp fringe out of her eyeline, she hammered the key to start the copy.

She needed to finish her work before her luck ran out. 

***

The fluorescent lights of the maintenance stairwell flickered, casting erratic shadows against the concrete. 

"Fifteen floors up with no elevator, Victor?" Harlan gasped, loosening the tie of his grey suit. He leaned against the metal railing to catch his breath. "You sure it's Null this time?"

Victor didn't slow his ascent. His black coat whipped at his ankles with each precise step. Overhead, the lights came up hard off the polished concrete of the stairwell. His eyes watered under the glare. He didn't blink it away. He kept climbing. The stark light caught his white hair when he turned at the landing, went through his eyes and came back red. He turned again, and it was gone.

"Positive, Harlan," Victor said, his voice steady, betraying no exertion. "We've been hunting them for months. They won't get past me again."

"If we don't have a heart attack first," Harlan muttered, pushing off the railing to keep up. 

"We have to intercept Null before Corporate Renewal does," Victor said, his gaze fixed on the landing above. "If Saito Takeda's enforcers find them first, we lose our only lead. And Null loses their life."

Victor reached the heavy door of the fifteenth-floor landing. The security lock was fried, a scorched mess of exposed wires. He didn't wait. Shoving the fire door open, he swept his weapon into the sterile hallway—a massive revolver that looked like a relic from another century. Its augmented magnetic rails hummed with a low charge, a red indicator light blinking on the side to signify its readiness to fire. 

"Oshima Police Depart—" 

Victor stopped dead. 

At the opposite end of the hall, the executive suite door stood ajar. Victor squinted at the violent, erratic blue light bleeding through the gap. Through the opening, he could see a section of the office's massive window. It was consumed by a glowing blue wireframe projection that perfectly outlined a towering, brutalist building in the distant city skyline.

Harlan burst through the stairwell door a second later, his weapon raised. "Jesus, Victor! You're being reckless—"

"Quiet," Victor murmured. The two detectives advanced several meters down the sterile corridor, closing the distance to the executive suite.

***

Inside the office, the terminal chimed a low tone. The massive data transfer had completed. 

Rin perked up, the abrupt shout from the hallway cutting through the hum of the cooling fans. She pulled the red thumb drive from the console, her fingers closing tightly around the warm metal. 

*Police?* she realized. *Better than Renewal.*

She pocketed the drive, her gaze shifting from the massive window to the ajar executive doors. 

*Time to go.*

Her fingers curled around a cylinder in the pocket of her leather jacket. She pulled it free into the blue light of the office. It was a high-yield CS gas canister, matte black with a faded yellow band painted around it. She held it in her palm. Rin ran her thumb over the rusted safety pin, staring at the small explosive for a fraction of a second as she calculated what she was about to do. 

With her free hand, she pulled a low-profile rebreather mask from her collar. It was a specialized filtration unit, far more advanced than the stained surgical masks worn by the crowds in the Dead Circuits below. She pulled the thick straps over her dark hair, the rubber sealing tightly around her nose and mouth with a faint hiss of pressurized air. 

Rin hooked her finger through the grenade's metal ring.

Out in the hall, Victor tightened his grip on his heavy revolver, stepping toward the door to breach the office. But before he could, a black canister with a yellow band rolled out from the gap, coming to a dead stop exactly at the tips of his standard-issue dress shoes. 

Victor and Harlan froze. They looked down at the grenade, then slowly looked at each other. 

"Gas," Harlan deadpanned. 

They scrambled backward just as the canister erupted with a violent hiss. Thick, choking white smoke instantly flooded the narrow hallway, creating a dense wall of fog between the detectives and the office. 

Victor dropped to one knee, coughing as the acrid chemical burned his sensitive eyes. He kept his weapon raised, squinting into the blinding white cloud. 

Through the haze, a lithe, feminine silhouette emerged. She was entirely backlit by the violent blue light of the office, the intense luminescence refracting through the fog and stretching into long rays around her like the fractured streaks of an astigmatism. 

Before Victor could blink through the pain and adjust his aim, she was moving. She didn't engage. She simply slipped past them like a ghost, sprinting down the sterile corridor. 

"She's running!" Harlan choked out, stumbling blindly against the hallway wall. 

Victor surged to his feet, ignoring the burning in his eyes, but she was too fast. He watched through the clearing smoke as she reached the end of the hall, sliding seamlessly into the elevator car. She kicked the heavy wrench from the floor track with her boot, freeing the doors from their open state. 

Victor reached the elevator a second later, his gun trained through the final sliver of the closing doors. For a moment, Victor and Rin's eyes met for the first time. Rin startled at the stern, focused glare of Victor's stark colorless eyes, his expression unyielding despite the tearing pain from the smoke. 

The doors sealed shut. Null was gone. 

Victor and Harlan stumbled backward, breaking through the fire door and collapsing into the stairwell, heaving desperate breaths of cleaner air. There was no way to get to the bottom floor in time to catch the elevator as it arrived.

Harlan leaned heavily against the concrete wall, his lungs burning as he gasped for air. "God... damn it!" he wheezed. "Did you... did you get a good look at her?"

Victor remained silent for a moment, his red, bloodshot eyes fixed on the heavy metal of the fire door. He replayed the split-second of eye contact in his mind. The sharp eyes. The striking, feminine silhouette backlit by the bright anomaly data. 

"I did," Victor said, finally catching his breath.

Before Harlan could say another word, the heavy, synchronized thud of tactical boots echoed up the stairwell. A squad of Saito Takeda Renewal Agents marched onto the landing. They moved with terrifying, mechanical precision, clad in sleek, form-fitting black tactical armor. 

Harlan grabbed Victor’s arm, his voice dropping to a harsh whisper. "Victor... they just climbed fifteen flights. They aren't even breathing."

Their humanity was obscured by glossy, faceless black helmets that reflected the harsh fluorescent light of the stairwell. The lead agent didn't speak. It simply stepped directly into Victor's personal space, towering over him. Victor could see his own pale reflection in the black glass. The agent shone a deliberately bright tactical light, scanning Harlan's upheld Oshima police badge before snapping the light, to burn directly into Victor's sensitive eyes.

Victor was still holding his heavy revolver. The entire squad shifted their weapons a fraction of an inch in response. A silent, lethal threat.

Victor made a point of slowly, deliberately holstering the massive gun so as not to instigate them. He winced, blinking through the blinding light. "Oh, look," he muttered, his sarcastic tone masking a spike of genuine adrenaline. "The cavalry finally showed up. Just in time, as always."

He didn't argue further. He just stepped aside, letting the silent, black-armored squad push past them to secure the executive suite. 

"We're done here, Harlan," Victor said, turning to head down the stairs. 

Harlan pushed himself off the wall, wiping a streak of sweat and soot from the dark skin of his forehead. "What's so important that Null would risk so much to access?"

"'What' indeed, Harlan," Victor muttered, starting the long descent back down to the Dead Circuits. He paused on the first step, his eyes narrowing. "But the name on the office she just hit... said Doctor S. Blackwell."

Harlan frowned, staring after him into the stairwell. "Blackwell?"
