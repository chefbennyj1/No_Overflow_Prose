  Rain lashed against the grit of the Dead Circuits. The lower city was a broken memory of what used to be, buried in the shadows of the "Towers", the daunting arcologies of the city. The brutalist structures of the upper grid disappeared into the thick fog, their windowless foundations looming overhead like the legs of iron giants. 
  
  The atmosphere was bitter, and thick with exhaust. The morning sun struggled to pierce the smog, washing the streets in a pale, tarnished light. Droves of people moved like a slow, dark river beneath a canopy of black umbrellas. Every human face beneath them disappeared behind overused surgical masks, no longer sterile, and stained black from filtering the air. Only the synthetics walked barefaced. The freezing rain beaded off their vinyl skin, while the luminescent serial codes stamped beneath their eyes cut through the fog.

  Rising from the center of the old blocks was a Company office building. The monolith structure was a pristine slab of mirrored, black glass towering over the broken city. A holographic billboard clung to its facade, bleeding blue static into the smog. It advertised: *Takeda-Holden*.
  
  Fifteen floors up, isolated from the decay and past the fried security locks of the maintenance stairwell, Rin stood in the company hallway, pulling wires from an office's magnetic lock.
The corridor was otherwise silent. Rin had done her homework; she had found the perfect floor. For whatever reason, the executive suite had been left completely unattended for weeks. The cool air conditioning filled her lungs, and the sharp scent of chemical cleaner sat high in her nose, building the pressured tears of an unsneezable sneeze. At the far end of the hall, the polished doors of the elevator stood jammed open, propped by a heavy steel wrench, wedged into the floor track to keep the car from being called away.

A sudden flash of blue light reflected off the hallway walls. Rin jerked backward, narrowly dodging the hidden retinal scanner. Her eyes widened as the sweeping laser beam searched for its missing data. She held her breath until the beam faded. A second later, she crossed the two stripped wires to bypass the scan and pulled a crude, wire-wrapped single-board computer from her jacket. She held the exposed motherboard against the lock's near-field communication display. The green crystal screen on the SBC flickered to life, scrolling rapidly, stopping on a hash of pixelated letters, and numbers.

The magnetic lock disengaged with a hollow thud. 

She ran her fingers along the messy fringe of bangs that hung across her eyes, tucking the jagged sidelock of her black hime-cut bob behind her ear. She slipped through the doorway, stepping into the muted silence of an executive suite. Water dripped from her oversized, heavy leather jacket onto the floor around her loosely tied combat boots. She navigated the darkness, making her way towards a sleek desk in the far corner. 

As she crossed the center of the office, the window overlooking the lower city horizon flared to life as a wave of blue pixels flowed across the reinforced glass. The sudden illumination bathed the office in a blue and white caustic light, as the pixels coalesced into streaming columns of information. She approached the terminal sitting on the desk.

She ignored the window's holographic display, and didn't admire the view. The data streaming across the glass represented sheer excess.  Rin pulled the black single-board controller from her jacket, jamming its connection wire into the main console. 

The terminal awoke, projecting a  Takeda-Holden Corporation login screen. A minimal, white screen with black text.

Her fingers hit the 'enter' key of the mechanical keyboard. *Hello Company employee: RD001, S. Blackwell.*

Lines of access data spooled across the tiny screen of the SBC in her hand, mirroring the forced terminal window on the workstation's display. Malicious code compiled a trojan, initiating a brute-force assault, its digital signature masked behind her alias: *Null*. The encryption fought back, a rotating cipher that worked against her algorithms. She waited impatiently for the firewall to give way. 

Down the hall, the elevator doors tried to close on the heavy steel wrench for the fifth time. *Clunk... ding.* The metal doors reopened.

The SBC chirped, and the login on the terminal screen flashed simultaneously, granting access to the executive files. 

*Gotcha.* Her focus snapped to the panel.

She bypassed the personal directories, diving straight into the infrastructure sub-networks. Her target was municipal electrical access—the price demanded by "The Filament," the cartel who ran the Dead Circuits. The network architecture in the company files was dense, built to deliberately slow data extraction.

"C'mon!" she hissed in frustration. The download was crawling. 

As the routing keys slowly copied to her drive, a folder in Blackwell's directory caught her eye: *Main Fusion Logs*. She opened the folder; it was full of power consumption logs. The current log file was growing at regular increments. She viewed the file. The numbers revealed a catastrophic, sustained hemorrhage of the city's fusion reactor that had been running continuously for weeks.

Beside the logs was one other hidden file, an operations file forced to become visible during her attack. It was named *Mnemo_1.opt*. She opened the file path. The screen flooded with classified schematics for a program called *Project Mnemosyne*. At the center of the complex data was a wireframe of a laboratory tower. Rin stopped, her eyes darting from the small screen to the window behind her. 

The blue holographic data on the glass was a scaled projection of the exact same tower schematic.

Rin moved away from the desk, and stepped backward in the room. Tilting her head, she focused her eyes, until her perspective of the windows glowing wireframe display eclipsed a structure looming in the distant city skyline. It was monitoring something specifically within that building—that had been tearing at the city's power grid for the better part of a month.

Before she could dig deeper, the power routing keys download completed. Rin rooted in her jacket pocket and pulled out a red thumb drive. She stopped for a moment, scanning the indecipherable symbols and diagrams, contemplating the repercussions of taking more than she had originally come for. 

Decidedly, she connected the red drive to the terminal. Her fingers instinctively found her jagged sidelock of dark hair and hooked it tightly behind her ear before she hammered the key to start the copy.

***

The fluorescent lights of the maintenance stairwell flickered, casting active shadows against the concrete. 

"Fifteen floors up with no elevator, Victor?" Harlan gasped, loosening the tie of his grey suit. He leaned against the metal railing to catch his breath. "You sure it's Null this time?"

Victor didn't slow his ascent. His black coat whipped at his ankles with each quick step. He kept climbing. The light caught his white hair when he turned at the landing, went through his Albinotic eyes and came back a red. 

"Yeah," Victor said, his voice steady. "Not losin' this lead, Harlan. Best we got to the Filament. Null won't get past me again."

"If we don't have a heart attack first," Harlan said, pushing off the railing to keep up. 

"We gotta get to Null before Corporate Renewal," Victor said, his gaze fixed on the landing above. "If Takeda-Holden finds them first, months of work will have been for nothing." Victor turned back, looking at Harlan on the landing below him. "And we want those mafia bastards."

Victor reached the heavy door of the fifteenth-floor landing. The security lock was fried, a scorched mess of exposed wires. He didn't wait. Shoving the fire door open, he swept his weapon into the sterile hallway—a heavy augmented revolver that looked like a relic snatched from another century, with retrofitted magnetic rails that vibrated with a low charge, and a red indicator light blinking on the side to signify its readiness to fire. 

"Oshima Police Depart—" Victor caught himself. At the opposite end of the hall, an executive suite door stood ajar, blue light bleeding through the gap. Behind him, the jammed elevator doors tried to close against a steel wrench. *Clunk... ding.* Victor heard the noise, his attention drawn quickly to the opened door. Through the opening in the suite, he could see a section of the office's picture window; the glowing blue wireframe projection that outlined a towering building in the distant city skyline.

Harlan burst through the stairwell door a second later, his weapon raised. "Jesus, Victor! You're being reckless—"

"Quiet," Victor murmured, raising his index finger towards Harlan, then pointing to the open door. Following the signal, Harlan snapped his focus to the end of the hall. The two detectives advanced down the corridor, closing the distance to the executive suite.

***

Inside the office, a low tone from the workstation terminal signaled the completed data transfer. 

Rin perked up, the abrupt shout from the hallway cutting through muted silence of the office. Keeping her eyes on the door, she reached down, feeling for the red thumb drive. She pulled it from the console, her fingers closing tightly around the warm metal. 

*Police?* she realized. *Better than Renewal.*

She pocketed the drive, her gaze shifting back to the window, then again to the ajar executive doors. 

*Time to go.*

Her fingers curled around a cylinder in the pocket of her leather jacket. She pulled it free into the blue light of the office. It was a high-yield CS gas canister, matte black with a faded yellow band painted around it. She held it in her palm. It had taken seven people to drag a hulking Renewal agent to the ground long enough for the canister to snap loose from its belt—a bloody memory she tried to forget. Rin ran her thumb over the rusted safety pin, staring at the small explosive as she calculated what she was about to do.

With her free hand, she pulled a low-profile rebreather mask from her collar. It was a specialized, far more advanced than the stained surgical masks worn by the crowds in the Dead Circuits below. She pulled the thick straps over her dark hair, the rubber sealing tightly around her nose and mouth with a faint hiss of pressurized air. 

Rin hooked her finger through the grenade's metal ring.



Out in the hall, Victor tightened his grip on his heavy revolver, stepping toward the office door. Harlan sidestepped beside him, slowly pulling back the slide of his pistol. The metal clicked, and he guided the slide softly forward to chamber a round, then gave Victor a tight nod.

But before they could breach the office, the familiar sound echoed through the hallway. *Clunk... ding*.

Only this time, the metallic ring was hollow and slightly out of tune.

Victor looked down. A black canister with a faded yellow band had rolled to a stop against the toe of his standard-issue dress shoes, having bounced off the metal doorframe of the office.

He felt the abrupt, hardened force of Harlan grabbing his coat with both hands, twisting him backward as they fell against the wall. 

The canister erupted with a violent hiss. Thick, choking white smoke instantly flooded the narrow hallway, creating a dense wall of fog between the detectives and the office. 

Victor propped himself up to one knee, coughing as the acrid chemical burned his sensitive eyes. He rasied his weapon, squinting into the blinding white cloud. 

Through the haze, a lithe, feminine silhouette emerged. Backlit by the blue light of the office, refracting through the fog and stretching into long rays around her. 

Before Victor could blink through the pain and adjust his aim, she was moving. She didn't engage. Slipping past them, sprinting down the corridor. 

"She's running!" Harlan choked out, stumbling blindly against the hallway wall. 

Victor surged to his feet, ignoring the burning in his eyes, but she was too fast. He watched through the clearing smoke as she reached the end of the hall, sliding seamlessly into the elevator car. She kicked the heavy wrench from the floor track with her boot, freeing the doors from their open state. 

Victor reached the elevator a second later, his gaze centered through the final sliver of the closing doors. 

Victor and Rin's eyes met for the first time. Suppressing a violent cough, he spoke with absolute certainty, "Nowhere to hide, Null." 

Rin startled at the sound of her alias, felt the weight of a vendetta in his voice. His stern, unyielding glare trained on her, dispite the tearing pain of the smoke.

The doors sealed shut. Null was gone. 

Victor and Harlan stumbled backward, breaking through the fire door and collapsing into the stairwell, heaving desperate breaths of cleaner air. There was no way to get to the bottom floor in time to catch the elevator as it arrived.

Harlan leaned heavily against the concrete wall, his lungs burning as he gasped for air. "God... damn it!" he wheezed. "Did you... did you get a good look at her?"

Victor remained silent for a moment, his red, bloodshot eyes fixed on the heavy metal of the fire door. He replayed the split-second of eye contact in his mind. The sharp eyes. The feminine silhouette backlit by the bright anomaly data. 

"I did," Victor said, finally catching his breath.

Before Harlan could say another word, the heavy, synchronized thud of tactical boots echoed up the stairwell. A squad of Takeda-Holden Renewal Agents marched onto the landing. They moved with terrifying, mechanical precision, clad in sleek, form-fitting black tactical armor. 

Harlan grabbed Victor’s arm, his voice dropping to a harsh whisper. "Victor... they just climbed fifteen flights. They aren't even breathing."

Their humanity was obscured by glossy, faceless black helmets that reflected the harsh fluorescent light of the stairwell. The lead agent didn't speak. He stepped directly into Victor's personal space, towering over him. Victor could see his own pale reflection in the black glass. The agent shone a deliberately bright tactical light, scanning Harlan's upheld Oshima police badge before snapping the light, to burn directly into Victor's sensitive eyes.

Victor was still holding his heavy revolver. The entire squad shifted towards him in response. A silent, lethal threat.

Victor made a point of slowly, deliberately holstering the gun so as not to instigate them. He winced, blinking through the blinding light. "Oh, look," he said, his sarcastic tone masking a spike of genuine adrenaline. "The cavalry finally showed up. Just in time, as always."

He didn't argue further. He stepped aside, letting the silent, black-armored squad push past them to secure the executive suite.

"We're done here, Harlan," Victor said, turning to head down the stairs. 

Harlan pushed himself off the wall, wiping a streak of sweat and soot from the dark skin of his forehead. "What's so important that Null would risk so much to access?"

"'What' indeed, Harlan," Victor started the long descent back down to the Dead Circuits. He paused on the first step, his eyes narrowing. "But the name on the office she just hit... said Doctor S. Blackwell."

Harlan frowned, staring after him into the stairwell. "Blackwell?"
