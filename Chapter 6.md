Victor rolled his thumb across the striker of his lighter, sparking a flame against the end of his cigarette. He exhaled, adding to the low-hanging smoke of the busy bullpen. The precinct was loud. Exhausted beat cops in wet bomber jackets and vinyl-covered eight-point caps pushed past his desk, shaking off the freezing rain. Up front, a crowd shouted over the intake counter—desperate people demanding answers for the Renewal sweeps currently tearing apart the lower grids.

"It doesn't fit." His words barely cut through the noise.

Harlan leaned forward, resting his elbows on the metal desk. "I told you to buy your jacket off the rack, not the bin," he said with a slight smirk.

Victor leaned back in his chair, ignoring the joke. He crossed his arms, letting the smoke spiral across his vision. "Null's M.O." he reached forward and crushed the half-smoked cigarette into the overflowing ashtray. 

Leaning his chin against his hand, Victor stared at the cluttered desk. "Null usually doesn't stick around." 

Harlan’s smirk vanished. "Null 'stuck around'," he paused, his expression hardening and with a serious tone, "—to shoot tear gas at my face. Remember?"

Victor shook his head at the complaint. "Null tripped a silent alarm because she was digging too deep." He pulled a thick folder across the desk. "I got the terminal logs from the office Null hacked. Doctor Silas Blackwell." 

A sudden commotion erupted across the room as two officers dragged a struggling vagrant across the floor. Victor kept his focus on the papers. 

"It’s heavily redacted," Victor said, his eyes scanning the thick black lines. "Except for this: *Mnemosyne*." He looked across the desk, waiting to see if he had Harlan’s attention. His partner remained silent. "The log has a second name in the metadata," Victor continued. "Doctor Elena Marlowe."

Harlan raised an eyebrow. "Marlowe? The cybernetics prodigy?" 

Victor knew the name. He knew her contributions had revolutionized the current generation of synthetics before suddenly vanishing from the headlines. She was the reason the city was crawling with them—the reason you couldn't walk down a street without some unblinking canner looking you over.

"Vic, she hasn't been seen public-side in five years," Harlan said.

"Because the Company buried her down there to build... whatever this thing is," Victor replied.

Harlan shifted in his seat, his posture relaxing. "Well, there's our answer. Takeda-Holden private data... That's a Renewal Agent's job. Let them deal with the headache." 

A second later, Harlan tensed.

Victor slammed his fist against the desk, the heavy thud cutting through the noise of the room. "No. Null is mine. This is how we get the Filament. Whatever this *Mnemo—thing* is, Null stole the keys."

The bullpen fell silent at the outburst. Victor ignored the stares, letting his temper cool as his focus narrowed. 

"Time we paid Doctor Blackwell a visit."
