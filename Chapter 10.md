Doctor Silas Blackwell stood on the mezzanine overlooking the Mnemosyne Engine, his hands clasped behind his back. The main floor of the lab was quiet, the cooling fans spinning in a steady rhythm. Below him, Elena Marlowe was seated at her terminal, dark shadows under her eyes, leaning heavily into her palm. 

"The alignment is holding," Elena said, her voice echoing faintly. "For now."

Blackwell didn't reply. He turned away from the railing and walked into his private office. He didn't turn on the lights. He walked toward the heavy wooden desk, but stopped halfway across the carpet.

His eyes locked onto the bookshelf.

Wedged between two heavy volumes was a black portfolio containing his original thesis paper on the necessity of Parity. The spine was usually flush with the leather books beside it. Now, it was jutting out of place.

He stepped closer, pulling the portfolio from the shelf. He turned it over in his hands. The pristine matte finish bore the faint, oily smudge of a fingerprint near the edge.

Blackwell closed his eyes, his eidetic memory hinking back through the week's events. *What was out of place? Did Elena finally find the shadow process?* He isolated moment after moment. 

He saw Iris, the boundary analyst, sitting at her terminal on the mezzanine. He remembered the annoying tapping of her stylus. It was what made him take note of her. He remembered the exact moment the shadow process had appeared in the readout,  the synthetics scrubbing the logs. 

Iris's demeanor change. He saw her subtle stagger of  breath. The shift in her eyes as she looked away. Yes, Iris clock the synthetics as they scrubbed the logs. 

*She knows,* Blackwell realized. She had come looking for answers.

He opened his eyes. He could feel his heart rate slow. He set the portfolio back on the shelf, aligning it perfectly. 

He walked out of the office and down the metal stairs, his heavy footsteps ringing out across the lab floor. 

Elena looked up from her terminal as he passed. "Doctor? Is something wrong?"

Blackwell didn't break stride, marching straight toward the heavy hydraulic doors. 

"Something important has come up," Blackwell said, his voice cold and absolute. "Continue to monitor the horizon boundary until I return."

He stepped through the doors, the heavy steel sealing shut behind him.
