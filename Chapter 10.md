The ambient temperature of the Dead Circuits was typically a humid, trapped heat, but standing at the base of the Takeda-Holden tower, the concrete beneath Harper's boots felt like a glacier.

The smog didn't touch the Takeda-Holden tower. The dense, toxic fog that choked the rest of the upper city actively broke around it, creating a massive, sheer cylinder of clear air rising endlessly upward. 

Harper tipped her head back. She couldn't see the actual top from street level. The forced perspective drew the tower's sheer walls together, vanishing into a point in the night sky. The illuminated peaks of the surrounding skyscrapers poked through the smog, almost merging at a point far above her.

She stared up through the clearing. For the first time in years, she saw stars.

They were faint, competing with the polluted light of the city, but they were there. Small pinholes of light in the black.

"You're staring," Rin said. 

Harper broke her gaze from the sky and looked back down at the street. Rin stood a few paces away, the collar of her jacket turned up against the localized cold. 

"We're never getting through that lobby," Harper said. Her voice was flat. The main concourse of the building was a fortress, and she knew it. Turnstiles, biometric scanners, and heavily armed Renewal agents flanked every entrance.

"We don't need the lobby," Rin replied. She pulled a crumpled schematic from her pocket. "Before the engine, Takeda stored data the old-fashioned way. Server farms. Deep basement storage. It's physically severed from the upper floors now, but the hardlines to their network are still viable."

Harper looked at the map. "We talkin' tunnels?"

"Subway," Rin confirmed. "There's old maintenance access doors throughout the city,  off the mag-rail line. They open right into the tunnels."

Harper smiled. "That's my girl. Always gotta plan," she said, her eyes lingering on Rin. "Let's go."

Rin's smile faded. She lowered her gaze. 

They descended the concrete steps into the Takeda-Holden Transit Terminal. 

The terminal vaulted upward, supported by massive arches of polished white stone and chrome. The floors were black marble, extending like spilled ink under banks of recessed lighting.

As they reached the platform, the temperature plummeted further. Takeda-Holden was venting the subterranean chill of the engine directly into the rail system to super-cool the tracks. The elite commuters didn't seem to notice, but as Harper watched their breath plume under the harsh lights, the dry, biting air stiffened her joints.

Frictionless magnetic trains surged through the tunnels, pulling the super-cooled air deep into New Veridia before it settled back into the terminal.

The station was crowded with the upper city's elite—corporate executives, Renewal office workers, and high-end synthetics. The commuters moved quickly across the platforms, racing to get out of Oshima as fast as possible. Some waited for trains, while others moved toward the tower's massive executive lifts, speeding up to the pedestrian sky-bridges hundreds of stories above them.

They wore the grime of the Dead Circuits. Harper's brown suede jacket was worn. Rin's heavy boots scuffed against the marble. The wealthy commuters actively parted around them, casting disgusted glances over their hand-held devices at the two girls dirtying the transit hub.

They both stuck out. Badly.

Harper's eyes darted toward the far end of the platform. Beside her, Rin was scanning the crowd.

"Oh, Rin, darlin'. You didn't say it'd be crawling with Renewal agents," Harper's voice cracked under the sudden tension.

Renewal agents were everywhere. Their heavy black armor, faces obscured, as they patrolled the marble floors in pairs. 

Harper’s hand drifted up to her collarbone. Her fingers found the jagged edge of her scar. She swallowed hard, her heart hammering against her chest.

"We're in the wrong place. This is going sideways." Harper whispered, fixing her eyes to the floor.

Rin leaned in close. "Hey," she said, her voice steady. "Stay calm. Try to blend in."

There was a sudden pressure drop in the terminal, their ears popping as the oncoming mag-train pulled the atmosphere out of the station like a vacuum. 

The massive machine tore into the terminal, reversing its magnetic polarity to kill its momentum. It came to a dead stop with a terrifying, unnatural silence. The only impact was physical—a massive gust of returning air. The pressure blasted across the platform. It whipped at the tailored long coats and dresses of the commuters. Harper and Rin were hit by the sudden gale, their hair blowing wildly across their faces in a tangled mess, blinding them for a split second.

As the doors opened on the train, and the switch of commuters began on and off, an amplified voice cut through the low hum of the station. 

"You. Vagrant. Halt."

Across the polished black marble, two Renewal agents were advancing through the crowd. Harper followed their trajectory. They were moving towards a young man. He was dressed like Harper and Rin—frayed clothes, scuffed shoes, equally out of place. He looked terrified.

"No, no," Harper breathed, staring at the young man across the terminal floor. "Don't run."

The boy panicked. He bolted. 

The agents didn't shout a second warning. They moved with terrifying speed, armored boots pounding against the stone. One of them closed the distance in seconds, driving a heavy shoulder into the boy's spine. The crack of his skull against the marble echoed sharply. 

The agent’s knee dropped onto his neck, pinning his face to the floor. The second agent grabbed his wrists and cinched a heavy zip-tie around them, pulling it tight enough to cut circulation. It was ruthless, practiced violence.

Harper couldn't look away. She had seen this many times, but it never got easier.

The crowd of wealthy commuters didn't pause. Corporate executives stepped around the boy without taking their eyes off their devices. To the upper city, it wasn't brutality. It was an obstacle on the morning commute.

"C'mon," Rin said. She hooked her arm through Harper's, pulling her forward. "Now's our chance."

Harper stumbled, unable to tear her eyes away from the boy on the floor. The agents hauled him up by his bound wrists. One of them tapped the side of his helmet. His head swiveled toward the rest of the crowd. Scanning. 

"Rin—" Harper choked out.

"Don't look at them. Keep your head down." Rin's grip on her arm tightened. They moved fast, cutting through the commuters and pushing toward the far end of the platform while the agents began sweeping the crowd behind them.

The terminal ended exactly as it had started: a set of concrete stairs leading up and out to the streets above. But Rin didn't take the stairs. She veered toward the wall, where a corridor of green doors ran along the side of the train tube.
