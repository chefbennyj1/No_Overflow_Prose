The concrete beneath Harper's boots was freezing. 

It didn't make sense. The ambient temperature of The Dead Circuits was a humid, stagnant warmth, trapped beneath the city's perpetual smog layer. But standing at the base of the Takeda-Holden tower, the chill biting through the soles of her shoes felt deep and unnatural. Like standing on a glacier. 

She tipped her head back, looking straight up the monolithic building. 

The smog didn't touch it. The dense, toxic fog that choked the rest of the upper city seemed to actively break around the Takeda-Holden tower, repelled by an invisible force. It created a massive, sheer column of clear air rising hundreds of floors into the sky. It was the only building in the entire grid where you could stand at the street level and see the top. 

Harper stared up through that clear atmospheric cylinder. For the first time in years, she saw stars. 

They were faint, competing with the neon bleed of the city, but they were there. Small,  pinholes of light in the black. Framing that patch of clear sky, the tips of the surrounding corporate skyscrapers poking through the top of the smog layer. Their lower halves were swallowed by the fog, but forced perspective drew their illuminated peaks together around the Takeda-Holden tower's clear airspace. 

"You're staring," Rin said. 

Harper broke her gaze from the sky and looked back down at the street. Rin stood a few paces away, the collar of her jacket turned up against the localized cold. 

"We're never getting through the lobby," Harper said. Her voice was flat. She didn't have to explain. The main concourse was a fortress. Turnstiles, biometric scanners, and heavily armed Renewal agents flanked every entrance.

"We don't need the lobby," Rin replied. She pulled a crumpled schematic from her pocket. "Takeda-Holden didn't always have quantum singularity computers in New Veridia." 

She paused, waiting to see if the sheer absurdity of the sentence would lighten the mood. It didn't. 

"During their rise to power," Rin pushed on, "they stored data the old-fashioned way. Server farms. Deep basement storage. It's completely severed from the upper floors now, but the connections to their network are still viable."

Harper looked at the map. "Access tunnels?"

"Subway," Rin confirmed. "There's an old maintenance access doors throughout the city,  off the secondary mag-rail line. They open right into the tunnels in one of their old data basements."

Harper smiled. "That's my girl. Always got a plan."

Rin felt Harper's eyes lingering on her. "Let's go."

"Okay," Harper said, her smile fading as she lowered her gaze. "Let's go."

Concrete steps lowered into the Takeda-Holden Transit Terminal.

The terminal might have been down in the Dead Circuits, but it didn't look like a subway. It looked like a cathedral.

The subterranean concourse vaulted upward, supported by massive arches of polished white stone and chrome. The floors were black marble, they extended like spilled ink under banks of recessed lighting. 

Silent magnetic trains slid through tubes, reaching far into New Veridia.

The station was crowded with the upper city's elite. Corporate executives, and high-end synthetics. The commuters moved quickly across the platforms, racing to get out of Oshima as quickly as possible.

Rin and Harper stepped down into the terminal. Most of the commuters were too busy to notice them, but they didn't belong there, and it showed.

They wore the grime of the Dead Circuits on their boots. Harper's brown suede jacket was frayed. Rin's heavy boots scuffed. The wealthy commuters actively parted around them. Once in a while a commuter would look up from their hand-held device, and cast disgusted glances at the two girls dirtying the transit hub.

Harper and Rin stuck out. Badly. 

Rin scanned the platform, as she looked to Harper. Harper looked nervously towards the far end of the platform.

"Oh Rin, darlin'. You didn't say it'd be crawlin' with Renewal agents," Harper's voice cracked under the sudden tension.

Renewal agents were everywhere. Their heavy black armor, faces obscured, as they patrolled the marble floors in pairs. 

Harper’s hand drifted up to her collarbone. Her fingers found the jagged edge of her scar. She swallowed hard, her heart hammering against her chest.

"We are in the wrong place. This is a bad idea." Harper whispered, fixing her eyes to the floor.

Rin leaned in close. "Hey," she said, her voice steady. "Stay calm. Try to blend in."

Rin felt the sudden pressure drop in the terminal, her ears popping as the oncoming mag-train pulled the atmosphere out of the station like a vacuum. Then came the shrieking whine., as the trains breaks clamped against the track. 

The train tore into the terminal, arriving with a massive gust of returning air. The pressure front blasted across the platform. It whipped the tailored coats of the commuters. Harper and Rin hit by the sudden gale, their hair blowing wildly across their faces in a tangled mess, blinding them for a split second.

As the doors opened on the train, and the switch of commuters began on and off, an amplified voice cut through the low hum of the station. 

"You. Vagrant. Halt."

Across the polished black marble, two Renewal agents were advancing through the crowd. Rin looked, the agents were moving towards a young man. He was dressed like Harper and Rin—frayed clothes, scuffed shoes,  just as out of place. He looked terrified.

Rin whispered, "Okay, Harper. Now's our chance."

"No, no," Harper breathed, staring at the young man across the terminal floor. "Don't run."

The boy panicked. He bolted. 

The agents didn't shout a second warning. They moved with terrifying speed, armored boots pounding against the stone. One of them closed the distance in seconds, driving a heavy shoulder into the boy's spine. The crack of his skull against the marble echoed sharply. 

Before the boy could even groan, the agent’s knee dropped onto his neck, pinning his face to the floor. The second agent grabbed his wrists and cinched a heavy zip-tie around them, pulling it tight enough to cut circulation. It was ruthless, practiced violence.

Harper couldn't look away, she had seen this many times. It never got easier.

The crowd of wealthy commuters didn't scream. They didn't even pause. Corporate executives stepped neatly around the bleeding boy's legs without taking their eyes off their devices. To the upper city, it wasn't brutality. It was an obstacle on the morning commute.

"C'mon," Rin said. She hooked her arm through Harper's, pulling her forward. "We go now."

Harper stumbled, unable to tear her eyes away from the boy on the floor. The agents hauled him up by his bound wrists. One of them tapped his visor. His armored head swiveled toward the rest of the crowd. Scanning. 

"Rin—" Harper choked out.

"Don't look at them. Look at the train." Rin's grip on her arm tightened like a vice. They moved fast, cutting through the sea of tailored suits as the agents began sweeping the platform behind them.

The terminal ended exactly as it started, a set of concrete stairs, leading up and out to the rainy streets above. Rin looked to the side of the stairs, a corridor of green doors ran along the side of the train tube.
