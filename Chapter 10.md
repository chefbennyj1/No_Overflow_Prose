Doctor Silas Blackwell stood on the mezzanine overlooking the Mnemosyne Engine, his hands clasped behind his back. The main floor of the lab was quiet, the cooling fans spinning in a steady rhythm. Below him, Elena Marlowe was seated at her terminal, dark shadows under her eyes, leaning heavily into her palm. 

"The alignment is holding," Elena said, her voice echoing faintly. "For now."

Blackwell didn't reply. He turned away from the railing and walked into his private office. He stopped at his heavy wooden desk.

His gaze snapped to the bookshelf.

Wedged between two heavy volumes was a black portfolio containing his original thesis paper on the Parity Effect. The spine was usually flush with the leather books beside it. Now, it was jutting out of place.

He stepped closer, pulling the portfolio from the shelf. He turned it over in his hands. The pristine matte finish bore the faint, oily smudge of a fingerprint near the edge.

Someone had been in his office. 

Blackwell set the portfolio back on the shelf, aligning it perfectly. He turned and walked back to the doorway, looking down at the lab floor. 

"Elena," Blackwell called out, his voice perfectly level. "Has anyone else been on this floor off-schedule this week?"

Elena didn't look up. "Just Iris. She was here early on Tuesday before my shift."

Blackwell didn't react. He closed his eyes, his eidetic memory thinking back with perfect clarity. 

He saw Iris, the boundary analyst, sitting at her terminal on the mezzanine. He remembered the annoying tapping of her stylus. It was what made him take note of her. He remembered the exact moment the shadow process had appeared in the readout, the synthetics scrubbing the errors. 

He watched Iris's demeanor change. He saw the subtle stagger of her breath. The shift in her eyes as she looked away. Yes, Iris had seen the synthetics as they scrubbed the logs. She hadn't just come in early. 

*She knows,* Blackwell realized. She had come looking for answers.

He opened his eyes. The thud of his pulse slowed to a calm, steady rhythm.

He walked out of the office and down the metal stairs, his heavy footsteps ringing out across the lab floor. 

Elena looked up from her terminal as he passed. "Doctor? Is something wrong?"

Blackwell didn't break stride, marching straight toward the heavy hydraulic doors. 

"Something important has come up," Blackwell said, his voice cold and absolute. "Continue to monitor the horizon boundary until I return."

He stepped through the doors, the heavy steel sealing shut behind him.
