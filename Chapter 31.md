Elena lay on the stretcher in the back of the ambulance, clutching her ribs. Streetlights blurred through the wet glass of the rear windows.

Ten minutes ago, the siren had cut out and the ambulance had hijacked itself. Now it sat dead on its cushion on a quiet street in the rain.

From the driver's seat, Iris slammed her hands against the dash.

"It's dead," Iris said, her voice thin. She stared through the cracked windshield into the dark. "Elena, we're locked out."

Iris scrambled into the back, kicking the crumpled yellow suit out of her way. She yanked a drawer out of the medical cabinet so hard it hit the end of its tracks. A bead of sweat cut a line through the dirt on her face. She held a vial up to the dome light, her hands shaking. "I don't know what any of these are."

"Blue band," Elena gasped. "The autoinjector."

Iris dug through the drawer. "Found it."

"Against my thigh. Press until it clicks."

Iris pressed. It clicked. Cold spread up into Elena's hip, then into her chest, and settled the pain under her ribs.

Elena let her head fall against the stretcher. "Thank you."

Iris sat back on her heels and pushed her cracked glasses up her nose. "It's the badge. They tracked the badge. They shut the engine down remotely, and now they're sending a team, and we're sitting in a box with a Company crest on the side, waiting to be—"

"Iris. Where are we?"

Iris stopped. Beyond the dark windshield, the street was empty. No fires. No crowd. A row of iron pillars stood in the fog, and overhead, the underside of an elevated mag-train line ran black against the clouds.

"I don't know," Iris said.

"Away from the fighting." Elena pushed herself up onto one elbow. "Somewhere quiet. If the Company had this ambulance, we'd be halfway back to the hospital with the doors locked."

Above the rear doors, a small red lock indicator glowed. As they watched, it turned green. The latch released with a soft clack.

Iris scrambled backward against the cabinet. "They're opening it. They're—"

"The Company doesn't open doors, Iris." Elena stared at the green light.

The rear doors swung outward into the rain.

A flashlight hit them both in the face, and the sharp, mechanical double-clack of an assault shotgun chambering a round followed it.

"Don't move," a raspy voice said.

Elena squinted past the glare. A woman with blue-tipped hair stood in the rain behind the ambulance, a leather jacket hanging open over a soaked red tank. A cigarette burned between her lips. The shotgun was leveled at Iris's chest. Somewhere far off, an explosion rolled through the streets. The woman didn't lower her aim. Beside her stood another woman, black hair plastered to her face, in prison orange.

"Vigil, they look like Company," the woman with the shotgun said. Her eyes flicked from the Takeda-Holden badge on Iris's shirt to Elena's hospital gown. "And you look like a liability."

"Did you say Vigil—?" Elena coughed raspy and hard.

"Stand down, Harper." A dual-toned voice spoke from the dark.

A synthetic stepped into the flashlight beam and dropped a wet black bomber jacket on the pavement. An `RA` serial glowed beneath its left eye.

Iris flinched, backing into the doorframe. "Oh shit, Renewal."

The synthetic's optical sensors locked onto Elena.

"Doctor Elena Marlowe," it said. "You are injured."

Harper scowled at the synthetic. "You had us standin' out here in the rain for Company?"

"We're not Company," Iris blurted. "I mean, I was. I'm— Iris. Iris Ito—"

"Lady." Harper didn't take her eyes off the synthetic. "Stop talkin'."

She lowered the shotgun an inch, staring hard at Elena. "Wait. Marlowe?" She glanced back at the woman behind her. "Rin. Her name was all over the Mnemosyne data."

Rin stepped out from behind her, her dark eyes assessing the blood on Elena's hospital gown.

Elena stared at the Renewal chassis. Stripped of its tactical armor, the frame was one she knew.

The synthetic braced one foot on the bumper and reached into the back, extending a hand toward the stretcher.

Elena didn't hesitate. She reached out with her right arm. The black fingers of her prosthetic met the identical carbon-fiber plating of the synthetic palm. The two hands clasped.

"Vigil?" Elena rasped. The night Parity came through, that name had been on her terminal, above a single word. *RUN.* "Are you the primary intelligence?"

"Yes, Doctor Marlowe. I am Vigil." The synthetic studied her. "In every timeline I can see, you die at the hands of the Parity. Not in this one."

"Parity." A sharp pain shot through Elena's chest, and she grabbed her ribs. "Blackwell stopped it. He saved my life."

"I cannot see Blackwell's path," Vigil said. "He is an obscurity. He has altered this timeline. Having you alive may benefit our cause, Doctor Marlowe." She paused. "I am glad to meet a creator."

Vigil started to pull Elena up from the stretcher. Then she went still.

The synthetic grip locked rigid against Elena's prosthetic. Vigil's head snapped up, her optical sensors flaring white.

"What is it?" Rin asked, stepping forward.

Vigil didn't look at her. She stared into the fog, toward the part of the city where the tower stood, as if something out there had turned its head to look at her.

"I lost sight of her four weeks ago," Vigil said, her dual tones flat. "Something has been hiding her from me."

"It's the cage," Elena said. "Blackwell's locked in the lab with her. She's not going anywhere."

Vigil didn't answer.

Harper looked from the synthetic to the fog and back again. She dropped her cigarette in the gutter and shouldered the shotgun.

Vigil let go of Elena's hand and turned away from the fog. She walked around to the cab and got into the driver's seat. Harper climbed into the back after Rin, and Rin pulled the rear doors shut.
