Strips of yellow emergency lights ran through the middle of the corridor walls, illuminating the darkness.

The counter-weighted vault doors of Deep Storage unlocked with the loud movement of metallic rotary gears. They opened with the grind and squeal on their heavy iron hinges.

Victor raised his revolver and clicked on his tactical flashlight. Its beam sliced through the room.

He stepped inside the door, and stopped. The floor was wide open. Nowhere to step without being seen. Plastic-draped synthetics stretched into long uniform rows. The ticks of their peristaltic pumps randomly sounding off in across the floor. A perfect maze for an ambush. 

Victor swept his light across the grid, calculating the odds. Null was a hacker, but he didn't know if she was armed, or how many people she had brought with her to pull off a physical breach. 

Harlan chambered a round, raised his gun, and clicked on his flashlight. His eyes met Victor’s. They nodded and sidestepped into the vaulted hangar.

Victor's eyes scanned the perimeter walls. At the far end of the grid, his beam caught the edge of an emergency service tunnel, its door propped half open.

Victor signaled to Harlan with a quick upward nod. "Over there," he whispered. "Lock it down."

Harlan broke away, moving quietly toward the emergency exit.
***
A harsh beam of tactical light carved through the dark. 

"The service tunnel is guarded. I'm on the main doors," the voice echoed across the room. "You're out of exits, Null. It's over."

"—Null. It's over," Vigil’s head lolled to the side. In the detective’s exact inflection, she spoke the words back. 

Rin crouched down among the plastic-draped synthetics, pulling Harper and Vigil with her into the shadows. 

Harper gripped Rin's arm in the dark, her voice a tight whisper. "Rin, what is it?"

Rin swallowed hard, staring at the approaching beams of light. "There's something I forgot to tell you about the night I hacked Blackwell's office."

Harper glared at her, unimpressed. "Yeah. And?"

Rin nodded toward the flashlights. "There were these two cops that showed up."

The layered resonance in Vigil's voice sheared away, flattening into a monotone. "Three curves and one obscurity, Echo." 

Harper's eyes widened. Rin threw Vigil's synthetic arm over her shoulder to help move it. Bracing for dead weight, it was shockingly light. 

"Okay, listen," Harper whispered frantically, helping Rin drag the wounded Vigil through the maze of plastic-draped bodies. "If we hit the auxiliary lights, we can blind them for two seconds, maybe rush the tunnel—"

"—rush the tunnel," Vigil's broken whisper reflecting back at her. The words fell from the synthetic's lips at the exact same moment Harper spoke them. Not an echo. She was reciting a timeline.

Harper’s head snapped toward the synthetic. “Hey—if you know something, now’s the time.”

"Harper," Rin said, her voice dead flat. 

"Okay, then we use the bodies," Harper rambled, her panic spiking. "We push the racks over, create a barricade—"

"—create a barricade," Vigil’s broken voice hung between them.

"Harper, stop." Rin stopped in the shadows of the dormant army. She looked at the frantic girl, the math was already complete: the detective had the main doors; his partner had the tunnel. They had made it this far, and had Vigil. There was no clean way to save them all. It was Checkmate. 

"Checkmate." Vigil's head fell forward.

"What are you doing, Erin?" Harper whispered.

If Rin told her the truth, Harper would stop her before she could do the one thing that still mattered.  Rin grabbed Harper by the collar of her jacket and pulled her in. She kissed her. It was soft, frantic—stolen as the flashlight beams swept over the plastic sheets beside them.

Rin broke away, shoving the weight of the synthetic entirely onto Harper. She shrugged off her oversized leather jacket and pressed it hard into Harper's chest. The corner of the mag-train ticket poked out of the pocket. She pushed the ticket back into her pocket. "Stay down. When they move, you run. And don't call me that. It's Rin."

Before Harper could argue, Rin stepped away from the cover of the plastic sheets and into the aisle. 

***

Victor swept his light across the long rows of the dormant army. Hundreds of plastic-draped bodies stood shoulder to shoulder in the dark, tethered to their I.V. stands. 

His beam passed over pale synthetic faces beneath the plastic sheets. Silicone feeding tubes glowed faintly in the glare. The light swept past another figure standing in the line, moved a few feet further—and then Victor's brain registered what he had seen. 

He snapped the beam back. 

Not a synthetic. A girl. She was wearing a black tank top, her arms covered in dark, intricate ink. She stood in a half-bent stance, her hands raised in the air to surrender.

But as the harsh light hit her face, her eyes didn't squint, they were sharp and defiant. Victor recognized them. The girl from the elevator.

*Null.* 

Victor didn't lower his revolver. He kept the sight trained directly on her chest. "Harlan!" Victor shouted, his voice echoing in the space. "On me! I got her!"

Rin’s eyes darted toward the maze of synthetics.

There was a slight shift in the metal of his revolver as Victor firmed his grip. "Hey. Don't do it."

The girl took a slow, calculated step to the right. 

"Don't do it," Victor warned again, framing his aim.

She bolted. 

She sprinted right, cutting deep into the center of the dormant army. Victor followed her with his light, and began the pursuit.

"She's on the move!" Victor roared, his long black coat tailing behind him. She was unarmed. Victor holstered his revolver so he could run faster. He wasn't going to lose her again. "Harlan, cut her off!"

Victor chased the girl as she wove blindly through the maze of plastic-draped bodies, her boots pounding against the concrete.

She slammed into the dormant synthetics as she slipped around each corner. The bodies buckled momentarily before their internal accelerometers whirred their electronic joints and pulled their bodies unnaturally back into their idle hunched position. 


Victor's flashlight slashed across the rows of synthetic bodies. The moving beam threw stretching shadows across the plastic tarps, turning every lifeless frame into a runner breaking for cover. He banked hard losing the girl in the strobe.

He stopped. Deep breaths of cold air stung deep in his lungs. "Shit!" 

Victor raised his flashlight holding the beam steady, freezing the shifting shadows. 

There, he saw it. Not the girl, but the buckling of random synthetics as they swayed their posture back to center. 

He was off.

He turned the aisle, his beam cutting through the dark. Suddenly, a figure stumbled out of the shadows, lunging directly at him. 

A synthetic. Pushed towards him. Its legs cycled mechanically as its balance routines tried to correct it. In the flashlight’s strobe, it looked alive.

Victor braced himself, grabbing the lunging figure by the shoulders and shoving it hard to the side. The machine's gyroscopes whined, abruptly stabilizing its posture behind him as he pushed past it. 

Before he could raise his light, another plastic-draped figure lurched out of the dark. He caught the hollow carbon frame, wrestling its mechanical momentum aside. Then a third. The girl was violently shoving them from behind into the aisle as she ran, forcing Victor to physically fight his way through a gauntlet of staggering, lifeless synthetics. 

He shoved the last one away, breaking through the barricade. 

Suddenly, a second flashlight beam cut across their path. Harlan stepped out from the line of synthetics directly in front of her.

The girl didn't have time to stop. She slammed hard into Harlan's chest. The impact knocked her stumbling backward onto the concrete. 

Victor closed in from behind, trapping her in the intersection of their blinding lights. 

On the floor, she raised her hands. Still breathing hard, she watched Victor from the corner of her eye.

***

The cold steel of the handcuffs ratcheted tight around Rin's wrists, biting into her skin. The detective hauled her to her feet, his revolver drawn, and pushed her toward the corridor.

Rin kept her head down, her dark hime-cut bangs falling over her eyes. As the detective forced her through the intersections, the blast doors were already retracted. The security keypads were dead, their red lockdown indicators dark. 

Nobody stopped two cops walking a cuffed girl through the middle of a catastrophic breach. The alarms blared on the upper floors, but their path was silent. 

Rin looked back one final time through the open blast doors. Deep in the shadows of the dormant army, Harper knelt with Vigil clutched against her. The service tunnel was wide open. They were safe.
