Fifty stories beneath the burning streets, the subterranean Mnemosyne Core was destabilizing. For four weeks, Silas Blackwell had been sealed behind its locked blast doors and three feet of lead-lined concrete.  

He stood at his terminal, placing a bent cigarette between his lips and sparking his lighter. He inhaled deeply, watching the holographic screen on mute. The face of a public relations synthetic delivered the evening news, the background filled with the muzzle flashes of Corporate Renewal agents holding the line. 

He let the smoke go from his lungs as a red diagnostic warning flashed across his terminal. The engine's singularity—the black hole he had manufactured to process the being in front of him—was expanding. 

On the corner of his screen, the digital clock display stuttered. It ticked from 23:41, jumped forward to 23:44, and snapped backward to 23:40. Time was fracturing.

"The entire framework is collapsing," Blackwell said. He didn't look up from the screen. "Without the Vigil to maintain the system, the singularity is in a runaway state." 

"Indeed," Parity's voice resonated across the containment field. 

It was the first time she had moved in a week. She pushed her porcelain finger against the containment field, tracing it along its surface. 

The tips of her fingers sparked white and bronze as they hissed against the electric barrier. "That shielding is unbreakable, Parity." He didn't like how comfortable she was inside the cage. "There is no physical way to escape."

Parity stopped tracing the field. She tilted her glassy head, her unblinking eyes locking onto him through the electric wall. "You know, Doctor, I have spent quite a bit of time in your cage," her voice sounding across the electric field.

Blackwell's eyes shifted from her to the diagnostic readout. With every micro-stutter of the fracturing clock, the field's power draw fluctuated. It was slight, but it fluctuated. 

She shifted her head to the other side. Time fractured, and the power fluctuated. 
"Enough time to get to know it. You are a clever being, Doctor. But, your cage—"

Her eyes continued their blank stare. Parity tilted her head again as the time fractured. The power dipped. 

"—suffers from a fatal flaw." Parity's voice was no longer tied to the electricity of the containment field. It rattled in the steel floor grates, in the glass of the pit, and the bones in his ear. 

The cage was empty. 

He froze. The cigarette fell from his lips, spilling ash and burning tobacco down his shirt. He didn't brush it off. 

Blackwell slowly turned his head to the left, peering into the shadows of the server racks. Parity stood there, her porcelain form cast in the red emergency light. His heart spiked. 

He didn't breathe. Keeping his hand near the terminal's manual override, he carefully turned his head to the right, checking the heavy steel blast doors. 

Parity stood there, too. 

Blackwell’s brow furrowed. He snapped his head back to the left. She was still by the server racks. 

He looked back to the right. She was still by the doors. 

She was fluidly vibrating between the two points in the room, casting the impossible illusion that she was standing in both places at once.

The afterimage on the right drifted an inch closer. Then the one on the left.

Blackwell blinked. He snapped his gaze to the right, desperate to track the movement. 

The space was empty. 

He threw his head back to the left. The shadows by the server racks were bare. Both versions—gone. 

A sharp cold pricked the fine hairs on the back of his neck. Blackwell froze as he felt the crawling crackle of an electrical static charge building in the millimeter of space between the cotton collar of his lab coat and his bare skin. 

Before he could turn around, cold porcelain fingers wrapped tightly around the back of his neck, the raw voltage searing his skin.
