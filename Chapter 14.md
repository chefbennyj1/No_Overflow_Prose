
The stench of standing water filled the tunnel. Flickering incandescent bulbs cast long, jaundiced shadows against the curved concrete walls. Heavy pipes lined the ceiling, weeping condensation onto the cracked floor. 

Harper kept her hands tucked into her jacket pockets, shivering as they moved deeper into the underbelly of the Takeda-Holden tower. They passed a series of unmarked, rusted doors until the tunnel widened into a small antechamber. 

At the far end stood a heavy blast door, marked with faded corporate caution stripes. A thick magnetic lock sealed it shut. 

Rin pulled her single-board computer back out, stepping up to the keypad. "Deep storage," she said, her voice echoing faintly in the damp space. 

Harper stared at the thick magnetic lock, then down at the crude circuitry in Rin's hand. "You're gonna need a bigger one of those if you wanna hack this."

"Just—," Rin raised her hand towards Harper, "give me a minute. " 

Before the plastic even touched the slot, a heavy, concussive *clack* echoed through the antechamber. The red indicator on the mag-lock snapped to a pristine, glowing green. 

The blast door groaned on its tracks, sliding open on its own. 

Rin didn't move, her hand still suspended in the air. 

Harper stepped back, the air spilling out of the dark was freezing. 

"Rin," Harper whispered, staring into the pitch-black void. "Tell me you did that."

"No," Rin said softly. "It knows we're here."

They stepped over the threshold. It was black, but Harper could feel the sheer, voluminous scale of the space opening up in front of them. The air didn't smell like rusted metal or damp concrete. It smelled entirely sterile, like a surgical ward.

High above them, motion sensors triggered.

A sequence of harsh white floodlights snapped on, firing down the length of the room in blinding succession.

The sub-level was a sprawling, echoing expanse of pristine white paneling. High above, thick steel air ducts lined the ceiling, scrubbing the air of dust.

Standing rigid in the center of the white tiles, arranged in a flawless grid like a dormant army, were hundreds of previous-generation Synthetics. 

Each body was draped in a loose, thin sheet of transparent plastic. Beside every synthetic stood a medical-style monitor mounted on a five-caster pole. Thick plastic tubes fed directly into the backs of the synthetic's, pumping a transparent blue bio-compute fluid into their wetware to keep their neural lattices stable in the long sleep.

"Oh my god," Harper breathed, staring at the plastic-draped army.

Rin pulled her SBC from her pocket, looking down at the string of text Vigil had given her. A new coordinate flashed on the small green screen, pointing toward the far end of the grid. 

"She didn't send us down here for data," Rin said, her voice echoing off the white tile. She looked back up at the terrifying, sleeping army. "She wants us to wake one up."
