
The stench of standing water filled the tunnel. Flickering incandescent bulbs cast long, jaundiced shadows against the curved concrete walls. Iron pipes lined the ceiling, weeping condensation onto the cracked floor. 

Harper kept her hands tucked into her jacket pockets, shivering as they moved deeper into the underbelly of the Takeda-Holden tower. She gnawed on a piece of synthetic bubblegum, the bright, incongruous smell of artificial strawberries cutting through the stench of standing water. They passed a series of unmarked, rusted doors until the tunnel widened into a small antechamber. 

At the far end stood a reinforced blast door, marked with faded corporate caution stripes. An oversized magnetic lock sealed it shut. 

Rin pulled her single-board computer back out, stepping up to the keypad. "Deep storage," she murmured.

Harper eyed the industrial lock, then looked down at the crude circuitry in Rin's hand. "You're gonna need a bigger one of those if you wanna hack this."

"Just—," Rin raised her hand towards Harper, "give me a minute. " 

Before the plastic even touched the slot, a deep, concussive *clack* rang through the antechamber. The red indicator on the mag-lock snapped to a pristine, glowing green. 

The blast door groaned on its tracks, sliding open on its own. 

Rin didn't move, her hand still suspended in the air. 

Harper stepped back. The air spilling out of the dark was freezing.

"Rin," Harper whispered into the pitch-black room. "Tell me you did that."

"No," Rin said softly. "It knows we're here."

They stepped over the threshold. It was black, but Harper could feel the sheer, voluminous scale of the space opening up in front of them. The air didn't smell like rusted metal or damp concrete. It smelled sterile, like a surgical ward.

Overhead, motion sensors triggered.

A sequence of harsh white floodlights snapped on, firing down the length of the room in blinding succession.

Standing rigid across the pristine white paneling, arranged in a flawless grid like a dormant army, were hundreds of previous-generation Synthetics. 

Each body was draped in a loose, thin sheet of transparent plastic. Beside every synthetic stood a medical-style monitor mounted on a five-caster pole. Braided silicone tubes fed directly into the backs of their necks, pulsing with a translucent blue fluid.

"Oh my god," Harper breathed.

Rin stepped up to the nearest medical monitor, reading the scrolling data. "Bio-compute fluid," she murmured. "It's keeping their neural lattices stable for the long sleep."

She looked down at the SBC in her hand, finding the string of text Vigil had given her. A new coordinate flashed on the small green screen, pointing toward the far end of the grid. 

"It didn't send us down here for data," Rin said, her words carrying across the stark white tile. She looked back up at the terrifying, sleeping army. "It wants us to wake one up."
