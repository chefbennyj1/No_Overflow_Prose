The dark of the observation deck was suffocating, bronze holographic circuit traces seared across Parity's weightless frame, illuminating the concrete under her. Parity keep her eyes inhuman gaze locked on Elena. 

Before Elena could move, the lab's overhead intercom crackled to life. Static cut through the heavy silence, followed by Doctor Blackwell's voice. It was stripped of its usual arrogance. He was breathless, frantic, screaming into the microphone from somewhere deep in the stairwell. 

"Elena! It wasn't supposed to break through the horizon boundary! It was supposed to be contained!" His voice echoed through the lab. "Run, Elena! Run!"

Elena was already backing away, her eyes locked on the porcelain-white figure in front of her. She edged toward the heavy, red manual lockdown lever on the far wall. If she pulled it, the hydraulic blast doors would seal permanently. She would be trapped with the entity, but it wouldn't be able to leave. 

Parity’s head tilted, her movement stuttering into fractured trails of motion. The entity's eyes tracked Elena's gaze to the lever. 

"In every version of your existence where you move to pull that lever." Parity's voice vibrated through the elements in the room, a sound devoid of breath or origin. "You never do."

The air in the lab rippled. A single frame of reality dropped. In one millisecond, Parity's subtle rise and fall was intimately close to Elena. The physical space between them freezing the molecules of the air. 
 She reached out and caught Elena’s wrist right as her fingertips brushed the red steel of the lever. With an effortless, sickeningly twist, there was a sharp crack—the sound of a wet tree branch snapping in half. 

Elena felt the searing pain shoot up her arm as Parity's open palm struck her chest. Her ribs fractured instantly. The air was violently evacuated from her lungs as she was launched backward into the dark. She flew across the width of the lab, before crashing violently into the base of the reinforced glass. 

Elena crumpled to the concrete floor. No breath. No movement. Her vision began to narrow into a dark tunnel. 

Through the ringing in her ears, she looked up toward the exit. She hadn't hit the lever.
