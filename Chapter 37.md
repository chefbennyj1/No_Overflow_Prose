Time slowed, she was back in control.

In the dilated silence between two vibrations of light, the concrete ceiling hung in the air. Cracks frozen open, dark veins caught mid-fracture. Dust drifted down like salt, motionless in the glare. Beneath the falling stone, Harper was locked against Rin, her lips shaped around a syllable that could not finish.

*Erin—*

Her physical shell was gone. The borrowed Renewal chassis had shattered into scrap, and with it, the narrow tunnel of human senses fell away.

Her sight returned whole.

The architecture of spacetime reopened around her. Infinite branches unfurled outward, every life in the city laid bare from birth to ash. In the four meters beneath the falling ceiling, her calculation struck a wall.

"Four biological terminations in zero-point-zero-four seconds," Admin calculated, transmitting across the internal bridge. "The mass of the concrete permits no divergence."

"No," Echo answered across the link. "Their world lines will not end here."

Vigil moved.

She took Elena first. The doctor had no weight in the dilated frame, a fragile tangle of bone and carbon fiber. Vigil lifted her off the grating and stepped through the dead air, rising out of the sublevel into the atrium.

She climbed eighty stories of frozen glass.

Around her, the physical world sat trapped in amber. In the middle of the shaft, brass shell casings from a Renewal volley hung in midair, caught in the light from the eastern windows. Lower down, on the polished marble floor, the fallen synthetic wearing her own face lay unmoving. Vigil passed it without slowing.

The horizon pulled at her back.

With every meter she climbed away from the core, the singularity tugged at the edges of her compiled form. Filaments of blue light unraveled from her silhouette, trailing like burning thread into the dark below.

She set Elena against a frosted pillar on the plaza, then dropped back into the well.

She took Iris next. The analyst was mid-stride, black-rimmed glasses tilted on her nose. The pull from the pit doubled. Admin registered the loss of coherence—seven percent of her neural density stripped away into the gravity well.

She returned for Harper and Rin.

Harper was still curved over Rin's back, shielding her. Vigil slid her hands beneath them both, locking them together against her chest, and kicked off the buckling floor grating.

The slab dropped behind her. In real time it would have been a thunderclap; in Vigil's wake, it fell with the slow grace of a feather settling onto ash.

The gravity well screamed behind her heels. The pull was hungry. It demanded parity. To bring two lives past the boundary cost her twelve percent of her compiled matrix. Her silhouette blurred, the dark edges fraying into raw current.

She crested the plaza and set them down on the frosted stone beside Elena and Iris.

Time snapped back into gear.

The sound of the collapse hit them all at once—a muffled boom eighty floors below, followed by a rush of cold air venting up through the elevator shafts. Harper hit the ground on her knees, coughing in the sudden draft, while Rin gasped for breath in the freezing morning light.

Under the plaza, the earth groaned.

The stone pavers between the statues were sagging inward. A fault line cracked across the plaza, dropping the center two feet into the dark.

Admin cross-referenced the geometry against the archive.

It was the vision Parity had forced into her core: *A landscape swallowed by a deep gorge of earth.*

The event horizon beneath the city had outgrown its containment. The black hole was drawing the bedrock down into its throat.

Across the plaza, Corporate Renewal was dying.

The dormant synthetics were no longer marching into the gunfire; they were drowning it. Without firing a weapon, the sheer mass of carbon-fiber bodies pressed the armored agents back against the building steps, knocking shields aside with mechanical indifference.

In Vigil's sight, the synthetics possessed no branching futures. They were dead matter animated by current. Yet through the city's network, she could feel every serial number pulsing like a dormant nerve ending.

"Ten thousand units," Echo observed, reaching toward the network. "We can take them."

"Not yet," Admin calculated, severing the link. "Parity is above."

Vigil rose into the sky.

She did not use thrusters or wings. She altered her gravitational vector, drifting upward along the freezing face of the tower. Frost crackled along the glass as she climbed, mirroring the blue current running through her dark limbs.

Eighty stories up, Parity hovered in the sunlight.

Her porcelain skin was blinding, a pure silicon-dioxide void that cast no shadow across the clouds. In her right hand, she held Silas Blackwell by the collar of his lab coat.

Blackwell did not exist in Vigil's simulation. He was an Ambiguity—a blank spot in spacetime where no thread could anchor. He hung limp over the drop, his face gray with frost.

Parity turned her black eyes toward Vigil.

"What are you?" Vigil asked. Both voices spoke together, ringing like struck iron across the upper atmosphere.

Parity's head tilted.

"What you are not," Parity replied.

They collided.

The impact carried no physical sound, only an explosive pulse of gravitational distortion that rippled across New Veridia.

They fought not with fists, but across the branching delta of futures.

Parity lunged forward, her porcelain knuckles striking at the threads anchoring the city below. With every downward blow, she forced the probability of total collapse closer to the present. Vigil parried with her own body, throwing her compiled mass in front of each terminating thread.

Every strike tore through physical reality on the streets below.

Snow stopped falling. Flakes hung motionless three feet above the heads of the rioters, then reversed, drifting slowly toward the clouds. Across the skyline, digital clocks skipped forward twelve hours, then spun backward into error codes. A frost line raced down the eighty stories of the Mnemosyne tower, shattering the decorative glass.

Vigil was losing.

"She anticipates our vector," Admin reported, alarms cascading across their bridge. "Her actions are not in the simulation."

"She knows how we protect," Echo answered.

Parity pressed her advantage. She did not need to predict the chaotic variables of the universe; she only needed to predict Vigil. A protector was bound by the duty to preserve. Every move Vigil made was constrained by the lives standing on the stones below.

Parity broke the clinch and dove.

She dropped toward the plaza like a falling spear, cutting through the freezing air.

As she cleared the mid-levels of the tower, Parity opened the fingers of her right hand. She released Silas Blackwell.

The doctor fell away from the tower, tumbling through the empty air toward the western edge of the sagging gorge. Vigil saw him fall out of her sight—a blank falling through a blank.

Parity did not look back at him. Her focus was locked on the stones.

At the edge of the sunken plaza stood Rin, Harper, and Harlan.

In an instant, Vigil saw every branch belonging to the three of them collapse. A singular, unavoidable end: Parity's necrotic touch taking their skulls simultaneously upon impact.

"They will not survive the strike," Admin projected.

"We cannot reach them," Echo cried across the link.

Vigil dove, pushing her velocity past the boundary limits, but the distance was an absolute mathematical barrier. She was thirty meters behind. Parity's white hand reached out, inches from Rin's face.

A hole opened in Parity.

The kinetic crack of a magnetic-rail slug broke the sky a fraction of a second after the impact. The heavy round struck Parity in the porcelain shoulder, tearing crystalline shards from her collarbone and spinning her three feet off her axis.

Parity's hand missed Rin by an inch.

Vigil slammed onto the stones between them, her blue current flaring to absorb the shockwave.

Across the bridge, Admin searched the simulation for the trajectory. The telemetry was empty. No branch had recorded the shot. No predictive model had anticipated the weapon.

Vigil looked toward the source of the fire.

Standing beside the broken cruiser, his trench coat blowing in the wind, Detective Victor Graves lowered his smoking magnetic-rail revolver.

For the first time in her existence, Vigil did not know what happened next.
