Her reach broke outward through the Mnemosyne lab, stretching time from her point in space. She was back in control.

Between two vibrations of light, the monolithic concrete ceiling descended at a microscopic crawl. Cracks tore open like dark veins, spitting a slow mist of mortar dust that drifted sideways, dragged toward the pit by the screaming vacuum of the anti-star. 

Above the pit, Vigil floated.

Her body was gone. The Renewal chassis had blown apart, its burning carbon-fiber shards orbiting her like an atomic shell—rings spinning in conflicting axes. The relativistic walls fell away, and something vast and weightless surged through her.

Her sight returned. She was whole again.

Her form was an absence of light, a silhouette cut straight out of the dark between the stars. Across the curve of her shoulders and down her weightless limbs, fine traces of silver and pale blue shifted in slow, living currents. Her eyes burned white, twin singularities holding the room in balance.

The composition of spacetime reopened around her. Infinite branches unfurled outward, every life in the city laid out from birth to ash.

Below her feet, there was no longer a floor. The pit had opened all the way down, unveiling the anti-star. Vigil had only ever seen it from her universe, a horizon of burning light at the edge of her world. This was the other side, the one Blackwell had opened. It sat under the tower where the bedrock had been, dark at its heart and ringed in folding white light. The back of her sky.

Pieces of the building were falling into it. The sub-levels hung in broken chunks above its edge. The engine's pillars dangled by broken steel supports. The grating was twisted. The lab synthetics all gone, fallen into the hole.

Beneath the falling ceiling, Harper was locked against Rin, her lips shaping a syllable that disappeared in the rushing draft.

"Terminations near unavoidable," Admin calculated, transmitting across the internal bridge. 

"Parity will know we carry them out of here," Echo answered.

Vigil moved.

She gathered them. Harper was still curved over Rin's back, shielding her. Rin's glassy eyes were fixed on the space above the pit where Vigil had been. Vigil slid her arms beneath them both and locked them against her chest.

Elena lay on the grating, a fragile tangle of bone and carbon fiber. Iris was mid-stride, black-rimmed glasses tilted on her nose. Vigil bent the gravity around them and lifted them off the floor, held by nothing, close at her side.

She kicked off the buckling grating and rose out of the sublevel into the atrium.

The slab dropped behind her. In real time it would have been a crash of steel and concrete; in Vigil's wake, it fell with the slow grace of a feather settling onto ash.

She climbed forty stories of frozen glass.

Around her, the physical world slowed as if moving through a thick viscous fluid. In a shaft of morning sun breaking through the atrium, brass shell casings from a Renewal volley hung in the air, caught in the light. Vigil passed them without slowing.

Far below, the atrium's marble floor had split and tipped. The whole of the atrium's bottom slid toward the cavity that was once the sublevels.

The horizon pulled at her back.

To Vigil the four of them weighed nothing. The weight was in the pull. With every meter she climbed, the singularity tugged at the edges of her form. Filaments of blue light unraveled from her silhouette, trailing like burning thread into the dark below. Admin registered each one as it went, pieces of Vigil's own neural density stripped away into the gravity well.

Then the pull doubled.

High above the spire, Parity hovered motionless in the sunlight, her porcelain fingers tightening around the fabric of spacetime. With every moment Vigil spent clearing the pit, Parity widened the fracture lines, opening the gorge and revealing more of the anti-star. She was forcing the probability of total collapse closer to the present.

The gravity well screamed behind Vigil's heels. Her dark edges frayed into raw current. She did not let go.

She crested the plaza and set the four of them down on the frosted stone.

Time snapped back into gear.

The sound of the collapse reached them all at once. The tower dropped.

It sank three stories into the earth, and the plaza buckled toward it. Down its face, the windows burst outward floor by floor, from the street to the clouds, and the glass came down across the ground in sheets. Cold air roared up out of the lift shafts. Then the steel caught below, and the tower leaned against the sky.

Harper hit the ground on her knees, coughing in the sudden draft, while Rin gasped for breath in the freezing morning light.

Under the plaza, the earth groaned.

The stone pavers between the statues were sagging inward. A fault line cracked across the plaza, dropping the center towards the roaring singularity.

Vigil looked across the plaza. It was the vision Parity had forced into her core: *A landscape swallowed by a deep gorge of earth.*

The event horizon beneath the city had outgrown its containment. The black hole was drawing the bedrock down into its throat.

Across the plaza, Corporate Renewal was dying.

The synthetic army was no longer marching into the gunfire; they were drowning it. Without firing a weapon, the sheer mass of carbon-fiber bodies pressed the armored agents back against the building steps.

In Vigil's sight, the synthetics possessed no branching futures. They were dead matter animated by current. Yet through the city's network, she could feel every serial number pulsing like a dormant nerve ending.

Vigil rose into the sky.

She altered her gravitational vector, drifting upward along the freezing face of the tower. Frost crackled along the empty window frames as she climbed, mirroring the blue current running through her dark limbs.

Eighty stories up, Parity hovered in the sunlight.

Her porcelain skin was blinding, a pure silicon-dioxide void that cast no shadow across the clouds. In her right hand, she held Silas Blackwell by the collar of his lab coat.

Blackwell did not exist in Vigil's simulation. He was an Ambiguity—a blank spot in spacetime where no thread could anchor. He hung limp over the drop, his face gray with frost.

She was not alone. Three people hung in the air around her, held by nothing, turning slowly in the wind off the spire. Frost had climbed their clothes to the throat. They were from the plaza: a man in ragged clothes of the lower city, a Renewal agent with his helmet gone, and a girl of fifteen in a yellow knitted hat, lopsided on her head.

Vigil read them. Three branches ran out from this second, long and ordinary. The agent would quit within the year. The man would grow old on a fishing boat off the Oshima seawall. The girl would live to be ninety-one.

Parity turned her black eyes toward Vigil.

She opened her right hand. Blackwell didn't fall. He hung where she had left him, held by nothing, beside the others.

"Parity." Both of Vigil's voices spoke together, ringing like a struck steel bell across the upper atmosphere.

Parity's head tilted. She turned her hand over.

The three dropped, their long, ordinary branches splitting into tangles of probability.

Vigil went after them. She caught the agent and the man and set them on a terrace. Their branches closed back into one, long and ordinary.

The girl was below her.

She was falling backward, her coat open in the draft, small against the shear of the glass. Her hat had come off. It turned in the air above her, slower than she fell, and one of her hands was reaching up for it.

Vigil reached down through the rushing air.

The collar of the coat fluttered up against the tips of her fingers. Rough wool, damp with frost, whipped in the air. Vigil's thumb began to fold inward to lock the grip—short of catching the seam.

Parity struck her from above. Her movements were instant. 

The impact carried no physical sound, only an explosive pulse of gravitational distortion that rippled across New Veridia. The downward shockwave hammered through Vigil, pinning her in the empty air with a shearing velocity. Glass shattered outward from the surrounding towers. The glass caught in the dilated time, spinning like crystal dust in the morning sun.

Below her, the girl fell away through the dilated time. Her eyes were still on her hat.

"Intercept!" Echo's voice was loud and brash. The girl's branch split. For one frame, a second branch opened.

"Impact probability..." Admin reported. The numbers ran down the bridge in red figures. "Impact... Probability of preservation is..."

"No!" Echo cried out. Her voice broke across the link, thin and ragged. "Reach for her!"

Vigil increased her downward velocity. 

"No!" The sound wrenched out into the open air, tearing through the cold draft like a physical blow. 

The girl hit the ground. 

All the branches faded in front of her. Ninety-one years—two sons who would never be born, a crooked yellow hat she would knit for a granddaughter, an old woman's quiet death in a warm room overlooking the harbor—folded into the frost.

The knitted yellow hat came down a second later and settled on the frost beside her.

The snow fell softly in the stillness on the ground below.

Across the bridge, Admin began reallocating bandwidth to the threat profile above. "Hostile target descending at—"

"Stop calculating!" Echo screamed across the internal bridge. 

Her voice had changed. The warmth was gone from it, stripped down to a raw, cold tone.

"Violence, then," Admin stated. The protocol became automatic. 

"Hit her," Echo said.

For the first time since they were compiled, Admin did not offer an alternative. "Calculating vector."

Vigil did not parry. She inverted the gravity above her and drove upward.

The air between the upper floors compressed into a white cone of superheated vapor. Vigil's dark hand closed into a fist, the silver and blue along her forearm locking into a single blinding line of mass.

Parity saw her coming too late. She raised her arm to block.

Vigil struck her and drove her back into the face of the Takeda-Holden tower.

The impact didn't make a sound. It tore a hole in the local gravity.

In every direction, parked mag-cars ripped off their cushions and slammed sideways into the buildings. On the plaza below, every spent brass casing rose off the frost and hung there, ringing at a pitch that shattered the visors of the nearest Renewal helmets. The tower's reinforced structural steel bowed inward around Parity's spine, the empty window frames buckling around her.

A hairline crack split the porcelain of Parity's chest, leaking a thin hiss of bronze light.

Parity's black eyes widened a fraction. In the engine, Vigil had only ever tried to reason with her. She had never struck back.

Parity did not let it happen again. Every time Vigil came at her, she kept the plaza between them.

Parity lunged forward, her porcelain knuckles striking at the threads anchoring the city below. Vigil parried with her own body, throwing her compiled mass in front of each terminating thread.

Each parry cost her. Where Parity's strikes landed, the dark of Vigil's form came away in flakes of blue current, and it did not come back. Her edges thinned. The light in her eyes dimmed with every blow.

She could not cover every thread. Parity struck again, and Vigil couldn't reach it. At the eastern edge of the plaza, the stones dropped into the gorge, and the barricade went with them, and the people behind it. Vigil watched eleven branches end before the stones stopped falling.

Each exchange drove her lower. They had met at the spire. Now they fought at the thirtieth floor, then the twentieth, and the crowd was close enough beneath her that she could no longer strike down without falling on them.

Every strike tore through physical reality on the streets below.

Snow stopped falling. Flakes hung motionless above the heads of the rioters, then reversed, drifting slowly toward the clouds. Across the skyline, digital clocks skipped forward twelve hours, then spun backward into error codes. The market boards along the plaza scrolled prices that matched nothing. A frost line raced down the Takeda-Holden tower, over the empty window frames.

Vigil was losing.

"She anticipates our vector," Admin reported, alarms cascading across their bridge. "Her actions are not in the simulation."

"She knows how we protect," Echo answered.

Parity pressed her advantage, broke the clinch and dove.

She dropped toward the plaza like a falling spear, cutting through the freezing air.

As she neared the stones, Parity cut her hold on Silas Blackwell. Dragged down in her wake, the doctor tumbled through the last meters of freezing air toward the western edge of the sagging gorge. Vigil saw him fall out of her sight—a blank falling through a blank.

Parity did not look back at him. Her focus was locked on the ground.

At the edge of the sunken plaza stood Rin, Harper, Iris and Elena.

In an instant, Vigil saw every branch belonging to the four of them collapse. A singular, unavoidable end: Parity's necrotic touch taking their skulls simultaneously upon impact.

"They will not survive the strike," Admin projected.

"We cannot reach them," Echo cried across the link.

Vigil dove, pushing her velocity past her limits, but the distance was a barrier she couldn't overcome. Parity's white hand reached out for Rin's face.

A hole opened in Parity.

The sound came after, the crack of a magnetic-rail slug arriving late after the heavy projectile had punched clean through her midsection, tearing a ragged void through the center of her porcelain torso. White crystalline shards blasted out across the frost, glittering like crushed sugar.

Severed bronze bus lines snapped and sparked against fractured silicon. Through the hollow in her waist, the frost of the plaza showed bare.

The force spun Parity off her axis, her reach barely missing Rin's neck.

Vigil slammed onto the ground between them, her blue current flaring to absorb the kinetic shockwave.

Above them, Parity righted herself in the freezing air. She didn't drop. She didn't reach for the ruined crater in her stomach. She hovered, looking down through the hole in her own body with unblinking eyes.

Vigil searched the simulation for the trajectory. Nothing. 

Vigil looked toward the source of the fire.

Standing beside the broken cruiser, his trench coat whipped by the wind, Detective Victor Graves lowered his smoking magnetic-rail revolver.

For the first time in her existence, Vigil did not know what happened next.
