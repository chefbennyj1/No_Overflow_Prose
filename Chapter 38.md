Thirty yards past the cruiser, the kid hit the plaza floor.

A girl in a ragged coat and a yellow knitted hat fell straight out of the sky onto the frost. 

Beside the open door, Victor's hand tightened on the revolver at his thigh. Across from him, the Minder still had a gun pressed into Harlan's ribs, his eyes turned up toward the spire.

Something dark dropped out of the glare and landed on its knees beside the dead girl. A sound tore out of it—two voices at once, like iron wrenching apart—and then it shot straight back up into the sky.

High up on the face of the tower, the steel caved inward around the white thing in dead silence.

A second later, the shockwave hit the plaza.

The blast ripped the Madame's cruiser off its magnetic cushion and flipped it onto its roof. Harlan was thrown clear across the frost. Three tons of the cruisers shiny black steel came down on the who was  Minder holding him, crushing him into the plaza floor under the crumpled frame. 

Victor hit the ground on his shoulder and came up on one knee, his revolver still in his hand. He looked toward Harlan, and took a deep breath when he saw Harlan slowly steady himself to his feet, coughing in the snow.

Down by the gorge, the dark shape dropped out of the sky a second time, threw a man in a white lab coat down into the frost beside Erin Adler, and rose back into the fight.

*Blackwell.*

Victor looked from the white lab coat to the front of the plaza.

The Madame was still on her feet. Her head came down from the sky, her wire-rimmed glasses fixing on the doctor at the edge of the drop.

In the cruiser, she had told him she came for the what was in the lab. The lab under the tower had caved into the gorge, and nobody was putting a leash on the things in the sky. 

Nobody stood between Victor and her back. Nobody had a gun on Harlan anymore.

Victor raised the revolver.

The rails woke against his palm, a charge crawling up into his wrist. He settled the front sight square on the back of the Madame's head, right above the collar of her white silk. Everything his father had been—every visit to that green door, an old man hollowed out as a Minder and left to rot in front of a cracked window—sat at the end of the barrel.

His finger took up the slack on the trigger.

Above the plaza, the white thing broke away from the dark one and dove.

It dropped like a spear, cutting straight down toward the edge of the gorge. At the bottom of its path, Erin Adler was on her knees on the frost.

The dark one was behind it, too far back to reach her.

Victor's eyes burned in the glare. Through the water in them, he held the back of the Madame's head in his sights for one more fraction of a second.

Then he wrenched the barrel off her, tracked the white blur above Erin Adler's face, and fired.

The revolver kicked hard in his hand. Smoke came off the rails in a thin gray line and bent away on the wind.

Across the plaza, the white thing hung in the air with a hole through its gut.

Victor could see the frost through it. The stones beyond, a spent casing, the dark edge of the gorge. Nothing ran out of the wound. The edges of it were white all the way in.

It turned its head and looked at him.

His eyes filled at once. The white of it burned, and the plaza swam. He blinked hard and made himself look. He had put a hole in it. He was going to look at it.

It came down out of the air.

It crossed the plaza slowly, a few feet above the ground, and the people in its path threw themselves flat. A Renewal agent let go of his rifle and crawled. Where it passed, the snow stopped falling and hung in the air behind it.

Victor didn't move.

The sun was at his back. His shadow ran long across the frost in front of him, and the white thing stopped at the end of it, close enough to touch. Its own shadow ran out behind it across the plaza, longer than his and blacker. The sun came through the hole in its gut and lay in the middle of that shadow, a white spot on the frost.

Up close, it had a woman's face. The skin was porcelain, with lines of bronze circuitry that seared across its body. The wind pulled at Victor's coat, but it didn't move a strand of its straight black hair hanging down its back. The eyes were black all the way across.

Through the hole in its gut, he could see Erin Adler on her knees on the frost.

The black eyes locked on his face.

"I see every life in this city whole," it said.

One voice, flat and calm. Nothing in the porcelain face moved. The sound resonated up through the stones under his boots, the crumpled door of the cruiser, and the steel of the revolver in his hand.

"Every one of them, from first breath to last. The probability of a second blind spot was zero." Its head tilted a fraction. "Yet here you stand. You, I see only here."

Victor's eyes ran. He let them. *A second blind spot?* It's words were making no sense to him. 

"Tell me..." The voice vibrated against his palm through the grip of the gun. "Are you a good man?"

Victor had heard it before.

Wet tile. An alarm going off in the dark. A machine with his gun in its hand, asking him that in two voices.

Same words. Same pause in the middle. One voice now instead of two.

Whatever they were, they were the same thing.

"No," Victor said.

The black eyes stayed on him. Nothing in the face moved.

"Then you will run."

His thumb found the striker of the lighter in his pocket. He rolled it once.

He didn't run.

It waited. Victor had sat across a table from a lot of people who were waiting for him to do what they had already decided he would do. He knew the look they got when he didn't. This one didn't have a face for it. It went on waiting anyway.

Behind it, every synthetic on the plaza lit at once.

The serials came up blue under their eyes, hundreds of them, from the Renewal steps to the lip of the gorge. Then their eyes. The light came off them in one tearing flare, the same light that had taken the interview room, and Victor turned his face from it out of old habit.

That was how he saw the dark one.

It stood where it had landed, in front of Erin Adler, a shape cut out of the morning. Silver ran across it in fine lines. It wasn't looking at the white thing. It was looking at the army.

The army turned.

They had been pressing the Renewal line back against the tower steps. Every one of them stopped. Together they turned their backs on the guns and faced the middle of the plaza.

At the front of the plaza, the Madame raised her hand.

Nothing happened.

She raised it higher. The bodies on either side of her began to walk, and none of them looked at her.

Victor looked.

For the first time since he had known her name, her face moved. The mouth came open. The long filter tipped in her fingers, and the cigarette dropped out of it onto the frost.

She found him across the plaza. She looked at him over the top of her wire-rimmed glasses.

*You should see how it ends.*

Victor turned his eyes back to the white thing.

The army went over the place where she had stood. He heard it. He didn't look.

They came past him on both sides, close enough to drag at his coat. Plastic sheeting slid across his boots. None of them touched him. The ranks split around the place where he stood and closed again in front of him, and then they were on the white thing.

They hit it from every side at once.

It didn't look at them. Bodies broke against it and came apart in the air, carbon fiber and blue coolant raining over the frost, and it kept its black eyes on Victor. It came forward through them.

Its hand came up, open, reaching for his face. The cold came off it before it touched him. Frost climbed the sleeve of his coat.

Victor raised the revolver. The rails woke against his palm.

He fired into its face.

The crack went flat across the stones. Half the face came away. Underneath, it was white too, sheared crystal and torn bronze wire, and one black eye sitting in a socket of shattered glass. It was still looking at him.

The hand kept coming.

It laid its palm flat on his chest.

The cold went in through his coat, and then it was inside him. His heart pushed once against it.

Then the dark one hit it.

Victor didn't see it come. The white hand was on him, and then it was gone, and the white thing went backward across the plaza with the dark one locked around it. The army poured after them, and all of it was moving toward the gorge.

His legs went. The stones came up and hit his shoulder.

He lay on his side on the frost. His breath went out white and came back smaller.

Down the plaza, at the edge of the gorge, a man in a lab coat was getting up on one elbow. The dark one and the white thing went past him toward the drop, and the army went with them.

Someone was shouting his name.

Harlan was running toward him across the frost, his tie flying loose. Going the wrong way. He was supposed to be leaving.

Victor wanted to tell him to go home. His mouth wouldn't make it.

Over Harlan's shoulder, the sun had come up clear of the towers. It was in Victor's eyes.

It didn't hurt.

He kept them open.
