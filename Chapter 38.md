Victor heard a sound rip across the plaza from above, two layered voices, like hardened steel wrenching apart.

A girl in a ragged coat and a yellow knitted hat fell out of the sky. Thirty yards past the cruiser, she hit the plaza floor.

"No!" Harlan's voice was choked. "What is that thing?" 
Across from Victor, the Minder still had a gun pressed into Harlan's ribs, his eyes turned up toward the spire.

Beside the open door, Victor's hand tightened on the revolver at his thigh. 

Something dark dropped out of the sky. Like a shadow of a woman. It landed on its knees beside the dead girl. Its eyes glowed burning hot. Something triggered inside Victor's memory. He recognized the sheen. The shadow turned its gaze upward, and shot straight up toward the spire.

High up on the face of the tower, the steel caved inward around the white entity. The shockwave slammed into the plaza, ripping the Madame's cruiser off its magnetic cushion and flipping it onto its roof. Harlan was thrown clear across the fallen snow. Three tons of the cruiser's black steel came down on the Minder who was holding him, crushing him into the plaza floor under the crumpled frame. 

Victor hit the ground on his shoulder and came up on one knee, his revolver still in his hand. He looked toward Harlan, and took a deep breath when he saw him slowly steady himself to his feet, coughing in the snow.

Beyond the wrecked cruiser, the Madame was still on her feet.  She had told him she came for what was in the lab. The lab under the tower had caved into the gorge, and nobody was putting a leash on the things in the sky. 

There was nothing between Victor and the Madame's back. Nobody had a gun on Harlan anymore.

Victor got to his feet and raised the revolver.

The rails woke against his palm, a charge crawling up into his wrist. He settled the front sight square on the back of the Madame's head, right above the collar of her white silk. Everything his father had been—every visit, an old man hollowed out as a Minder and left to rot in front of a cracked window—sat at the end of the barrel.

His finger pressed against the slack on the trigger.

Overhead, the two entities tore at each other above the crowd. The east edge of the plaza broke away into the gorge with people still standing on it. The snow stopped falling. Ice crystals suspended in the air, caught in the morning sun sparkling blue, violet, and green.

Victor paused. It was beautiful.

He focused the sight back on the Madame. He couldn't have found a more perfect moment to collect the Madame's debt—to make her pay. 

The flakes of snow began to rise, drifting backward toward the sun.

Above the plaza, the white figure broke away from the dark one and dove.

It dropped like a spear, cutting straight down toward the edge of the gorge, and slammed into the plaza floor directly in front of Erin Adler. A torrent of fallen snow plumed beneath its feet, then slowed—locking mid-air in the disruption around it as Erin knelt on the frost.

The dark entity was behind it, too far back to reach her.

Victor's eyes burned in the glare. He held the back of the Madame's head in his sights for another moment.

His finger eased the tension off the trigger. He let out a breath that fogged in front of him, then drew a sharp, freezing lungful as he wrenched the barrel off the Madame, tracked the white figure above Erin Adler, and fired.

The revolver kicked hard in his hand. Smoke came off the rails in a thin gray line and bent away on the wind.

Above the gorge, the white entity hung in the air with a hole through its gut. He had put a hole in it. 

Victor could see the frost through it. The plaza beyond, the dark edge of the gorge. Nothing ran out of the wound. The edges of it were white.

It turned its head and looked at him.

His eyes watered at once. The white of the entity burned into his eyes. He blinked hard and made himself look. 

It glided toward him a foot above the ground, its black eyes locked squarely on Victor's albinotic ones.

It didn't look at the people scrambling in its path. One by one, it killed them without turning its head.

A Renewal guard raised his rifle; the entity flicked two fingers to the side, and the man's helmet and skull caved inward with a wet crunch before he hit the stone. A rioter stumbled in front of it, and a wave of its hand flash-froze the man mid-scream—when its shoulder brushed past him, his chest shattered across the plaza like dropped porcelain. Another man was lifted off his feet and twisted in the air until his spine snapped, then dropped aside like trash.

"Vic!" Harlan called out from behind the wrecked cruiser. "What are you doing?"

Victor kept his eyes locked on the white figure. "Harlan, stay back!"

Where it passed, the snow stopped falling and hung in the air behind it.

Victor didn't move.

The sun was at his back. His shadow ran long across the frost in front of him, and the white figure stopped at the end of it, close enough to touch. Its own shadow ran along the snow behind it. The sun came through the hole in its gut and lay in the middle of its shadow, a white spot on the frost.

Up close, it had a woman's face. Her skin was porcelain, with lines of bronze circuitry that seared across her body. The wind pulled at Victor's coat, but it didn't move a strand of the straight black hair hanging down her back. Her eyes were black all the way across.

Through the hole in her gut, he could see Erin Adler on her knees on the frost.

The black eyes locked on his face.

"I see every life," she spoke. 

One voice, flat and calm. Nothing in the porcelain face moved. The sound resonated up through the steel in the collapsing building, under his boots, and the steel of the revolver in his hand.

"Every one of them, from first breath to last. The probability of a second Ambiguity is zero." Her head tilted a fraction. "Yet here you stand. You, I see only here."

Victor's eyes ran. *An Ambiguity?* Victor had heard that before.

"Tell me..." The voice vibrated against his palm through the grip of the gun. "Are you a good man?"

The morning with Erin Adler. Wet tile. An alarm going off in the dark. A machine with his gun in its hand, asking him that in two voices.

Same words. Same pause in the middle. One voice now instead of two.

Whatever they were, they were the same thing.

"No," Victor said.

Her black eyes stayed on him. Nothing in her face moved. He steadied the weight of his revolver, securing his grip.

He didn't run.

Her hand came up toward his throat. The cold came off her fingers before they reached him. Frost climbed over his collar and stiffened it against his neck.

Victor didn't look away.

The hand stopped—hanging there, next to his skin. Her face hovered close to Victor's, but the focus changed, her black eyes shifted upward.

"So, this is Takeda-Holden's big secret, is it?"

The voice came from right behind her, low and reverent.

She lowered her hand and pivoted slowly, head and shoulders locked together in a single rigid turn until her back was to him.

Victor dropped to a knee, taking in a ragged breath. Harlan started forward to help him, but Victor threw up a palm and shook his head, stopping him in mid-step.

The Madame was already standing close in the frost, the long filter lowered from her mouth. Snow had settled on the shoulders of her white silk. Behind her, her army had stopped dead against the Renewal line on the tower steps. Hundreds of serials glowed at the exact same height. Not one of them moved.

Victor knelt with the revolver at his side. He had felt the cold of those fingers on his throat. He had been dead. Now she had turned her back on him, and the Madame had her attention.

The Madame looked up into the white face through her wire-rimmed glasses, her head in a slight bow, her eyes daring and hungry behind the lenses.

"Takeda-Holden kept *you* in a box under that tower?" the Madame said softly, shaking her head as if it were a crime. "Such a shame they did that to you... And now, all this happening."

The white figure said nothing.

The Madame's voice dropped, warm, and velvety—the one Victor had heard her use when she was about to buy a man's soul and make him thank her for it.

"You don't belong in their basement," she said. "And you shouldn't have to dirty yourself with the insects down here. That army is mine." She gestured with an open palm toward the ranks behind her. "The police in this city are mine. Every street, every port, every terminal in Oshima answers to me—and I can lay all of it at your feet." A sycophantic smile pulled at the deep lines around her mouth. "Tell me what you want, and it's yours."

The ceramic woman drifted closer to the Madame over the frost, and the snow stopped falling between them. The Madame's eyes went once to the hole in her gut and came back to the porcelain face. The filter shook in the old woman's fingers, and she stilled it.

The long black shadow fell over the Madame and covered her to the feet.

"There," the Madame whispered, breathless. "You see? You can be very useful to—" She stopped. "We can be very useful to each other."

She reached up, as if touching an altar, and laid her hand on the porcelain cheek.

Frost came up white over her diamond rings. It climbed her knuckles to the wrist. 

The white face tilted into her palm, leaning into the touch.

"Useful..." the entity said.

The sound came up through the stones softer this time, almost mimicking the warm cadence of the Madame's own voice.

The Madame's breath caught. Her smile widened, triumphant, the hunger in her eyes sharpening behind the wire-rimmed lenses. She thought she had it. Victor could see it in the set of her shoulders—she was already measuring the leash.

"Tell me what you want," the voice said through the frost, "and it's yours."

"Yes," the Madame whispered.

The entity slowly raised her white hand. Her porcelain fingers delicately brushing the collar of the Madame's silk.

"I see you whole, Madame," she said softly. "Every way you end. And in all of them, you give me everything I want."

Her fingers closed around the Madame's throat.

The sound was small. A twig under a boot.

The Madame's head went over to one side, farther than a head could go. The long filter dropped out of her fingers. The porcelain hand opened, and the Madame folded down into the snow and lay still. 

Victor couldn't move.

He had chased her his whole career. He had wanted her dead for longer than that. He had pictured it a thousand ways, and none of them looked like this. One hand. Less than a second.

Behind the body, her army stood in its rows. Nobody owned them now.

She didn't look down at the snow. She turned back to Victor.

A gun barked three times from beside the wrecked cruiser.

"Get away from him!" Harlan shouted, his service pistol kicking in both hands.

The entity didn't even turn her head. She knew Harlan's whole life—she had already seen every bullet before his finger found the trigger. Without taking her black eyes off Victor, she raised her left hand to the side and closed her porcelain fist in midair.

Three cracks snapped against her palm. She opened her fingers, and three flattened brass-and-lead slugs dropped onto the frost, steaming in the snow.

Slowly, her head turned toward Harlan.

Harlan's slide locked back on an empty chamber, his tie blowing loose across his vest. He didn't step back.

She took a step toward him.

*No.* Harlan had a wife packing boxes. He had a ticket out of Oshima.

Victor raised the revolver. The rails woke against his palm.

"Hey!" Victor barked.

He fired straight into her face.

The crack went flat across the stones. She hadn't seen it coming. Half her porcelain face came away in a spray of white crystal and torn bronze light, spinning her head back toward him. One black eye sat in a socket of shattered glass, locking back onto Victor's face.

She forgot Harlan. She came straight at Victor.

Her hand came up, open, the cold rolling off her fingers before she touched him.

She laid her palm flat on his chest.

The cold went in through his coat, and then it was inside him. His heart pushed once against it.

Then the dark one hit her.

Victor didn't see it come. The white hand was on him, and then it was gone, and she went backward across the plaza with the dark one locked around her. 

His legs went. The stones came up and hit his shoulder.

He lay on his side on the frost. His breath went out white and came back smaller.

Someone was shouting his name.

Harlan was running toward him across the frost, his tie flying loose. He was alive. 
Over Harlan's shoulder, the sun had come up clear of the towers. It was in Victor's eyes, for the first time the light didn't hurt.
A smile crossed his lips; a final breath left his lungs in Harlan's arms.
