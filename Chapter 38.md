Victor heard a sound rip across the plaza from above, two layered voices, like hardened steel wrenching apart.

A girl in a ragged coat and a yellow knitted hat fell out of the sky. Thirty yards past the cruiser, she hit the plaza floor.

"No!" Harlan's voice was choked. "What is that thing?" 
Across from Victor, the Minder still had a gun pressed into Harlan's ribs, his eyes turned up toward the spire.

Beside the open door, Victor's hand tightened on the revolver at his thigh. 

Something dark dropped out of the sky. Like a shadow of a woman. It landed on its knees beside the dead girl. It's eyes glowed burning hot. Something triggered inside Victor's memory. He recognized the sheen. The shadow turned it's gaze upward, and shot straight up toward the spire.

High up on the face of the tower, the steel caved inward around the white entity. The shockwave slammed into the plaza, ripping the Madame's cruiser off its magnetic cushion and flipping it onto its roof. Harlan was thrown clear across the fallen snow. Three tons of the cruiser's black steel came down on the Minder who was holding him, crushing him into the plaza floor under the crumpled frame. 

Victor hit the ground on his shoulder and came up on one knee, his revolver still in his hand. He looked toward Harlan, and took a deep breath when he saw him slowly steady himself to his feet, coughing in the snow.

Victor looked to the front of the plaza. The Madame was still on her feet. 

In the cruiser, she had told him she came for what was in the lab. The lab under the tower had caved into the gorge, and nobody was putting a leash on the things in the sky. 

Nobody stood between Victor and her back. Nobody had a gun on Harlan anymore.

Victor got to his feet and raised the revolver.

The rails woke against his palm, a charge crawling up into his wrist. He settled the front sight square on the back of the Madame's head, right above the collar of her white silk. Everything his father had been—every visit to that green door, an old man hollowed out as a Minder and left to rot in front of a cracked window—sat at the end of the barrel.

His finger took up the slack on the trigger.

Overhead, the two entities tore at each other above the crowd. The east edge of the plaza broke away into the gorge with people still standing on it. The snow stopped coming down. It started going up.

He kept the sight on her.

Above the plaza, the white figure broke away from the dark one and dove.

It dropped like a spear, cutting straight down toward the edge of the gorge. At the bottom of its path, Erin Adler was on her knees on the frost.

The dark entity was behind it, too far back to reach her.

Harlan was already running for the gorge.

Victor's eyes burned in the glare. He held the back of the Madame's head in his sights for one more fraction of a second.

Then he wrenched the barrel off her, tracked the white blur above Erin Adler's face, and fired.

The revolver kicked hard in his hand. Smoke came off the rails in a thin gray line and bent away on the wind.

Across the plaza, the white entity hung in the air with a hole through its gut.

Victor could see the frost through it. The plaza beyond, the dark edge of the gorge. Nothing ran out of the wound. The edges of it were white.

It turned its head and looked at him.

His eyes watered at once. The white of it burned, and the plaza swam. He blinked hard and made himself look. He had put a hole in it. He was going to look at it.


It glided across the plaza slowly, a feet above the ground, and the people in its path threw themselves flat. A Renewal agent let go of his rifle and crawled. Where it passed, the snow stopped falling and hung in the air behind it.

Victor didn't move.

The sun was at his back. His shadow ran long across the frost in front of him, and the white thing stopped at the end of it, close enough to touch. Its own shadow ran out behind it across the plaza, longer than his and blacker. The sun came through the hole in its gut and lay in the middle of that shadow, a white spot on the frost.

Up close, it had a woman's face. Her skin was porcelain, with lines of bronze circuitry that seared across her body. The wind pulled at Victor's coat, but it didn't move a strand of the straight black hair hanging down her back. Her eyes were black all the way across.

Through the hole in her gut, he could see Erin Adler on her knees on the frost.

The black eyes locked on his face.

"I see every life," she spoke. 

One voice, flat and calm. Nothing in the porcelain face moved. The sound resonated up through the steel in the collapsing building, under his boots, and the steel of the revolver in his hand.

"Every one of them, from first breath to last. The probability of a second Ambiguity is zero." Her head tilted a fraction. "Yet here you stand. You, I see only here."

Victor's eyes ran. *An Ambiguity?* Victor had heard that before.

"Tell me..." The voice vibrated against his palm through the grip of the gun. "Are you a good man?"

The morning with Erin Adler. Wet tile. An alarm going off in the dark. A machine with his gun in its hand, asking him that in two voices.

Same words. Same pause in the middle. One voice now instead of two.

Whatever they were, they were the same thing.

"No," Victor said.

Her black eyes stayed on him. Nothing in her face moved. His thumb found the striker of the lighter in his pocket. He rolled it once.

He didn't run.

Her hand came up toward his throat. The cold came off her fingers before they reached him. Frost climbed over his collar and stiffened it against his neck.

Victor didn't look away.

The hand stopped.

It hung there, a breath from his skin. Her black eyes were still on his face, but they had stopped looking at him.

"So, this is Takeda-Holden's big secret, Is it?"

The voice came from right behind her, low and almost reverent.

She lowered her hand and turned slowly, the whole body turning with the head, until her back was to Victor.

The Madame was already standing there, three paces off in the frost, the long filter lowered from her mouth. Snow had settled on the shoulders of her white silk. Behind her, her army had stopped where it stood against the Renewal line on the tower steps. Hundreds of serials glowed at the same height. Not one of them moved.

Victor stood with the revolver at his side. He had felt the cold of those fingers on his throat. He had been dead. Now she had turned her back on him, and the Madame hadn't even looked at him.

The Madame looked up into the white face through her wire-rimmed glasses, her head tilted in a slight bow, her eyes bright and hungry behind the lenses.

"Takeda-Holden kept *you* in a box under that tower?" the Madame said softly, shaking her head as if it were a crime. "Such a shame they did that to you... And now, all this happening."

The white figure said nothing.

The Madame's voice dropped, warm, and velvety—the one Victor had heard her use when she was about to buy a man's soul and make him thank her for it.

"You don't belong in their basement," she said. "And you shouldn't have to dirty yourself with the insects down here. That army is mine." She gestured with an open palm toward the ranks behind her. "The police in this city are mine. Every street, every port, every terminal in Oshima answers to me—and I can lay all of it at your feet." A sycophantic smile pulled at the deep lines around her mouth. "Tell me what you want, and it's yours."

She drifted closer to the Madame over the frost, and the snow stopped falling between them. The Madame's eyes went once to the hole in her gut and came back to the porcelain face. The filter shook in the old woman's fingers, and she stilled it.

The long black shadow fell over the Madame and covered her to the feet.

"There," the Madame whispered, breathless. "You see? I can be very useful to you."

She reached up, as if touching an altar, and laid her hand on the porcelain cheek.

Frost came up white over her diamond rings. It climbed her knuckles to the wrist. 

The white face tilted into her palm, leaning into the touch.

"Useful to me," the entity said.

The sound came up through the stones softer this time, almost mimicking the warm cadence of the Madame's own voice.

The Madame's breath caught. Her smile widened, triumphant, the hunger in her eyes sharpening behind the wire-rimmed lenses. She thought she had it. Victor could see it in the set of her shoulders—she was already measuring the leash.

"Tell me what you want," the voice said through the frost, "and it's yours."

"Yes," the Madame whispered.

The entity slowly raised her white hand. Her porcelain fingers delicately brushing the collar of the Madame's silk.

"I see you whole, Madame," she said softly. "Every way you end. And in all of them, you give me everything I want."

Her fingers closed around the Madame's throat.

The sound was small. A twig under a boot.

The Madame's head went over to one side, farther than a head could go. The long filter dropped out of her fingers. The porcelain hand opened, and the Madame folded down into the snow and lay still. 

Victor couldn't move.

He had chased her his whole career. He had wanted her dead for longer than that. He had pictured it a thousand ways, and none of them looked like this. One hand. Less than a second.

His thumb had stopped on the striker of the lighter.

Behind the body, her army stood in its rows. Nobody owned them now.

She didn't look down at the snow. She turned back to Victor.

Behind her, every synthetic on the plaza lit at once.

The serials came up blue under their eyes, hundreds of them, from the Renewal steps to the lip of the gorge. Then their eyes. The light came off them in one tearing flare, the same light that had taken the interview room, and Victor turned his face from it out of old habit.

That was how he saw the dark one.

It stood where it had landed, in front of Erin Adler, a shape cut out of the morning. Silver ran across it in fine lines. It wasn't looking at the white one. It was looking at the army.

The army turned.

Along the tower steps, every one of them put its back to the Renewal guns and faced the middle of the plaza. Then they walked.

The army went over the place where the Madame lay. He heard it. He didn't look.

They came past him on both sides, close enough to drag at his coat. Plastic sheeting slid across his boots. None of them touched him. The ranks split around the place where he stood and closed again in front of him, and then they were on her.

They hit her from every side at once.

She didn't look at them. Bodies broke against her and came apart in the air, carbon fiber and blue coolant raining over the frost, and she kept her black eyes on Victor. She came forward through them.

Her hand came up again, open, reaching for his face. The cold came off it before she touched him. Frost climbed the sleeve of his coat.

Victor raised the revolver. The rails woke against his palm.

He fired into her face.

The crack went flat across the stones. Half her face came away. Underneath, it was white too, sheared crystal and torn bronze wire, and one black eye sitting in a socket of shattered glass. She was still looking at him.

Her hand kept coming.

She laid her palm flat on his chest.

The cold went in through his coat, and then it was inside him. His heart pushed once against it.

Then the dark one hit her.

Victor didn't see it come. The white hand was on him, and then it was gone, and she went backward across the plaza with the dark one locked around her. The army poured after them, and all of it was moving toward the gorge.

His legs went. The stones came up and hit his shoulder.

He lay on his side on the frost. His breath went out white and came back smaller.

Down the plaza, at the edge of the gorge, a man in a lab coat was getting up on one elbow. The two of them went past him toward the drop, and the army went with them.

Someone was shouting his name.

Harlan was running toward him across the frost, his tie flying loose. Going the wrong way. He was supposed to be leaving.

Victor wanted to tell him to go home. His mouth wouldn't make it.

Over Harlan's shoulder, the sun had come up clear of the towers. It was in Victor's eyes.

It didn't hurt.

He kept them open.
