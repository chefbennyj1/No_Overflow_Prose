The magnetic rails were scorching hot against Victor's palm. A thin trail of gray smoke peeled off the barrel and whipped away on the freezing wind.

Above the gorge, the white entity hung suspended in the air with a hole torn clean through its gut.

Victor could see the frost through it. The plaza beyond, the dark edge of the gorge. Nothing ran out of the wound. The edges of it were white.

Ten seconds ago, the Madame had been in his sights. The shockwave from the tower had flipped her cruiser onto its roof, throwing Harlan clear and crushing the Minder who held him under a wreckage of black steel. Beyond the debris, the Madame was still on her feet. Victor had settled his sight square on the back of her head. Everything his father had been—every visit, an old man hollowed out as a Minder—sat at the end of the barrel.

Then the white figure dropped like a spear. It came to a halt at the ground, looming over Erin Adler. *She would die.*  Victor wrenched the barrel off the Madame, throwing away the only shot he had waited his whole life for.

The entity turned its head and looked at him.

His eyes watered at once. The reflection of the morning sun glared off it, burning through the tears in his eyes. He blinked hard, forcing himself to look.

It glided toward him a foot above the ground, its black eyes locked squarely on Victor's albinotic ones.

A man scrambling across the frost stumbled into its path. It didn't turn its head. The man was lifted off his feet, twisted in the air, and dropped lifeless in the snow.

"Victor!" Harlan called out from behind the wrecked cruiser. "What are you doing?"

Victor kept his eyes locked on the white figure. "Harlan, stay back!"

Where it passed, the snow stopped falling and hung in the air behind it.

Victor didn't move.

The sun was at his back. His shadow ran long across the frost in front of him, and the white figure stopped at the end of it, close enough to touch. Its own shadow ran along the snow behind it. The sun came through the hole in its gut and lay in the middle of its shadow, a white spot on the frost.

Up close, it had a woman's face. Her skin was porcelain, with lines of bronze circuitry that seared across her body. The wind pulled at Victor's coat, but it didn't move a strand of the straight black hair hanging down her back. Her eyes were black all the way across.

Through the hole in her gut, he could see Erin Adler on her knees on the frost.

The black eyes locked on his face.

"I see every life," she spoke.

One voice, flat and calm. Her lips didn't part. The sound came through the stones of the plaza and the cold air around him.

"Every one of them, from first breath to last. The probability of a second obscurity is zero."

Her head tilted a fraction.

"Yet." The tone dropped, harsh and grinding, scraping through the groaning frame of the tower. "Here." The revolver rattled against his palm. "You." Nothing in her porcelain face moved as the sound tore wider, heavy with fury, and shook the snow off the wrecked cruiser. "Stand."

The word slammed up through the soles of his boots and locked his breath in his throat.

Then the sound dropped back to a hollow whisper. "You, I see only here."

Victor's eyes ran. *An obscurity?*

"Tell me... Are you a good man?"

The morning with Erin Adler. Wet tile. An alarm going off in the dark. A machine with his gun in its hand, asking him that in two voices.

Same words. Same pause in the middle. One voice now instead of two.

Whatever they were, they were the same thing.

"No," Victor said.

Her black eyes stayed on him. Nothing in her face moved. He tightened his grip on the revolver.

He didn't run.

Her hand came up toward his throat. The cold came off her fingers before they reached him. Frost climbed over his collar and stiffened it against his neck.

Victor didn't look away.

The hand stopped above his skin. Her face hovered close to his, then her focus shifted—her black eyes looking past him.

"So, this is Takeda-Holden's big secret, is it?"

The voice came from right behind her, low and reverent.

She lowered her hand and pivoted slowly, head and shoulders locked together in a single rigid turn until her back was to him.

Victor dropped to a knee, taking in a ragged breath. Harlan started forward to help him, but Victor threw up a palm and shook his head, stopping him in mid-step.

The Madame was already standing close in the frost, the long filter lowered from her mouth. Snow had settled on the shoulders of her white silk. Behind her, her army had stopped dead against the Renewal line on the tower steps. Hundreds of serials glowed at the exact same height. Not one of them moved.

The cold of her fingers was still on his throat. A second ago he had been dead; now she wasn't even looking at him.

The Madame looked up into the white face, her head in a slight bow, her eyes daring and hungry.

"Takeda-Holden kept *you* in a box under that tower?" the Madame said softly, shaking her head as if it were a crime. "Such a shame they did that to you... And now, all this happening."

The white figure said nothing.

The Madame's voice dropped, warm and velvety—the one Victor had heard her use when she was about to buy a man's soul and make him thank her for it.

"You don't belong in their basement," she said. "And you shouldn't have to dirty yourself with the insects down here. That army is mine." She gestured with an open palm toward the ranks behind her. "The police in this city are mine. Every street, every port, every terminal in Oshima answers to me—and I can lay all of it at your feet." A sycophantic smile pulled at the deep lines around her mouth. "Tell me what you want, and it's yours."

The ceramic woman drifted closer to the Madame. The snow stopped falling between them. The Madame's eyes went once to the hole in her gut and came back to the porcelain face. The filter shook in the old woman's fingers as the long black shadow fell over her.

"There," the Madame whispered, breathless. "You see? You can be very useful to me—" She stopped. "We can be very useful to each other."

She reached up, as if touching an altar, and laid her hand on the entity's porcelain face.

Frost climbed over her diamond rings, from her knuckles to her wrist. 

The white face tilted, leaning into the touch.

"Useful..." the entity said.

The sound came up through the stones softer this time, almost mimicking the warm cadence of the Madame's own voice.

The Madame's smile widened, triumphant, a spark in her eyes sharpening. Victor could see it in the set of her shoulders—she was already measuring the leash.

The entity slowly raised her white hand, porcelain fingers brushing the the Madame's silk collar.

"Yes," the Madame whispered. He tone motherly, but with a sick twist of devious intent.

"I see you, Madame," the entity said softly. "Every way you end. And in all of them, you give me everything I want."

Her fingers closed around the Madame's throat.

The sound was small. A twig under a boot.

The Madame's head went over to one side, farther than a head could go. The long filter dropped out of her fingers. The porcelain hand opened, and the Madame folded down into the snow and lay still. 

Victor remained motionless.

He had chased her his whole career. He had wanted her dead for longer than that. He had pictured it a thousand ways, and none of them looked like this. One hand. Less than a second.

Behind the body, her army stood in its rows. Nobody owned them now.

The entity didn't look down at the snow. She turned back to Victor.

A gun fired from beside the wrecked cruiser.

"Get away from him!" Harlan's commanding shout echoed across the snow. His service pistol kicked in both hands, as he steadily emptied the magazine toward the porcelain figure.

The entity didn't even turn her head. Without taking her eyes off Victor, she raised her left hand and caught the bullets in her porcelain fist.

They snapped against her palm. She opened her fingers, and the flattened slugs dropped onto the frost, steaming in the snow.

Slowly, her head turned toward Harlan.

Harlan's slide locked back on an empty chamber, his tie blowing loose across his vest. 

She took a step toward him.

*No.* Harlan had a wife packing boxes. He had a way out of Oshima.

Victor raised the revolver. The rails woke against his palm.

"Hey! That's far enough, canner!" Victor yelled.

He fired straight into her face.

The crack tore through the open air. She hadn't seen it coming. Half her porcelain face sprayed away in white crystals and bronze light, wrenching her head back around. One black eye sat in a socket of shattered glass, locking onto Victor's face.

She raised her hand, open, the cold rolling off her fingers before she touched him.

She laid her palm flat on Victor's chest.

The cold went in through his coat, and then it was inside him. His heart pushed once against it.

Then the dark entity, with her glowing white eyes, hit her.

Victor didn't see it come. The white hand was on him, and then it was gone, and she went backward across the plaza with the dark figure locked around her. 

His legs gave out. His shoulder hit the plaza floor.

He lay on his side on the frost. His breath went out long and came back shorter.

Someone was shouting his name.

Harlan was running toward him across the frost, his tie flying loose. Harlan was alive.

Harlan's hands grabbed him and pulled him close.

Over Harlan's shoulder, the sun had come up clear of the towers. It was in Victor's eyes.

For the first time, the light didn't hurt.
