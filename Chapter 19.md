The darkness of the service corridor was illuminated by strips of yellow emergency lights running through the middle of the corridor walls.

The counter-weighted vault doors of Deep Storage unlocked with the loud movement of metallic rotary gears. They opened with the grind and squeal of their heavy iron hinges.

Victor raised his revolver, and clicked the button on his tactical flashlight, slicing a beam of light through the silent room.

He stopped just inside. The scale of the room was a strategic nightmare.  Plastic-draped synthetics stretched into long uniform rows. The ticks of their peristaltic pumps randomly sounding off in across the floor. A perfect maze for an ambush. Victor swept his light across the grid, calculating the odds. Null was a hacker, but he didn't know if she was armed, or how many people she had brought with her to pull off a physical breach. 

Harlan was chambering a round in his gun. He raised it and clicked on his own flashlight. His eyes met Victors. They both nodded, as they slowly sidestepped into the vaulted hangar.

Victor's eyes scanned the perimeter walls. At the far end of the grid, his beam caught the edge of an emergency service tunnel, its door propped half open.

Victor signaled to Harlan with a quick upward nod. "Over there," he whispered. "Lock it down."

Harlan broke away, moving quietly toward the far end of the hangar to cut off the emergency exit.
***
A harsh beam of tactical light carved through the dark. 

"The service tunnel is guarded. I'm on the main doors," The voice echoed across the room, cold and brutally pragmatic. "You're out of exits, Null. It's over."

"—Null. it's over," Vigil's synthetic voice whispered between them. The synthetic's head lolled to the side, her voice a hollow, broken harmony mirroring the detective's exact inflection. 

Rin immediately crouched down among the plastic-draped synthetics, pulling Harper and Vigil with her into the shadows. 

Harper gripped Rin's arm in the dark, her voice a tight whisper. "Rin, what is it?"

Rin swallowed hard, staring at the approaching beams of light. "There's something I forgot to tell you about the night I hacked Blackwell's office."

Harper glared at her, unimpressed. "Yeah. And?"

Rin gestured toward the flashlights with her head. "There were these two cops that showed up."

Harper's eyes widened, the light catching a sudden glossiness that wasn't there before. She threw Vigil's synthetic arm over her shoulder to help move it. Bracing for dead weight, it was shockingly light. Beneath the vinyl skin, there was no dense bone mass, only carbon-fiber framing. 

"Okay, okay, listen," Harper whispered frantically, helping Rin drag the wounded Vigil through the maze of plastic-draped bodies. "If we hit the auxiliary lights, we can blind them for two seconds, maybe rush the tunnel—"

"—rush the tunnel," Vigil's broken whisper reflecting back at her. The words fell from the synthetic's lips at the exact same moment Harper spoke them. Not an echo. She was reciting a timeline.

Harper's head spun toward the synthetic, "Hey if you know something—now's the time." Her voice a little more than a whisper.

"No," Rin said, her voice dead flat. 

"Okay, then we use the bodies," Harper rambled, her panic spiking. "We push the racks over, create a barricade—"

"—create a barricade," the broken tones of Vigil's speech hung between them. 

"Harper, stop." Rin halted in the shadows of the dormant army. She looked at the frantic girl, the math already complete in her head. The detective had the main doors. His partner had the tunnel. It was Checkmate. 

"Checkmate." Vigil's head fell forward.

Rin grabbed Harper by the collar of her jacket and pulled her in. She kissed her. It was soft,  frantic, and terrified—a collision of desperation and adrenaline. 

Rin broke away, shoving the weight of the synthetic entirely onto Harper. Without a word, Rin shrugged off her oversized leather jacket and pressed it into Harper's chest. "Stay down. When they move, you run." 

Before Harper could argue, Rin stepped away from the cover of the plastic sheets and into the aisle. 

***

Victor swept his light across the long rows of the dormant army. Hundreds of plastic-draped bodies stood shoulder to shoulder in the dark, tethered to the IV stands. 

His beam washed over the aisle, catching the pale faces of the synthetics obscured by the plastic sheets, their silicone feeding tubes glowing faintly in the glare. The light swept past another figure standing in the line, moved a few feet further—and then Victor's brain registered what he had seen. 

He snapped the beam back. 

Not a synthetic. A girl. She was wearing a black tank top, her arms covered in dark, intricate ink. She stood in a timid, half-bent stance, her hands raised in the air to surrender.

But as the harsh light hit her face, her eyes didn't squint. They were sharp, focused, and defiant. They were the exact same eyes that had stared back at him through the closing gap of the elevator door. 

*Null.* 

Victor didn't lower his revolver. He kept the sight trained directly on her chest. "Harlan!" Victor shouted, his voice echoing in the space. "On me! I got her!"

In the harsh glare of the flashlight, Rin's eyes darted to the side, looking deep into the maze of the hangar. 

Victor saw the wheels turning in her head. There was a slight shift in the metal of his revolver as he firmed his grip. "Hey. Don't do it."

Rin took a slow, calculated step to the right. 

"Don't do it," Victor warned again, framing his aim.

Rin bolted. 

She sprinted hard to the right, cutting deep into the center of the dormant army. Victor followed her with his light, and began the pursuit.

"She's on the move!" Victor roared, his long black coat tailing behind him. She was unarmed, he holstered his revolver, to focus on the chase. He wasn't going to lose her again. "Harlan, cut her off!"

Victor chased the girl as she wove blindly through the maze of plastic-draped bodies, her boots pounding against the concrete.

She slammed into the dormant synthetics as she slipped around each corner. The bodies buckled momentarily before their internal gyroscopes and accelerometers pulled their lifeless bodies unnaturally back into their idle hunched position. 


Victor's flashlight swayed violently across the rows of synthetic bodies. The stretching shadows acted like hundreds of figures sprinting in different directions. It made the zigzag of the chase near impossible, as he tried to keep up with the girl.

He stopped. Deep breaths of cold air stung deep in his lungs. "Shit!" 

Victor raised his flashlight holding the beam steady, freezing the shifting shadows, and surveilled the area. 

There, he saw it. Not the girl, but the buckling of random synthetics as they swayed their posture back to center. 

He was off.

He turned the aisle, his beam cutting through the dark. Suddenly, a figure stumbled out of the shadows, lunging directly at him. 

A synthetic. Pushed towards him. Its dormant equilibrium subroutines fired as the lifeless legs mechanically cycled, stepping forward to catch its balance. In the strobe of the flashlight, it looked exactly like a living person charging him. 

Victor braced himself, grabbing the lunging figure by the shoulders and shoving it hard to the side. The machine's gyroscopes whined, abruptly stabilizing its posture behind him as he pushed past it. 

Before he could raise his light, another plastic-draped figure lurched out of the dark. He caught the hollow carbon frame, wrestling its mechanical momentum aside. Then a third. The girl was violently shoving them from behind into the aisle as she ran, forcing Victor to physically fight his way through a gauntlet of staggering, lifeless synthetics. 

He shoved the last one away, breaking through the barricade. 

Suddenly, a second flashlight beam cut across their path. Harlan stepped out from the line of synthetics directly in front of her.

The girl didn't have time to stop. She slammed hard into Harlan's chest. The impact knocked her stumbling backward onto the concrete. 

Victor closed in from behind, trapping her in the intersection of their blinding lights. 

She was breathing heavily on the floor, as she slowly raised her hands in the air, looking up and out of the corner of her eyes at Victor.

***

The cold steel of the handcuffs ratcheted tight around Rin's wrists, biting into her skin. 

The detective pushed a firm hand into her shoulder, forcing her down to her knees on the hard concrete floor of the hangar. Breathing heavily, and keeping their weapons raised,  they secured the perimeter around her. 

Rin kept her head down, her dark hime-cut bangs falling over her eyes. She glanced to her side, looked back through the uniform rows of the synthetic army. 

Deep in the shadows, unnoticed by the detectives, Rin saw the faint silhouette of Harper. She was kneeling in the dark, clutching Vigil tightly, watching Rin take the fall. 

The service tunnel was wide open. They were safe.
