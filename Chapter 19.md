The darkness of the service corridor was illuminated by strips of yellow emergency lights running through the middle of the corridor walls.

The heavy, counter-weighted vault doors of Deep Storage unlocked with the loud movement of metallic rotary gears. They opened with the grind and squeal of their heavy iron hinges.

Victor raised his revolver, and clicked the button on his tactical flashlight, slicing a beam of light through the silent room.

He stopped just inside. The scale of the room was a strategic nightmare.  Plastic-draped synthetics stretched into long uniform rows. A perfect maze for an ambush. Victor swept his light across the grid, calculating the odds. Null was a hacker, but he didn't know if she was armed, or how many people she had brought with her to pull off a physical breach. 

Harlan was chambering a round in his gun. He raised it and clicked on his own flashlight. His eyes met Victors. They both nodded, as they slowly sidestepped into the cavernous hangar.

Victor's eyes scanned the perimeter walls. At the far end of the grid, his beam caught the edge of an emergency service tunnel, its heavy door propped half open.

Victor signaled to Harlan with a quick upward nod. "Over there," he whispered. "Lock it down."

Harlan broke away, moving quietly toward the far end of the hangar to cut off the emergency exit.
***
A harsh beam of tactical light carved through the dark. 

"The service tunnel is guarded. I'm on the main doors," The voice echoed across the massive room, cold and brutally pragmatic. "You're out of exits, Null. It's over."

"—Null. it's over," Vigil's synthetic voice whispered between them. The synthetic's head lolled to the side, her voice a hollow, broken harmony mirroring the detective's exact inflection. 

Rin immediately crouched down among the plastic-draped synthetics, pulling Harper and Vigil with her into the shadows. 

Harper gripped Rin's arm in the dark, her voice a tight whisper. "Rin, what is it?"

Rin swallowed hard, staring at the approaching beams of light. "There's something I forgot to tell you about the night I hacked Blackwell's office."

Harper glared at her, unimpressed. "Yeah. And?"

Rin gestured toward the flashlights with her head. "There were these two cops that showed up."

Harper's eyes widened, the light catching a sudden glossiness that wasn't there before. She threw Vigil's synthetic arm over her shoulder to help move it. Bracing for dead weight, it was shockingly light. Beneath the vinyl skin, there was no dense bone mass or fluid—hollow carbon-fiber framing. 

"Okay, okay, listen," Harper whispered frantically, helping Rin drag the wounded Vigil through the maze of plastic-draped bodies. "If we hit the auxiliary lights, we can blind them for two seconds, maybe rush the tunnel—"

"—rush the tunnel," Vigil's broken whisper reflecting back at her. The words fell from the synthetic's lips at the exact same millisecond Harper spoke them. Not an echo. She was reciting a timeline.

Harper's head spun toward the synthetic, "Hey if you know something—now's the time." Her voice a little more than a whisper.

"No," Rin said, her voice dead flat. 

"Okay, then we use the bodies," Harper rambled, her panic spiking. "We push the racks over, create a barricade—"

"—create a barricade," the broken tones of Vigil's speech hung between them. 

"Harper, stop." Rin halted in the shadows of the dormant army. She looked at the frantic girl, the math already complete in her head. The detective had the main doors. His partner had the tunnel. It was Checkmate. 

"Checkmate." Vigil's head fell forward.

Rin grabbed Harper by the collar of her jacket and pulled her in. She kissed her. It was soft,  frantic, and terrified—a collision of desperation and adrenaline. 

Rin broke away, shoving the weight of the synthetic entirely onto Harper. "Stay down. When they move, you run." 

Before Harper could argue, Rin stepped away from the cover of the plastic sheets and into the aisle. 

***

Victor swept his light across the endless rows of the dormant army. Hundreds of plastic-draped bodies stood shoulder to shoulder in the dark, tethered to the IV stands and peristaltic pumps. 

His beam washed over the aisle, catching the pale faces of the synthetics obscured by the plastic sheets, their silicone feeding tubes glowing faintly in the glare. The light swept past another figure standing in the line, moved a few feet further—and then Victor's brain registered what he had seen. 

He snapped the beam back. 

Not a synthetic. A girl. She was wearing an oversized leather jacket, standing in a timid, half-bent stance, her hands raised in the air to surrender. 

But as the harsh light hit her face, her eyes didn't squint. They were sharp, focused, and defiant. They were the exact same eyes that had stared back at him through the closing gap of the elevator door. 

*Null.* 

Victor didn't lower his heavy revolver. He kept the sight trained directly on her chest. "Harlan!" Victor shouted. There was a slight shift in the metal of his revolver as he firmed his grip, and framed his aim. "On me! I got her!" 

***

The cold steel of the handcuffs ratcheted tight around Rin's wrists, biting into her skin. 

The detective pushed a heavy hand into her shoulder, forcing her down to her knees on the hard concrete floor of the hangar. His  partner jogged up a second later, breathing heavily, keeping his weapon raised as they secured the perimeter around her. 

Rin kept her head down, her dark hime-cut bangs falling over her eyes. But out of the corner of her eye, she looked back through the uniform rows of the synthetic army. 

Deep in the shadows, unnoticed by the detectives, Rin saw the faint silhouette of Harper. She was kneeling in the dark, clutching Vigil tightly, watching Rin take the fall. 

The service tunnel was wide open. They were safe.
