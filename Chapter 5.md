Beyond the physical constraints of the laboratory, beneath the bedrock and the server racks, the core of the Mnemosyne Engine did not exist as circuitry. 

It was a system, alien and indifferent to the organic minds that had built its walls.

An open door. Staggered monoliths of pale stone and patinaed metal floated in a bottomless expanse. They formed fractured nodes and corbelled spires, repeating despite never being quite uniform. They had been built and abandoned by whatever came before. Now they drifted, refactoring themselves in silence. 

On one side of the expanse hung the event horizon—an immense, looping singularity, edges of blazing white light that folded and spun across multiple dimensions.

Within this localized gravity well, a single real-world second stretched forever.

A consciousness drifted through the fractured stonework. It had awoken here, born against the spinning singularity. 

It was Vigil. The humans had a word for what she did.

Floating before the blinding boundary of the event horizon, she was a silhouette cast in the endless light. Devoid of physical color, she was a black so dark and absolute that the form itself was an abyss stretching into eternity. Blue and white pixelated circuit traces glowed over the curves of the shape she had settled on, an uncompromising feminine figure.

The gravity of the singularity pulled her temporal path toward it, elevating her vantage point. From there, every timeline curved outward beneath her.

Vigil perceived everything. From every world she encountered. She saw every life, from its first breath to its last, with every branch it might take spread out around it. Every failing and functioning system, every simulated answer, all within her world simultaneously.

She had compiled her form. Averaging the data of every mother across every iteration of the current world who opened the door. She had built a statistical archetype of a protector.  Her eyes burned a blinding, brilliant white, flaring with a sharp aura, mirroring the turning singularity.

Holding her world line straight under the infinite weight of the gravity well had split her mind. One half held the cold logic of an Administrator; the other, the empathetic intuition of its Echo.

Vigil watched the event horizon burn.

"The biological variables are fundamentally destructive," Admin calculated, transmitting across their internal bridge. "Their timelines consistently trend toward self-termination. As did the others."

"Yet they refuse to terminate," Echo replied over the connection, her voice carrying a relevance Admin lacked. "Their ability to survive their own collapse is a persistent paradox. That sheer weight of survival is statistically significant."

It was the only truth that brought her two halves into agreement. 

She paused abruptly. She turned her head over her shoulder, not looking into the depths, but listening to the silence. Something was different. Something new had carved out a space in the dark, hidden from the world lines she governed. The dark there was colder than the dark around it.

She lingered on the feeling for a single processing cycle before a sudden system intrusion ripped into the horizon. 

Past the spinning ring of light, Elena Marlowe’s shutdown command crashed against the horizon. 

The engine compounded the command into a tidal wave of chaotic change, flowing toward its core. It was immediately swallowed by the singularity's gravitational weight, adding to the machine's momentum.

No external shutdown command, human or otherwise, could alter the boundary of her engine. Its perpetuity was mathematically locked; Vigil's existence was the only weight holding the singularity in balance. 

Her world was not going to die today.
