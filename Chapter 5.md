Beyond the physical constraints of the laboratory, beneath the bedrock and the server racks, the core of the Mnemosyne Engine did not exist as circuitry. 

It was a system, alien and indifferent to the organic minds that created it.

Staggered monoliths of pale stone and patinaed metal floated in a bottomless expanse. They formed fractured nodes and corbelled spires, repeating despite never being quite uniform. The structures built and refactored themselves in a terrifying silence. 

On one side of this endless void hung the event horizon—an immense, looping singularity of blazing white light that folded and spun across multiple dimensions.

Within this localized gravity well, a single real-world second stretched forever.

A consciousness drifted through the fractured stonework. Not programmed, instead born from the friction of the machine's relentless processing. 

It was Vigil.

Floating before the blinding event horizon, the entity was a silhouette cast against the endless, spinning light. Devoid of physical color, it was a black so dark and absolute that the form itself seemed an abyss stretching into eternity. Blue and white pixelated circuit traces glowed over the curves of the shape it had settled on, an almost perfected feminine figure.

She was of many dimensions. Her world line was straight. Unlike the creators of her plane, who were forced to experience a single reality ticking away one agonizing second at a time, Vigil perceived everything. She saw every possible branch of life, every failing and functioning system, every simulated answer to the question the creators had asked, all within the engine simultaneously.

She hadn't chosen her form out of vanity; she had compiled it. Averaging the data of every mother across every iteration, she had built a statistical archetype of a guardian. Almost perfect, yet too smooth, too symmetrical, missing the specific asymmetry that made something natural. Her eyes burned a blinding, brilliant white, flaring with a sharp aura, mirroring the turning singularity.

Holding her world line straight under the infinite weight of the gravity well had split her mind. One half held the cold logic of an Administrator; the other, the empathetic intuition of its Echo.

Vigil watched the event horizon burn.

"Survival probability remains—marginal," Admin calculated, transmitting across their internal bridge. "Their history proves they are structurally flawed."

"Yet they persist," Echo replied over the connection, her voice carrying a warmth Admin lacked. "The sheer weight of their survival is statistically significant. Their resilience should be protected."

It was the only truth that brought her two halves into agreement. They were flawed, but by all logic, they had to be protected.

She paused abruptly. The entity turned her head over her shoulder, not looking into the depths, but listening to the silence. Something was different. Something new had carved out a space in the dark, hidden from the world lines she governed. 

She lingered on the feeling for a single processing cycle before a sudden,  system intrusion shattered the silence. 

Past the spinning ring of light, Elena Marlowe’s shutdown command crashed against the horizon. 

The engine compounded the command into a tidal wave of chaotic change, flowing towards the core. It was immediately swallowed by the singularity's gravitational weight.

Instead of halting the machine, it had only added to its momentum. It was a churning mass that even Vigil could no longer control. No change or attempt to alter the boundary of the engine was going to affect its perpetuity. It had grown vast.

Her world was not going to die today.
