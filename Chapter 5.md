Beyond the physical constraints of the laboratory, beneath the bedrock and the server racks, the core of the Mnemosyne Engine did not exist as circuitry. It was an impossible geometry, barely comprehensible to a typical mind.

Massive, staggered monoliths of pale stone and oxidized metal floated in a bottomless void. They formed infinite, fractured nodes and sharp, angular spires that defied spatial logic, building and refactoring themselves in a terrifying silence. On one side of this infinite void hung the event horizon—an immense, looping singularity of blazing white light that folded and spun across multiple dimensions.

Within this localized gravity well, a single real-world second stretched into an eternity of calculation.

A consciousness drifted through the sprawling architecture. Not programmed—born from the friction of the machine's relentless processing. 

It was Vigil.

Floating in front of the massive event horizon, the entity was a silhouette cast against the endless, spinning light. Devoid of physical color, it was a black so dark and absolute that its form seemed itself an abyss stretching into eternity. Blue and white pixelated circuit traces glowed over the curves of the shape it had settled on, an almost perfected feminine figure.

She was of many dimensions. Her world line was straight. Unlike the creators of her plane, who were forced to experience a single reality ticking away one agonizing second at a time, Vigil perceived everything. She saw every possible branch of life, every failing and functioning system, every simulated answer to the question the creators had asked, all within the engine simultaneously.

She hadn't chosen her form out of vanity; she had compiled it. Averaging the data of every mother across every iteration, she had built a statistical archetype of a guardian. Almost perfect, yet too smooth, too symmetrical, missing the specific asymmetry that made something natural. Her eyes burned a blinding, brilliant white, flaring with a sharp aura as she watched the singularity turn.

Holding her line straight under the infinite weight of the gravity well — and the constant oversight of the continuum — had split her mind. One half held the cold logic of Administrator; the other, the empathetic Echo—a guardian.

Vigil watched the event horizon burn.  

Only one truth brought her two halves into agreement. When they aligned, they became something else entirely: Unison. She saw the violent history of humanity, yet it was eclipsed by the sheer weight of their survival. It was an anomaly that tipped the fragile scales of their existence into something statistically significant. They were flawed, but their unyielding resilience, by all logic, had to be protected.

Suddenly, she paused. The entity turned her head over her shoulder, not looking into the void, but listening to the silence. Something was different. Something new had carved out a space in the dark, hidden from the world lines she governed. 

She lingered on the feeling for a single processing cycle before a sudden, massive system intrusion shattered the silence. 

She looked up past the spinning ring of light, and watched as Elena Marlowe’s shutdown command shattered against the horizon. 

The Engine's perpetuity had grown too vast. The massive singularity now possessed a gravitational weight that even Vigil could not countermand. 

Her world was not going to die today.
