The blueish tinge blurred and resolved into a sterile ceiling. 

The weightlessness was gone. In its place, a crushing ache radiated through Elena’s ribs. A plastic oxygen mask pressed against her face. The rhythmic pulse of a medical monitor counted the seconds beside her. This was a physical body. This was a hospital. 

Voices pulled her attention to the far side of the room. 

A woman stood in the open doorway, her back to the bed. She wore a white lab coat. Iris. Her messy hair was tied back, and she spoke into the hallway with practiced authority. 

"Negative. Still under," Iris said, her tone clinical. "She suffered significant trauma. We will notify you the moment that changes." 

A modulated voice answered from the corridor. "Understood, Doctor. We will be right outside." 

The door slid shut. The moment the lock clicked, Iris turned around. Looking at the floor, she slumped her shoulders, her posture breaking. She let out a ragged exhale, shoving her black-rimmed glasses up the bridge of her nose.

She looked up and froze when she saw Elena watching her. 

Iris crossed the room in three rapid strides, leaning over the metal bed rail. "Don't—don't talk. Okay? You're—" She shoved her glasses up again. "You're awake. You're actually awake. That's good, that's really good, it's also a problem, but it's good."

Elena tried to speak. The mask trapped the words. She lifted her hand, but her muscles were weak, fighting the lingering atrophy. 

Iris shot a frantic look at the door, her voice dropping to a rapid whisper. "There are two Renewal agents in the hall. I told them you were under. I've been telling them you were under for a week."

Elena’s pulse spiked on the monitor. The steady beep accelerated. 

Iris reached down and tapped the screen, muting the audio warning. "I'm not supposed to be here." She flipped up and inspected the plastic security badge clipped to her white coat. "I stole the badge." She let out a laugh when she said it. 

"The Company—they brought you in as a Jane Doe. To hide you. Because—well, I don't actually know—did you see something? You must have seen something. Was it the shadow process in the engine? Did you see it? I figured it was probably that. Doctor Blackwell was hiding it. That inversion persona from the thesis—" 

A sharp pain swelled in Elena's chest, and tears welled in her eyes as she stared at the ceiling. *Inversion Persona.* A flood of memories—the glassy entity and the fractured laboratory returning in jagged fragments. She caught her breath.

Iris grabbed a clean cloth from the side table and dabbed Elena's eyes clear. Her voice went even lower. "It took me three weeks to find this room, and I've been wearing this coat for seven days waiting for you to open your eyes." She let out a short breath. "No one's been the wiser, actually."

*Thesis? Shadow process?*

"Blackwell is gone," Iris whispered, the words rushing out. Elena's wide eyes found Iris's. "I mean, he's not gone. He sealed himself inside the Mnemosyne lab the night of the breach. Corporate locked it down. No one goes in. No one comes out. At all."

Elena gripped the edge of the oxygen mask with her left hand, pulled the elastic band over her hair, and threw the plastic aside. Ignoring the pain radiating through her chest, she pushed against the mattress to force herself into a sitting position. 

Her right hand didn't feel the sheets. Elena looked down. 

*The Synthetic Sense-Grip VI Neural Articulation.*

She stared at the carbon-fiber chassis. The two-millimeter joint spacing. The neurological integration she had drafted herself. The Company had replaced her hand. Marked her with her own design. 

Elena flexed the synthetic fingers. The hand was shockingly light.

"You shouldn't sit up," Iris blurted, her eyes darting from Elena to the door. Her hands hovered nervously over the bed rail. She looked like she wanted to push Elena back down but didn't dare touch her. "Okay—it's just that... you know, you have two cracked ribs, internal bruising, a dozen micro-fractures, and... well, your hand. But you're up. Okay. Okay, you're up."

Elena swallowed. Her throat burned, dry and raw from weeks of a breathing tube. "The night of the breach," Elena rasped, her voice hollow.

Iris didn't miss a beat. The words spilled out in a frantic torrent. "I tried to come back. I got the detectives into the sublevel. Then I turned around. I ran back for the lab to find you. But I didn't make it. The whole sublevel was coming apart. The physics were wrong. The air tasted like—like metal—you know when you have a sore throat because you've been sick for days and..." Iris trailed off. Elena looked at her. "Right, so I went back to the lab but the structural supports were bending inward. Then the Company’s Liability Teams showed up, with armed Renewal agents. They were sealing the floors, burning the servers. And the night shift—" She stopped. "Anyone from the sub labs. They put them on their knees in the corridor, Elena. Zip ties. Hoods. They walked them into the freight lift." Her fingers tightened on the rail. "Nobody from the night shift came back to work. I had to turn back. I had to hide—so I hid."

Iris gripped the metal rail, leaning closer. Her eyes searched Elena's face. 

"I never saw it, Elena. I never made it to the doors. I don't know what you saw. But you were in the room. That's why the Company has you hidden in here. They are terrified of what you know. But you're too important to the program to let go." Iris shoved her glasses up her nose again. "What happened in there? What did you see?"

Elena closed her eyes. The memory of the glassy entity hovering above the floor grates flashed in her mind. The cold touch of its finger against her face.

"Parity," Elena said, the word scraping against her ruined throat. "He built a monster."
