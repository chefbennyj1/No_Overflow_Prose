The blueish tinge blurred and resolved into a sterile ceiling. 

The weightlessness was gone. In its place, a crushing ache radiated through Elena’s ribs. A heavy, negative-pressure respirator was strapped over her face. The rhythmic pulse of a medical monitor counted the seconds beside her. This was a physical body. This was a hospital.

Voices pulled her attention to the far side of the room. 

A figure stood in the open doorway, their back to the bed. They were encased in a yellow bio-containment suit. Their face obscured by encapsulating soft hood. An coiled orange air tube extended from the suit to a connection on the ceiling. The figure spoke into the hallway with practiced authority. 

"Negative. Still under," the muffled voice said, sounding clinical. "The biological exposure protocols remain active. We will notify you the moment her status changes."

A modulated voice answered from the corridor. "Understood, Doctor. We will maintain the seal." 

The heavy, airtight door slid shut. The moment the lock hissed into place, the figure turned around. Looking at the floor, they slumped their shoulders.

A cold spike of adrenaline hit Elena's chest. 

The figure reached up to the neck seal, unzipped the heavy collar, and pulled the encapsulating hood back over her shoulders to reveal a messy bun and black-rimmed glasses. 

Iris. 

She let out a ragged exhale, shoving her glasses up the bridge of her nose. She looked up and froze when she saw Elena watching her. 

Iris crossed the room in three rapid strides, leaning over the metal bed rail. "Don't—don't talk. Okay? You're—" She shoved her glasses up again. "You're awake. You're actually awake. That's good, that's really good, it's also a problem, but it's good."

Elena tried to speak. The mask trapped the words. She lifted her hand, but her muscles were weak, fighting the lingering atrophy. 

Iris shot a frantic look at the door, her voice dropping to a rapid whisper. "There are two Renewal agents in the hall. I told them you were under. I've been telling them you were under for a week."

Elena’s pulse spiked on the monitor. The steady beep accelerated. 

Iris reached down and tapped the screen, muting the audio warning. "I'm not supposed to be here." She checked the intravenous bags by the bed.

"The Company—they brought you in as a Jane Doe. You're in bio-containment... Oh... Actually you can probably see that. They are hiding you, Elena. Because—well, I don't actually know... did you see something? You—You must have seen something." Iris' eyes widened she looked at Elena. "Was it the shadow process in the engine?" 

*Shadow process?*

Iris shot a nervous glance toward the door. "We have to keep quiet—those agents—I definitely do not like them." She turned back to the monitors beside Elena's bed. "I figured you caught the shadow process. Doctor Blackwell was hiding it. That inversion persona from the thesis—" 
A sharp pain swelled in Elena's chest. Tears welled in her eyes as she stared at the ceiling. A flood of memories—the glassy entity and the fractured laboratory returning in jagged fragments. She caught her breath.

Iris muted the pulse monitor alarm, again. She grabbed a clean cloth from the side table and dabbed Elena's eyes clear. Her voice dropped even lower. "Okay, okay. You're upset. The inversion persona. I shouldn't have brought it up.  Just... just take it easy." 

Iris forced a lighter tone, tapping the plastic security badge clipped to the hip of her yellow containment suit. "I stole the badge." She let out a short nervous laugh, pushed her glasses up, and looked at Elena out of the corner of her eye. For Elena, it felt good to smile, but pain flared in her chest as she tried to join the laugh.

"Because you were in direct contact with the entity, the Company classified you as an unknown pathogen risk," Iris explained, gesturing to the heavy yellow fabric. "It took me three weeks to find this Isolation Room, and I've been sweating in this suit for seven days waiting for you to open your eyes." She let out a short breath. "No one's been the wiser, actually."



"Blackwell is gone," Iris whispered, the words rushing out. Elena's wide eyes found Iris's. "I mean, he's not *gone* gone. He sealed himself inside the Mnemosyne lab the night of the breach. Corporate locked it down. No one goes in. No one comes out. At all."

Elena gripped the edge of the oxygen mask with her left hand, pulled the elastic band over her hair, and threw the plastic aside. Ignoring the pain radiating through her chest, she pushed against the mattress to force herself into a sitting position. 

Her right hand didn't feel the sheets. Elena looked down. 

*The Synthetic Sense-Grip VI Neural Articulation.*

She stared at the carbon-fiber. The two-millimeter joint spacing. The neurological integration she had drafted herself. The Company had replaced her hand. Marked her with her own design. 

Elena flexed the synthetic fingers. The hand was shockingly light.

"You shouldn't sit up," Iris blurted, her eyes darting to the door. Her hands hovered nervously over the bed rail. She looked like she wanted to push Elena back down but didn't dare touch her. "Okay—it's just that... you know, you have two cracked ribs, internal bruising, a dozen micro-fractures, and... well, your hand. But you're up. Okay. Okay, you're up."

Elena swallowed. Her throat burned, dry and raw from weeks of a breathing tube. "The night of the breach," Elena rasped, her voice hollow.

Iris didn't miss a beat. The words spilled out in a frantic torrent. "I got two detectives into the sublevel. Then I turned around. I ran back to the lab to find you, but I didn't make it."

"Detectives?" Elena's word scraped against her ruined throat. 

"From the lower grid. I don't know what happened to them. The whole sublevel was coming apart." Iris's voice dropped, her fingers tightening on the bed rail. "Then the Company’s Liability Teams showed up with armed Renewal agents. They were sealing the floors, burning the servers. And the night shift—" 

She stopped, her eyes wide with the memory. "Anyone from the sub-labs. They put them on their knees in the corridor, Elena. Tied them up with hoods over their heads and walked them into the freight lift. Nobody from the night shift came back. I had to hide."

Iris searched Elena's face. "I don't know what you saw in that room. That's why the Company has you hidden in here. They are terrified of what you know, but you're too important to the program to dispose of." 

Elena closed her eyes. The memory of the glassy entity hovering above the floor grates flashed in her mind. Its cold fingers against her face. 

She opened her eyes and looked down at her new synthetic hand. 

"Parity," Elena rasped. "He built a monster."
