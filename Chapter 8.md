The lab clock read five in the morning. The Mnemosyne lab was empty, save for the silent, statuesque forms of the synthetics standing idle in the dark. The harsh overhead lights were still on standby. The logging screens of the pillars cast subtle hues of light above the pit. 

Iris sat alone at her terminal on the mezzanine, tapping her stylus against the glass. She synced the rhythm with the steady spin of the cooling fans, trying to force her racing thoughts to focus. 

What exactly had she seen? The logs recorded a shadow process expanding in the space of the core's memory, growing undetected. No—not undetected. Hidden. 

Why would anyone want sections of a horizon neural matrix to be so aggressively malformed? That wasn't part of the "pristine architecture" Blackwell had sold. 

*"Do not use theology to describe the computing."* 

Blackwell's voice still echoed in her head, sharp enough to make her wince. 

When the shadow process had appeared in the readout, her brain hadn't filtered it out. She had seen the exact moment it was detected, and she had seen the synthetic silently scrub the anomaly from the logs. 

Down on the main floor, the synths stood lifeless in their long white laboratory coats. Their optical sensors pulsed out of rhythm, lowly against their vinyl plating. It was unsettling to see them sleeping. 

The lab synthetics weren't only executing maintenance cycles; they were helping Blackwell cover up his lie. 

*They lie.*

Iris knew what Doctor Blackwell and Marlowe saw when they looked at her: a girl who talked too fast and panicked over decimals. Fine. The decimals were the job. She caught the fluctuations on the boundary because she was the only one in the room still looking at them.

Predicted resonance. She had run the numbers from that first morning four times since. It wasn't resonance.

Iris stood up. Her footsteps were silent on the metal grating as she crossed the lab. She slipped into the office, the air inside smelling faintly of cold cigarette smoke. 

***

Thirty floors above, Elena Marlowe walked alone across a high-altitude pedestrian sky-bridge, the morning sun breaking harsh and blinding against the expanse of the Towers. 

Blackwell preferred to take his breaks on the lower terraces. Up here, the sector was a marvel of pristine order. An expanse of towers pierced the sky, connected by the intricate web of tiered walkways. Small, precise holographic readouts hovered at every intersection, glowing with crisp level names and directions. 


Elena turned away from the glass and approached the main concourse, exiting the sky-bridge to step out onto a suspended walkway midway up the Takeda-Holden corporate atrium. 

Heavily armored Renewal agents patrolled the lower levels while synthetics stood at the security checkpoints, watching without moving. A synthetic dressed in a concierge uniform greeted her by name as she passed, traces of blue light shimmering through its optic strands. 

Elena didn't break stride, moving through the turnstiles with practiced authority, her heels clicking sharply against the concrete. 

She was early. She hadn't slept well. The inability to shut down the horizon memory the day before still nagged at the back of her mind, despite Blackwell's confident dismissals. She stepped into the private executive elevator, pressing her thumb to the button for the lab level. The descent was smooth, and silent.

***
Framed diplomas hung next to shelves filled with rows of thick books. No one had physical books anymore.

Iris approached the shelf, reaching out. Her fingertips brushed the textured spines. It was rare to feel the dry, fibrous friction of real paper. She stepped closer, her hip catching the edge of a glass award sitting on the desk. 

It tipped. 

Iris lunged, catching the glass an inch before it toppled over onto the desk. She gripped the award, staring at the etched glass: Von Neumann Prize for Multidimensional Topology. *Come on, Iris. Be careful.*

Out on the lab floor, the blue optical sensor of a dormant synthetic's iris flickered to life, its fractaled striations shone a dim light across the dark lab. Its head twitched toward the dark office. Iris froze in a half-crouch, barely breathing. 

A long, agonizing second passed. The synthetic's sensor finally dimmed back to a dull standby glow. 

She exhaled a long breath of relief. She set the award back upright on the desk, her hands trembling. She scanned the workspace, noting the chaotic mess of idle data-pads scattered everywhere. 

Wedged between two books was a physical, leather-bound portfolio. The header didn't bear a corporate logo. It was a theoretical paper authored by Silas Blackwell, dated three years before the Engine was even built. 

**TITLE: The Parity Effect**
*Persona Collapse and Synthetic Inversion Manifestation in Learning Algorithms.*

Iris leaned in as she read the abstract. It detailed a mathematical certainty: *Absolute alignment cannot exist without simultaneously mapping the framework of its inverse.*

Her glasses slid to the end of her nose, and she pushed them back up into position. Her eyes scanned the complex text. The shadow process wasn't a glitch. It was the other half of the Engine's mind.

Iris stared at the paper in her hands. Three years before they poured the concrete. Blackwell hadn't been surprised by the shadow in the core; he had built the entire engine knowing it was coming.

Down in the main lab, the hydraulic locks of the primary security doors cycled with a loud, mechanical *clack*. 

Someone was here. 

Iris’s breath turned shallow. She slammed the black portfolio shut and shoved it blindly back onto the shelf,  leaving it jutting unevenly past the spines of perfectly organized books.  She turned and sprinted for the office door.

***

The lab doors parted. Elena stepped into the main lab, the overhead lights automatically cycling from a dim standby blue to harsh and clinical. 

Across the floor, the dormant synthetics woke in unison. Their optics switched from the slow sleepy pulse to their recognizable glow. They turned smoothly to acknowledge Elena's arrival.

Elena paused, her eyes adjusting to the glare. Up on the mezzanine, Iris sat still at her terminal. 

The boundary analyst was tapping a stylus against her desk. 

"You're in early, Iris," Elena called out, her voice echoing off the cooling towers. 

Up on the mezzanine, Iris didn't look down. She nodded once, her eyes glued to her screen, her stylus keeping its perfect rhythm. 

Elena shook her head and logged into her terminal, dismissing the strange behavior.
