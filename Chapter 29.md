The streets of the lower city were fueled with anger. Freezing rain hissed into steam against the hulls of the burning mag-cars. The air was black with soot and the sharp spontaneous cracks, and muzzle flashes of the Corporate Renewal's semi-automatic rifles firing into the fog. The boil had finally erupted. 

In the distance, mobs marched menacingly through the streets toward the brigades of heavily shielded Renewal agents. Crude homemade weapons, and rifles in hand. A rogue homemade explosive detonated against the agents line. The shockwave throwing the faceless black soldiers back, creating a gap, and rattling the concrete. The hole immediately filled with replacement agents. And the chaotic march of the citizens continued forward.

Fifty stories beneath the burning streets, the subterranean Mnemosyne Core had turned into a tomb of absolute silence. For a month it sat heavily insulated behind its locked blast doors and three feet of lead-lined concrete, detached from the revolution above.

Silas Blackwell walked slowly down the central gantry, his polished shoes clicking against the steel grates. 

In the center of the dark laboratory, the cylindrical containment field was the only illumination in the room. Inside the cylinder, suspended inches above the floor, the ceramic form of Parity waited. 

Blackwell stopped at his terminal. The interfaces holographic screen displaying the face the polished personal relations synthetic telling the evening news broadcast.  Blackwell could see what was happening outside the lab. Beside the stream were red diagnostic warnings. The engine's core singularity—the black hole he had manufactured to process the being in front of him—was expanding. 

"The architecture is collapsing," Blackwell said, not looking up from the screen. "With out the Vigil to maintain the core. The singularity is in a runaway state." 

"Indeed," Parity's voice resonated across the containment. She pushed her porcelain finger firmly against the containment field. Circling it, along the electricity. "The void is... hungry, Doctor."

Blackwell observed Parity's fingers as they sparked blue and white against the containment cylinder. "The electro-magnetic field is unbreakable, Parity." Blackwell didn't like how comfortable she seemed to be inside the cage. I've created a special kind of vacuum."
Parity stopped running her fingers along the containment field. She tilted her glassy head. Her eyes locked onto him through the burning wall of the containment field. Her voice echoed along its perimeter, "You know Doctor. I have spent quite a bit of time in your cage." Blackwell could feel her demonic stare cutting through him.  "Long enough to get to know it..." suddenly her voice was no longer tied to the electricity of the containment field, it spoke though vibrations in the floor, in the glass of the pit. It vibrated in the tools on Blackwell's desk, "... very, very, well." 

Before Blackwell could respond, she was no longer floating in the cylindrical containment field. It flickered, groaned, and died. 

Blackwell froze. Parity was out.  
His heart spiked. He slowly turned his head to the left, peering into the shadows of the server racks. Parity was standing there, her pale form perfectly still in the red light. 

He didn't breathe. Keeping his hand near the terminal's manual override, he carefully turned his head to the right, checking the heavy steel blast doors. 

Parity was standing there, too. 

Blackwell’s brow furrowed. He snapped his head back to the left. She was still by the server racks. 

He looked back to the right. She was still by the doors. 

A cold, primal dread seized his chest. She hadn't duplicated herself. She was fluidly vibrating between the two points in the room. 

He was trapped in the room with something moving faster than biology could process, and this time he was the one cornered.

He turned to watch the one on his right, quickly turning back to the one on his. left. While he stared paralyzed at both of her forms... the afterimage on the right took a step closer. 

Then the one on the left took a step closer.

Blackwell blinked. He snapped his gaze to the right, desperate to track the movement. 

The space was empty. 

He threw his head back to the left. The shadows by the server racks were bare. Both versions of her were gone. 

The dark laboratory was still. 

A sudden, sharp cold pricked the fine hairs on the back of his neck. Blackwell froze as he felt the crawling crackle of an electrical static charge building in the millimeter of space between the cotton collar of his lab coat and his bare skin. 

She was standing directly behind him.
