Victor walked down the faded seafoam colored hallway of the long-term care facility. The blue and yellow checkered linoleum stuck to his boots. The fluorescent lights overhead died with an electrical buzz, then flickered back to life. 

He stopped at the front desk, dragging the chained stylus across the visitor log. He looked up at the nurse behind the desk. "Victor Graves, for Arthur Graves." 

The nurse turned her screen toward him before he finished signing. The balance had a third number under it now. Victor stared at the screen, he let out a slow breath. He let the stylus drop, the metal chain rattling against the counter, and walked away.

Harlan walked beside him until they reached the green wooden door. He stopped in the hallway, and leaned back against the wall, beside the the cheap vase holding the arrangement of fake blue flowers.

Victor adjusted the collar of his trench coat and pushed the door open. 

Arthur Graves sat in the faded, patched armchair. He didn't look at him. 

Victor pulled a plastic chair across the room and sat down directly in front of the glass, blocking the old man's view. He leaned forward, resting his elbows on his knees. He didn't have time for the ritual today. "Dad. The Madame. If she wanted to get into a locked-down Company facility using custodial access... how would she do it?" 

"Close it," Arthur whispered, his voice dry and trembling. His fingers gripped the armrests. "You gotta keep an eye—" He pointed his wrinkled, tremoring finger toward the window. "There is an expectation here. The Madame don't just trust anyone, ya know." 



Arthur stopped pointing. His hand dropped slowly back to the armrest. He stared at Victor, his clouded eyes squinting as a memory broke through the haze. 

"Janitors?" Arthur muttered, a rough, humorless sound rattling in his chest. "Altered services credentials. We're doin' that one are we?"

*Altered service credentials?* Victor thought back to the Deep Storage hangar. He saw the long, uniform rows of plastic-draped bodies in the dark. *She's going for the synths.* 

Before Victor could speak, Arthur's head snapped toward him. The brief clarity in the old man's eyes vanished, replaced by a sudden, fierce suspicion.

"You," Arthur spat. "You smell like a cop."

Victor closed his eyes. The ledger had wiped clean again. "No, Dad. Not today." 

"Then you should be watchin' the window," Arthur whispered, his voice trembling as his fingers dug back into the corduroy. "You want to get us strung up?"

Two sharp raps on the wooden doorframe broke the tension. Harlan stood in the doorway, holding his police radio. "Vic. Dispatch just called a Code Orange at the Takeda-Holden sub-levels."

Victor stood up instantly. He didn't look back at the old man in the chair. He pushed the plastic chair away and walked out into the hall. He had lasted exactly two minutes.
