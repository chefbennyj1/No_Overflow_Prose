Victor walked down the faded seafoam colored hallway of the long-term care facility. The blue and yellow checkered linoleum stuck to his boots. The fluorescent lights overhead died with an electrical buzz, then flickered back to life. 

He stopped at the front desk, dragging the chained stylus across the visitor log. He looked up at the nurse behind the desk. "Victor Graves, for Arthur Graves." 

The nurse turned her screen toward him before he finished signing. The balance had a third number under it now. Victor stared at the screen. He let out a slow breath, dropped the stylus against the counter, and walked toward his father's room.

Harlan walked beside him until they reached the green wooden door. He stopped in the hallway, and leaned back against the wall, beside the cheap vase holding the arrangement of fake blue flowers.

Victor adjusted the collar of his trench coat and pushed the door open. 

Arthur Graves sat in his faded, patched armchair. He didn't look up as Victor entered. 

Victor pulled a plastic chair across the room and sat down directly in front of the window, blocking the old man's view. He leaned forward, resting his elbows on his knees. He didn't have time for the ritual today. "Dad. The Madame. What would she want with Takeda-Holden janitorial access?" 

"Close it," Arthur whispered, his voice dry and trembling. His fingers gripped the armrests. "You gotta keep an eye—" He pointed his wrinkled, tremoring finger toward the window. "There is an expectation here. The Madame don't just trust anyone, ya know." 

Arthur stopped pointing. His hand dropped slowly back to the armrest. He stared at Victor, his clouded eyes squinting as a memory broke through the haze. 

"Twenty questions..." Arthur spat. "Janitors?" Victor could see focus appear in the old man's eyes. "Altered service credentials. Janitors gotta get in to clean ya know? They give the job to synths because they only care about the job. They don't care what they see."

*Synths?* Victor thought back to the Deep Storage hangar. He saw the long, uniform rows of plastic-draped bodies in the dark. *She's going for the synths... An entire army of synths.* 

Before Victor could say a word, Arthur's head snapped toward him. The brief clarity in the old man's eyes vanished, replaced by a sudden recognition.

"Victor," Arthur whispered, his voice trembling as his fingers dug back into the corduroy. "You gotta watch that window."

Two sharp raps on the wooden doorframe broke the tension. Harlan stood in the doorway, holding his police radio. "Vic. Dispatch just called. They want everyone back at the precinct. The whole city's gone crazy."

Victor stood up instantly. He didn't look back at the old man in the chair. He pushed the plastic chair away and walked out into the hall.
