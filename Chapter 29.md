The streets of the lower city were fueled. Freezing rain hissed into steam against the hulls of the burning mag-cars. The air was black with soot and the sharp spontaneous cracks, and muzzle flashes of the Corporate Renewal's semi-automatic rifles firing into the fog. The boil had finally erupted. 

In the distance, mobs demonstrated menacingly through the streets toward the brigades of heavily shielded Renewal agents. Crude homemade weapons, and rifles in hand. A rogue homemade explosive detonated against the agents line. The shockwave throwing the faceless black soldiers back, creating a gap, and rattling the concrete. The hole immediately filled with replacement agents. And the chaotic march of the citizens continued forward.

Fifty stories beneath the burning streets, the subterranean Mnemosyne Core had turned into a tomb of absolute silence. For a month it sat heavily insulated behind its locked blast doors and three feet of lead-lined concrete, detached from the revolution above.

Silas Blackwell walked slowly down the central gantry, his polished shoes clicking against the steel grates. 

In the center of the dark laboratory, the cylindrical containment field burned with a harsh, blinding light. It was the only illumination in the room. Inside the cylinder, suspended inches above the floor, the pale, glassy form of Parity waited. 

Blackwell stopped at his terminal. The glass interface was bleeding with red diagnostic warnings. The engine's core singularity—the black hole he had manufactured to process the dead circuits—was expanding. The mathematical boundaries were failing. 

"The architecture is collapsing," Blackwell said, not looking up from the screen. "You are pulling too much power from the grid."

"I am pulling nothing," Parity's voice resonated through the dark room, vibrating off the steel walls without a source. "The void is simply hungry, Doctor Blackwell."

Blackwell tapped a sequence of commands into the terminal. He was calm, calculating. "I built this cage to contain you indefinitely. If you do not stabilize the core, I will initialize the purge protocol."

Parity tilted her glassy head. Her eyes locked onto him through the burning wall of the containment field. 

"There is ambiguity in your power grid, Silas."

Before Blackwell could respond, the heavy steel floor beneath him violently shuddered. A concussive boom from the surface riots had hit the primary substations. 

The laboratory plunged into darkness. 

The cylindrical containment field flickered, groaned, and died. 

Blackwell froze. The emergency backup lights snapped on, painting the laboratory in a dim, blood-red glow. 

He looked at the center of the room. The containment field was empty. 

Blackwell’s heart spiked. He slowly turned his head to the left, peering into the shadows of the server racks. Parity was standing there, her pale form perfectly still in the red light. 

He didn't breathe. Keeping his hand near the terminal's manual override, he carefully turned his head to the right, checking the heavy steel blast doors. 

Parity was standing there, too. 

Blackwell’s brow furrowed. He snapped his head back to the left. She was still by the server racks. 

He looked back to the right. She was still by the doors. 

A cold, primal dread seized his chest. She hadn't duplicated herself. She was moving. She was vibrating between the two points in the room so rapidly, so fluidly, that his human retinas were retaining the afterimage of both locations at the exact same time. 

He was trapped in the room with something moving faster than biology could process. 

And then, while he stared paralyzed at both of her forms... the afterimage on the right took a step closer. 

Then the one on the left took a step closer.
