The night market, in her part of Oshima, was a claustrophobic canyon of commerce. Signs, half-dead and bleeding colors into the fog. Rin hauled her boots along the broken sidewalk, the collar of her leather jacket pulled up against the night air. She shoved her way through the crowd in the storm, avoiding the sweeping gaze of the dirty corporate security cameras mounted on the street lamps. 

Meeting the detective eye to eye was a mistake she kept replaying in her mind. *How long have they known my alias?* she wondered. *How long have they been chasing Null?* The work for the Filament had made her known. She wiped a streak of freezing rain from her cheek. *Always tastes like there's dirt in my mouth,* she thought, feeling the weight of the Dead Circuits again.

Veering away from the main thoroughfare, Rin stepped through piles of garbage and broken glass. She ducked under the dripping, corrugated awning of a derelict electronics shop to escape the downpour. The storefront was barred, protecting a graveyard of obsolete technology. Behind the glass, a dozen vintage, bulky television sets were stacked haphazardly atop one another. They were all tuned to the same channel, flickering light on the slick street. On every screen, the identical, impassive face of a corporate anchorwoman delivered the evening broadcast. 

"—officials continue to investigate an early morning data breach at a Takeda-Holden office building downtown," the anchorwoman’s voice buzzed through a blown-out speaker above the door. "Renewal Agents are sweeping parts of the lower city, in order to apprehend the suspect responsible—"

Rin tuned it out, as she pulled the red drive from her pocket. A muffled siren wailed deep in the city blocks behind her.  She turned the metal casing over, her thumb tracing the edges. *Renewal wouldn't care about stolen electrical keys.* She paused. *They're looking for this.* Pocketing the drive she stepped back out from under the awning. 

Degraded vehicles suspended inches above the broken pavement on magnetic cushions glided passed slowly along the wet road. They vibrated loudly, pushing the flooding rainwater away in a continuous ripple. Rin crossed the road between the passing mag-cars. 

As she hit the adjacent curb she looked up, in the darkened glass of a storefront, the electronics shop flickered behind her. The dozen vintage screens spasmed in unison, and shut off. 

The streetlights died. People shouted out into the sudden powerless intersections, as the lights of the passing mag-cars painted long shadows of the pedestrians on the broken brick.

Rin turned to take in the blackout, as the power surged back into the grid. The corporate anchorwoman reappeared on the screens, droning on about curfews and unauthorized civilian gatherings in the east end of the lower city. 

Rin stared at the televisions for a long moment. The freezing rain soaked past her jacket and the cloth of her black tank, chilling to her skin.  She ran her fingers along the casing of the red drive again, in her pocket.
Unable to shake the creeping sensation of the blackout, Rin pulled her collar up higher and vanished into the fog.

Three blocks deep into the slums, an angled luxury cruiser idled in the rain, out of place. It was a low-slung wedge of glossy black panels and tinted glass that reflected the city's lights like a dark mirror. Its magnetic cushion was a flawless, silent vibration.

The storm had begun a downpour. Rin stopped where the street opened out, her hand drawn to the pistol under her jacket. The cruiser sat low on its cushion, its taillights scattering a red glow on the flooded pavement. A man stood at the curb beneath a black umbrella, wearing a long black coat over a suit. His polished loafers—a dead giveaway—were getting ruined by the grime. He was a Minder. Hired security for important people. Someone significant was in the vehicle.

He nonchalantly watched the car, though his gaze kept shifting to the street and back. The Minder hauled on his cigarette as the headlights of a passing mag-car caught the bulge of the gun under his coat. She didn't need to look for the second one. He was across the road in the doorway of a gutted storefront, his grimacing face already watching her from under his umbrella. He gave a slow nod, signaling her to proceed, before stepping back against the storefront. 

Rain beat down, flooding the street in front of her. The rear passenger window of the black cruiser glided open, venting gray smoke in to the atmosphere.

Lifting her leather jacket above her head to shield her from the down pour, Rin crossed.

A man leaned forward from the plush darkness, his broad shoulders blocking the rear-facing seat and most of the interior. His suit was sharp, hair cut short, a wrinkled face held one brown eye, the other blue. "I see you made it out alive," he said, tapping an unlit cigarette against the package he drew it from. 

Rin kept her expression neutral and stepped forward. Holding her jacket with one hand over her head, she reached into the back pocket of her jeans and pulled out a generic black thumb drive. She tossed it through the open window. It landed on the plush seat. 

The man sat back in his seat. Across from him sat a skeletal woman beneath an impeccably tailored white silk dress. Dark wire-rimmed glasses. She didn't speak. She drew on a cigarette fixed to a long filter and let the smoke go. The diamonds on her hands caught what light there was. Nothing about her belonged here, yet 'here' belonged to her.  

"Custodial authorizations, just like we agreed," Rin said, her voice loud over the falling rain. Rin looked at the woman in the car. Rin knew exactly who she was, and the fact that the Madame was there at all meant she was no longer overlooked by the Filament.  Rin tried to hide the nerves in her voice.

The man snatched the drive up. Without looking at Rin, he turned and presented it to the old woman. 

The Madame took it and turned it over, a grim smile pulling at the deep lines around her mouth. 

"It seems you have a small issue of the Renewal following you down here, Erin Adler," the old woman's voice was low and gritty.  Hearing the  Madame of the Filament speaking her full name didn't sit well with Rin.

"You should be more careful when you bring heat into my house." The woman paused, looking over the top of her glasses. "But, because you have been of service to us, if the Filament can be of assistance to you—" she took a long drag off her cigarette, the smoke spilling out of her mouth and nostrils as she spoke, "—we will."

"Yes, ma'am," Rin said, acknowledging an offer she had no intention of accepting.
