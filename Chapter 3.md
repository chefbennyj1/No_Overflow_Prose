The night market of her sector was a claustrophobic canyon of commerce. Signs, half-dead and bleeding harsh pink and green into the fog. Rin scuffed her boots along the broken sidewalk. The smell of boiling noodles drifted from street carts as she shoved her way through the crowd in the storm.

Rin walked with her head down, the collar of her heavy leather jacket pulled up against the night air. She had kept a low profile all day. Meeting the detective eye to eye was a mistake she kept replaying in her mind. *How long have they known my alias?* she wondered. *How long have they been chasing Null? Was I really that careless?* She moved with purpose, wiping a streak of freezing rain from her cheek. *Always tastes like there's dirt in my mouth,* she thought, feeling the weight of the Dead Circuits again.

Veering away from the main thoroughfare, Rin stepped under the dripping, corrugated awning of a derelict electronics shop to escape the downpour. The storefront was heavily barred, an iron cage protecting a graveyard of obsolete technology. Behind the rain-streaked glass, a dozen vintage, bulky television sets were stacked haphazardly atop one another. They were all tuned to the same channel, illuminating the slick street a flickering, sickly blue. On every screen, the identical, impassive face of a corporate anchorwoman delivered the evening broadcast. 

"—officials continue investigating an early morning unauthorized data breach at the Takeda-Holden data hub," the anchorwoman’s modulated voice buzzed through a blown-out speaker above the door. "Renewal Agents are conducting authorized sweeps through several lower-city districts —"

Rin tuned it out, as she pulled the red drive from her pocket. She turned it over, her thumb tracing the metal casing. *They wouldn't care about stolen routing keys.* She paused. *They're looking for this.* She pocketed it and stepped back out from under the awning.

In the darkened glass of an adjacent storefront, the electronics shop flickered behind her. The dozen vintage screens spasmed in unison. 

The anchorwoman's face fractured into heavy scanlines. Beneath it, in the static — hidden amongst  the  ancient noise — a new visage formed. A face devoid of color. An absolute black. Pixelated circuit lines glowed, tracing out the shape of a face. Eyes burned a blinding, brilliant white, flaring with a blue aura along their lensed edges. She was breathtaking. She was terrifying.

Its expression remained still, hauntingly focused. The glowing white eyes in the reflection were tracking Rin's every step.

Rin stopped cold, and whipped around to face the electronics shop. 

But the screens were normal. The corporate anchorwoman droned on about the data breach, entirely undisturbed. 

Rin stared at the televisions for a long moment, the freezing rain soaking into her collar. Her pulse hammered in her ears. She let out a shaky breath.
*It's just the exhaustion playing tricks,*  she told herself, though she didn't quite believe it. 

Unable to shake the creeping sensation that she was still being watched, Rin pulled her collar up higher and vanished into the fog.

***

Three blocks deep into the slums, a massive, geometric luxury cruiser idled silently, out of place, in the rain. It was a low-slung wedge of glossy black panels and tinted glass that reflected the city's lights like a dark mirror.  The heavy chassis was suspended inches above the broken pavement on a magnetic cushion. It vibrated with a low frequency that pushed the flooding rainwater away from its undercarriage in a continuous ripple.

Rin stopped where the street opened out, her hand going to the pistol under her jacket. The cruiser sat low on its cushion, taillights bleeding red across the flooded pavement. A man stood at the curb beneath a black umbrella, pea coat over a suit, polished loafers ruined by the grime.  He watched the car, then the street, then the car again. The glow of his cigarette caught the bulge of the gun under his coat. She didn't need to look for the second one. He was across the road in the doorway of a gutted storefront, already looking at her. Had been, probably, since the corner.

Across the flooded road, the rear passenger window glided down with a quiet hiss, venting gray smoke into the freezing rain.

Rin crossed.

A man leaned forward from the plush darkness, broad shoulders blocking the rear-facing seat, and most of the interior. His suit was sharp. Everything else about him wasn't. One eye brown, the other blue, shifting to the street, to the rain, and back —  as though he'd learned not to look at anything directly.

Across from him sat a woman almost skeletal beneath an impeccably tailored silk dress. Dark wire-rimmed glasses. She didn't speak. She drew on a cigarette fixed to a long filter and let the smoke go, and the diamonds on her hands caught what light there was. Nothing about her belonged here, yet 'here' seemed to belong to her.  

"I see you made it out alive," the man said, his voice flat. 

*The Filament,* Rin thought, keeping her expression neutral. *Not a group I should underestimate. But they hate the Corporation too.*

Rin stepped up to the window and reached into her jacket, bypassing the red drive and pulling out a secondary, generic black thumb drive. She tossed it through the open window. It landed on the plush seat. 

"Municipal routing keys, just like we agreed," Rin said. "You should have complete control over the sector's grid by midnight."

The man snatched the drive up. Without looking at Rin, he turned and presented it to the old woman. 

Madame Filament took it with her skeletal, ring-adorned hand. She turned it over, a grim smile pulling at the deep lines around her mouth. 

"It seems you have a small issue of the Renewal following you down here, Rin," the old woman rasped, exhaling a thin stream of smoke. "When I control the routing, the district stops paying Takeda-Holden for the privilege of freezing in the dark."

"I hope so," Rin said aloud.

***

Living in the Dead Circuits rarely had its benefits, but as Rin approached her apartment building, a light flickered over the doorway. *There's power tonight,* she thought. 

She pushed through the heavy front doors into the lower lobby. The air inside smelled stale. Chipped green paint curled off the walls, and the corners choked with shadows and dirt. She walked down the first-floor hallway, passing a long row of identical, battered apartment doors. Some were heavily locked, others broken and vacant, their hinges snapped. On one, a faded welcome mat. On another, a child's drawing taped at eye level.

Rin slipped past the misery and stepped into the rusted service elevator at the end of the hall. 

She leaned against the back wall, exhaustion finally starting to settle into her bones as the car rattled upward. 

The light on the elevator panel hit her floor. The doors parted. 

Rin started to step out, and froze. 

Standing directly in front of the open door, waiting for the lift, were two Takeda-Holden Renewal Agents in their black tactical armor. Down the hall behind them, a frail old man was zip-tied and kneeling on the dirty floor outside his apartment, his face bruised as his meager belongings were tossed into the corridor. 

Rin slammed her hand against the elevator's 'Close Door' button, stepping backward into the car. 

The rusted doors began to slide shut, but an agent jammed a heavy, armored forearm between them. The metal groaned to a stop as the agent effortlessly forced the doors back open, stepped into the elevator, and blocked her escape. 

"Citizen!" the female agent barked, her voice heavily modulated through the helmet. "State your name, your prior location, and destination!"

Rin was trapped in the small metal box. It was the closest she had ever been to a Renewal agent. *Stay calm,* she told herself, keeping her face neutral. "I work at the Renewal offices downtown," she lied. "I'm just returning home."

The agent didn't budge. "Resident identification required for Renewal Protocol. Citizen, show me your I.D."

"Identification?" Rin asked, offering a forced, compliant nod. Her right hand slid slowly into her jacket, as she stepped forward toward the agent, "Sure, it's—"

She dropped the act, ripping the heavy pistol from her holster and driving the muzzle square into the center-mass of the agent's armor. 

"—RIGHT HERE!"

She held the gun firmly against the agent and pulled the trigger point blank. The blast inside the tiny metal elevator car was agonizingly loud, instantly taking her hearing and replacing it with a shrill, piercing whine. The muzzle flashed against the agent's chest as the round sparked and ricocheted off the sleek black plating. The agent stumbled. Rin shoved past her into the hall. The agent planted her feet, looking down at her chest. A split second, then the face of the black mask snapped toward Rin.

"WEAPON! CONTACT FRONT!" the agent yelled, raising her tactical rifle, together with the other agent.

Orange muzzle flashes erupted as both agents opened fire. The deafening gunfire was reduced to a muffled, rhythmic thumping beneath the high-pitched ringing in Rin's ears. Hot brass casings showered the concrete. She threw herself backward, scrambling behind the corner of the elevator shaft as a hail of bullets chewed through the plaster wall exactly where she had been standing. 

"Holy shit!" Rin exhaled through a heavy breath.

*Corporate-grade plating.* Rin's heart hammered against her ribs. *My bullets aren't even gonna scratch that armor. Gotta get out.*

Without hesitating, Rin abandoned the hallway and sprinted for the emergency stairwell, taking the concrete steps three at a time. 

"Dammit! Gone!" a muffled, modulated voice echoed down the stairwell behind her, barely audible over the ringing in her ears. "Radio it in. Tell them to send drone flyovers. Whoever she is, she won't get far."

Rin hit the landing below, holstering her useless pistol and rubbing her throbbing ear before disappearing into the dark alleyway.
