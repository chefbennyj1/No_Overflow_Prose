The night market of her sector was a claustrophobic canyon of commerce. Signs, half-dead and bleeding harsh pink and green into the fog. Rin scuffed her boots along the broken sidewalk. The smell of boiling noodles drifted from street carts as she shoved her way through the crowd in the storm.

Rin walked with her head down, the collar of her heavy leather jacket pulled up against the night air. She had kept a low profile all day. Meeting the detective eye to eye was a mistake she kept replaying in her mind. *How long have they known my alias?* she wondered. *How long have they been chasing Null? Was I really that careless?* She moved with purpose, wiping a streak of freezing rain from her cheek. *Always tastes like there's dirt in my mouth,* she thought, feeling the weight of the Dead Circuits again.

She veered away from the main thoroughfare, ducking under the dripping, corrugated awning of a derelict electronics shop to escape the downpour. The storefront was heavily barred, an iron cage protecting a graveyard of obsolete technology. Behind the rain-streaked glass, a dozen vintage, bulky television sets were stacked haphazardly atop one another. They were all tuned to the same channel, illuminating the slick street a flickering, sickly blue. On every screen, the identical, impassive face of a corporate anchorwoman delivered the evening broadcast. 

"—officials continue investigating an early morning unauthorized data breach at the Saito Takeda data hub," the anchorwoman’s modulated voice buzzed through a blown-out speaker above the door. "Renewal Agents are conducting authorized sweeps through several lower-city districts. Company representatives insist there is no evidence of self-directed machine intelligence—"

Rin tuned it out, as she pulled the red drive from her pocket. She turned it over, her thumb tracing the metal casing. *They wouldn't care about stolen routing keys,* she realized. She paused. *They're looking for this.* She pocketed it again securely and stepped back out from under the awning.

As she passed the darkened glass of an adjacent storefront, she caught the reflection of the electronics shop behind her. The dozen vintage screens had simultaneously spasmed. 

The anchorwoman's face fractured into heavy scanlines. Bleeding through the thick static in the reflection, a new visage formed in the cathode-ray fuzz. It was a face devoid of color—a black so dark and absolute it looked as though it formed an abyss stretching into eternity. Across that endless dark, subtle blue and white pixelated circuit traces glowed, tracing out her shape to form an almost perfected feminine face. Her eyes burned a blinding, brilliant white, flaring with a subtle blue aura along their lensed edges. She was breathtaking. She was terrifying.

Its expression remained perfectly still, hauntingly focused. The glowing white eyes in the reflection were tracking Rin's every step. Watching her.

Rin stopped cold, and whipped around to face the electronics shop. 

But the screens were perfectly normal. The identical, impassive face of the corporate anchorwoman droned on about the data breach, undisturbed. 

Rin stared at the televisions for a long moment, the freezing rain soaking into her collar. Her blood had run cold, she let out a shaky breath.
*It's just the exhaustion playing tricks,*  she told herself, though she didn't quite believe it. 

Unable to shake the creeping sensation that she was still being watched, Rin pulled her collar up higher and vanished into the fog.

***

Three blocks deep into the slums, a massive, geometric luxury cruiser idled silently, out of place, in the rain. It was a low-slung wedge of highly polished, glossy black panels and opaque tinted glass that reflected the city's lights like a dark mirror.  The heavy chassis was suspended inches above the broken pavement on a magnetic cushion. It vibrated with a low frequency that pushed the flooding rainwater away from its undercarriage in a perfect, continuous ripple.

Rin stopped at the mouth of the alley, her hand instinctively resting near the heavy pistol inside her jacket. Sharp angles defined the cruiser's shape, its red taillights bled onto the wet pavement like a warning.

The rear passenger window glided down with a quiet hiss, venting a cloud of gray smoke into the freezing rain. 

Madame Filament sat in the plush darkness of the backseat, her heavily wrinkled face illuminated only by the glowing cherry of her cigarette. Her gray hair was pulled back into a severe, immaculate bun, and her sharp gray eyes cut through the smoke to fixate on Rin. She was the undisputed matriarch of organized crime, a woman who controlled more raw power in the Dead Circuits than the mayor himself.

"Rin," the old woman rasped, her voice like grinding stones. "I see you made it out alive." She grinned a crooked smile, "But, it would seem you have the small issue of Renewal following you down here." The old woman's face almost looked concerned. But Rin knew better.

*The Filament,* Rin thought, keeping her expression perfectly neutral. *Not a group I should underestimate. But they hate the Corporation too.*

Rin stepped up to the window and reached into her jacket, bypassing the red drive and pulling out a secondary, generic black thumb drive. She tossed it through the open window. It landed on the luxurious leather seat beside the old woman. 

"Municipal routing keys, just like we agreed," Rin said. "You should have complete control over the sector's grid by midnight."

Madame Filament picked up the drive with a skeletal, ring-adorned hand. She turned it over, a grim smile pulling at the deep lines around her mouth. "When I control the routing, the district stops paying Saito Takeda for the privilege of freezing in the dark."

"I hope so," Rin said aloud.

***

Living in the Dead Circuits rarely had its benefits, but as Rin approached her apartment building, she noticed the flickering light over the doorway. *There's power tonight,* she thought. 

She pushed through the heavy front doors into the lower lobby. The air inside smelled stale. Chipped green paint curled off the walls, and the corners choked with shadows and dirt. She walked down the first-floor hallway, passing a long row of identical, battered apartment doors. Some were heavily locked, others stood broken and vacant, their hinges snapped. A few doors with pathetic attempts at normalcy—a faded welcome mat, a child's drawing taped to the door—desperate signs of families trying to carve out a decent living in otherwise dire straits. 

Rin slipped past the misery and stepped into the rusted service elevator at the end of the hall. 

She leaned against the back wall, exhaustion finally starting to settle into her bones as the car rattled upward. 

The light on the elevator panel hit her floor. The doors parted. 

Rin started to step out, but instantly froze. 

Standing directly in front of the open door, waiting for the lift, were two Saito Takeda Renewal Agents menacing in their sleek, form-fitting black tactical armor and featureless face helmets. Down the hall behind them, a frail old man was zip-tied and kneeling on the dirty floor outside his apartment, his face bruised as his meager belongings were tossed into the corridor. 

Rin slammed her hand against the elevator's 'Close Door' button, stepping backward into the car. 

The rusted doors began to slide shut, but an agent jammed a heavy, armored forearm between them. The metal groaned to a stop as the agent effortlessly forced the doors back open, stepping into the elevator, and blocked her escape. 

"Citizen!" the female agent barked, her voice heavily modulated through the helmet. "State your name, your prior location, and destination!"

Rin was trapped in the small metal box. It was the closest she had ever been to an actual Renewal agent. *Stay calm,* she told herself, keeping her face perfectly neutral. "I work at the Renewal offices downtown," she lied. "I'm just returning home."

*Dammit, don't ask me for I.D.* Rin thought.

The agent didn't budge. "Resident identification required for Renewal Protocol. Citizen, show me your I.D."

"Identification?" Rin asked, offering a forced, compliant nod. Her right hand slid slowly into the opening of her heavy leather jacket. "Sure, it's—"

In a single, fluid motion, she ripped her heavy black semi-automatic pistol from her shoulder holster, pushing it square into the center-mass of the agent's chest. 

"—RIGHT HERE!"

She held the gun firmly against the agent and pulled the trigger point blank. The blast inside the tiny metal elevator car was agonizingly loud, instantly taking her hearing and replacing it with a shrill, piercing whine. She watched the flash of her gun against the agent's chest—her round sparked, ricocheting off the sleek black plating. The agent stumbled. Rin took the opportunity to move past her into the hall. The agent planted her feet, shifting the face of the black mask towards Rin, tilting her head to the side like a curious animal. Rin didn't need to see the expression on the agent's face to know exactly what that meant.

"WEAPON! CONTACT FRONT!" the agent yelled, raising her tactical rifle, and drawing the attention of the other agent. 

Orange muzzle flashes erupted as both agents opened fire. The deafening gunfire was reduced to a muffled, rhythmic thumping beneath the high-pitched ringing in Rin's ears. Hot brass casings showered the concrete. She threw herself backward, scrambling behind the corner of the elevator shaft as a hail of bullets chewed through the plaster wall exactly where she had been standing. 

"Holy shit!" Rin exhaled out loud through a heavy breath.

*Corporate-grade plating,* Rin realized, her heart hammering against her ribs. *My bullets aren't even gonna scratch that armor. Gotta get out.*

Without hesitating, Rin abandoned the hallway and sprinted for the emergency stairwell, taking the concrete steps three at a time. 

"Dammit! Gone!" a muffled, modulated voice echoed down the stairwell behind her, barely audible over the ringing in her ears. "Radio it in. Tell them to send drone flyovers. Whoever she is, she won't get far."

Rin hit the landing below, holstering her useless pistol and rubbing her throbbing ear before disappearing into the dark alleyway.
