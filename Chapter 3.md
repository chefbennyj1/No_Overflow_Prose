Rin hauled her boots along the broken sidewalk, the collar of her leather jacket pulled up against the night air. The night market, in her part of Oshima, was a claustrophobic canyon of commerce, signs, half-dead and bleeding colors into the fog.  She shoved her way through the crowd in the storm, avoiding the sweeping gaze of the dirty corporate security cameras mounted on the street lamps. 

Meeting the detective eye to eye was a mistake she kept replaying in her mind. *How long have they known my alias?* she wondered. *How long have they been chasing Null?* The work for the Filament had made her known. She wiped a streak of freezing rain from her cheek. *Always tastes like there's dirt in my mouth,* she thought, feeling the weight of the Dead Circuits again.

Veering away from the main thoroughfare, Rin stepped through piles of garbage and broken glass. She ducked under the dripping, corrugated awning of a derelict electronics shop to escape the downpour. The storefront was barred, protecting a graveyard of obsolete technology. Behind the glass, a dozen vintage, bulky television sets were stacked haphazardly atop one another. They were all tuned to the same channel, casting a flickering light on the slick street. On every screen was the identical, impassive face of a synthetic news anchor delivering the evening broadcast. Under her eye, the glowing personal relations serial number bloomed against the old tubes.

"—officials continue to investigate an early morning data breach at a Takeda-Holden office building downtown," the anchorwoman’s voice buzzed through a blown-out speaker above the door. "Renewal Agents are sweeping parts of the lower city, in order to apprehend the suspect responsible—"

Rin tuned it out, as she pulled the red drive from her pocket. A muffled siren wailed deep in the city blocks behind her.  She turned the metal casing over, her thumb tracing the edges. *Renewal wouldn't care about stolen sanitation data.* She paused. *They're looking for this.* Pocketing the drive she stepped back out from under the awning. 

Degraded vehicles suspended above the broken pavement on magnetic cushions glided past sluggishly along the wet road. They vibrated loudly, pushing the flooding rainwater away in a continuous ripple. Rin crossed the road between the passing mag-cars and vanished into the fog.

Three blocks deep into the slums, an angled luxury cruiser idled in the rain, out of place. It was a low-slung wedge of glossy black panels and dark mirror glass that reflected the city's lights. Its magnetic cushion was a flawless, silent vibration.

The storm had begun a downpour. Rin stopped where the street opened out, her hand drawn to the pistol under her jacket. The cruiser sat low on its cushion, its taillights scattering a red glow on the flooded pavement. A man stood at the curb beneath a black umbrella, wearing a long black coat over a suit. His polished loafers—a dead giveaway—were getting ruined by the grime. He was a Minder. Hired security for important people. Someone significant was in the vehicle.

He nonchalantly watched the car, though his gaze kept shifting to the street and back. The Minder hauled on his cigarette as the headlights of a passing mag-car caught the bulge of the gun under his coat. She didn't need to look for the second one. He was across the road in the doorway of a gutted storefront, his grimacing face already watching her from under his umbrella. He gave a slow nod, signaling her to proceed, before stepping back against the shadows. 

Rain beat down, flooding the street in front of her. The rear passenger window of the black cruiser glided open, venting gray smoke in to the atmosphere.

Lifting her leather jacket above her head to shield her from the downpour, Rin crossed.

A man leaned forward from the plush darkness, his broad shoulders blocking most of the passenger cabin. His suit was sharp, hair cut short, a wrinkled face held one brown eye, the other blue. "I see you made it out alive," he said, tapping an unlit cigarette against the package he drew it from. 

Rin kept her expression neutral and stepped forward. Holding her jacket with one hand over her head, she reached into the back pocket of her jeans and pulled out a generic black thumb drive. She tossed it through the open window. It landed on the plush seat. 

The man sat back. Across from him sat a skeletal woman beneath an impeccably tailored white silk dress. Dark wire-rimmed glasses. She didn't speak. She drew on a cigarette fixed to a long filter and let the smoke go. The diamonds on her hands caught what light there was. Nothing about her belonged here, yet 'here' belonged to her.  

"Service access to the sites, just like we agreed," Rin said, her voice loud over the falling rain. She knew exactly who the woman in the car was. The fact that the Madame was there at all meant she was no longer overlooked by the Filament.  Rin tried to hide the nerves in her voice.

The man snatched the drive up. He turned it over in his heavy hands before presenting it to the old woman. 

The Madame took it, turning the drive over in her diamond-ringed fingers. "It seems you have a small issue of the Renewal following you down here, Erin Adler," her voice was low and gritty. Hearing the Madame of the Filament speaking her full name didn't sit well with Rin.

The Madame looked over the top of her wire-rimmed glasses, her eyes locking onto her. "They are sweeping the lower city. That is an awful lot of heat for service access. Tell me... is this all you took?"

Before Rin could answer, the streetlights above them died. 

The neon signs down the block spasmed and shut off. The entire area of the lower city plunged into a sudden blackout. People shouted out from deep in the city, as the lights of passing mag-cars painted long shadows on the broken brick. The only steady light left on the flooded street came from the red glow of the cruiser's taillights.

The Madame didn't even flinch. She took a slow drag from her long cigarette, the ember flaring bright in the dark. Smoke spilled from her lips just as the power surged back into the grid. The streetlights flickered to life. 

Rin felt the weight of the red drive in her pocket. She looked the Madame dead in the eye. "That's all of it. Nothing else."

The streetlights hummed overhead, warming from a flash of violet toward the orange of their sodium burn. In the growing light, the Madame studied Rin without blinking. "You should be more careful when you bring heat into my house," she said, exhaling a slow, steady cloud of gray smoke. "But, because you have been of service to us, if the Filament can be of assistance to you... we will."

"Yes, ma'am," Rin said, acknowledging an offer she had no intention of accepting.

The Madame gave a slight nod. The broad-shouldered man reached into his suit jacket and held something out the open window.

Rin snatched it out of the freezing rain. It was exactly what she had asked for. The gold lettering on the single mag-train ticket caught the red glow of the taillights: *SONORA TERRITORIES*.

The Madame leaned forward slightly, her eyes dropping to the ticket in Rin's hand. "The Sonora Territories," she said, a faint smile pulling at the deep lines around her mouth. "A very long way from the rain, Erin Adler."
