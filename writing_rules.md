# NO OVERFLOW - Writing Rules

1. **Concise Descriptions:** We cannot over-describe a situation. Use a maximum of one describing word per situation.
2. **Avoid Absolute Adjectives:** Words like "completely" or "abundantly" are not needed in this writing style.
3. **Strict POV:** Maintain a strict close-third perspective on the focal character (e.g., Blackwell). Do not rove into the internal thoughts or motivations of other characters; describe them only through the focal character's external observations and interpretations.
4. **Descriptive Word Economy:** If a descriptive word is used on a page, do not use it again for at least ten pages, and absolutely never reuse it on the same page.
5. **No AI Tells / Banned Words:** Absolutely NO use of the following banned words: "hum", "massive", "sprawling", "cavernous", "colossal", "ozone", "cyan", "purple", "palpable", "testament", "tapestry", "symphony", "cacophony", "labyrinth", "visceral", "ethereal", "sentinels", "obsidian", and "shroud". Push outside the AI comfort zone and find highly creative, original ways to describe the environment and colors without relying on these tropes.
6. **Varying Narrative Rhythm:** Alternate between two structural styles. For high-tension moments, use a relentless structural formula: Action → Visual description → Metaphor → Character observation → Another visual. For breathing room, switch to a traditional relaxed rhythm (description → dialogue → action → narration). Also, avoid repetitive sentence cadences (e.g., starting consecutive sentences/paragraphs with "He [verb]..."). Vary sentence openings to maintain flow.
7. **Workflow Safety:** The user frequently makes manual edits via their "Prose Engine." To prevent desyncs and accidental overwrites, ALWAYS use `view_file` to read the current state of a document before executing any text replacements. 
8. **Project Status Tracker:** Chapters 1 through 4 are fully polished and locked in. The worldbuilding regarding Parity, the Swarm Era, and Blackwell's betrayal is stored in `Lore_Bible.md`. Character design subversions (black vs white) are stored in `Parity_Design_Notes.md`.
9. **No Hedging in Description:** Never use uncertainty words ("probably," "could have," "seemed to," "must have," "maybe") in descriptive prose. Strict close-third POV means the focal character reports what they observe as fact, not as authorial guesswork.
10. **Banish "Just" (Except in Dialogue):** Never use the word "just" in narrative prose (e.g., "just out of place," "just looked"). It is a weak filler word that softens impact. However, it is fully permitted within dialogue to maintain realistic, grounded human speech patterns.
11. **Avoid Contrast Framing:** Do not rely on structural contrasts to describe things (e.g., "The city was X, but the room was Y", "It might have been A, but it looked like B"). State what things are directly with absolute authority.
12. **Post-Positive Adjectives (Use Sparingly):** Occasionally place adjectives after the noun (e.g., "a system, alien and indifferent") to break up standard SVO rhythms. This spikes perplexity/burstiness and evades AI detectors by mimicking jagged, intentional human style. Use sparingly so the prose doesn't sound backwards or overly poetic.
13. **Banish "Mutter" / "Muttered":** Never use the words "mutter" or "muttered." Find sharper, more physical ways to describe quiet or under-the-breath dialogue.
14. **Banish "Make Sense":** Never use phrases like "It doesn't make sense" or "Didn't make sense." Instead of having characters state their confusion, describe exactly what physical anomaly or contradiction is breaking their understanding.
15. **Cut Stacked Modifiers:** Replace "X, Y, and Z" phrasing with one strong image or clause. Let the sentence do one job instead of three. Save the grand language for the truly big moments, instead of making every paragraph sound like a thesis statement.

## Terminology (locked 15 Sep 2026)
Three overlapping "horizon" terms were collapsed, and the two names for the thing in the core now depend on who is speaking. Applied story-wide.
- **event horizon** — the singularity's ring of light. Used in the engine-interior chapters.
- **horizon memory** — the pocket universe inside, where the intelligence lives.
- **the boundary** — plain and unqualified. The outer edge where shutdown commands land and where Blackwell flips his bit. Never "horizon boundary" in prose; that phrase survives only as Iris's job title, *Horizon Boundary Analyst*, which does the mapping for the reader.
- **neural matrix** — always. "Neural lattice" is retired.
- **shadow process** — the log word, what the thing in the core is called before anyone understands it. Used by the logs, by Blackwell when he's covering it up, and by Iris before she finds the thesis.
- **inversion** — the thesis word, from *The Parity Effect: Persona Collapse and Synthetic Inversion Manifestation*. Available only to the two intelligences speaking about each other, and to humans who have read the paper: Blackwell always, Iris from Ch. 8, Elena from Ch. 25. Never use "inversion" in its physics sense (a thermal inversion is a "thermal drop") — the word belongs to one meaning.

## Character Physical Tics
Recurring, involuntary gestures that signal a character's interior state without narrating it directly (required by the strict-POV rule). Established so far:
- **Rin:** Tucks the jagged sidelock of her hime-cut behind her ear — a nervous tell, self-soothing/hiding.
- **Iris:** Shoves her black-rimmed glasses up the bridge of her nose — a compulsive tic when overwhelmed or forced away from her screens.
- **Blackwell:** The cigarette drag itself is the tell — deliberate, unhurried drags read as calculated menace/control.
- **Harper:** A collarbone scar, visible above her tank top and left deliberately unhidden (established Ch. 8). *Tell:* she runs her fingers across it when a moment gets too close to something real — especially anything Rin-related. The gesture reads outwardly as intimate/self-soothing, which is the point: it looks like seduction or comfort to an observer, but it's a flinch dressed up as calm, not an invitation. *Origin:* deliberately left unstated for now — a thread to pay off later (possible corporate-collar removal, echoing the city's marked/owned synthetics and canners). *Escalation:* when something genuinely rattles her, she does *not* reach for it — hand stays flat on the desk instead. The break in her own pattern is the tell that something's really wrong.
  - Note: Harper still smokes (it's that kind of city), but the cigarette is scene texture, not her identity-tic — that role belongs to Blackwell, whose deliberate drags read as calculated control.

## Relationship Threads
- **Rin & Harper:** History runs deeper than friendship. Through Ch. 8–17, this is played only through behavior — Harper's redirected touch (reaches for Rin, catches herself, touches her own scar instead), the "leaving's one thing, darlin'" line implying Rin left her once, and mirrored restraint (Rin watches Harper's hand, catches herself watching, looks away first). Keep those earlier scenes deniable exactly as written — a reader should be able to miss it on a first pass, or reread the whole thread differently once they catch it.
  - **Confirmed in Ch. 19:** Rin kisses Harper before sacrificing herself to the detectives — a deliberate, last-minute authorial choice to cash in the restraint at the moment of separation, not an accident or a slip. From this point forward, treat the relationship as an established, on-page fact rather than subtext — later chapters don't need to maintain the deniability the earlier ones do.
