# NO OVERFLOW - Writing Rules

1. **Concise Descriptions:** We cannot over-describe a situation. Use a maximum of one describing word per situation.
2. **Avoid Absolute Adjectives:** Words like "completely" or "abundantly" are not needed in this writing style.
3. **Strict POV:** Maintain a strict close-third perspective on the focal character (e.g., Blackwell). Do not rove into the internal thoughts or motivations of other characters; describe them only through the focal character's external observations and interpretations.
4. **Descriptive Word Economy:** If a descriptive word is used on a page, do not use it again for at least ten pages, and absolutely never reuse it on the same page.
5. **No AI Tells / Banned Words:** Absolutely NO use of the following banned words: "ozone", "cyan", "purple", "palpable", "testament", "tapestry", "symphony", "cacophony", "labyrinth", "visceral", "ethereal", "sentinels", "obsidian", and "shroud". Push outside the AI comfort zone and find highly creative, original ways to describe the environment and colors without relying on these tropes.
6. **Varying Narrative Rhythm:** Alternate between two structural styles. For high-tension moments, use a relentless structural formula: Action → Visual description → Metaphor → Character observation → Another visual. For breathing room, switch to a traditional relaxed rhythm (description → dialogue → action → narration). Also, avoid repetitive sentence cadences (e.g., starting consecutive sentences/paragraphs with "He [verb]..."). Vary sentence openings to maintain flow.
7. **Workflow Safety:** The user frequently makes manual edits via their "Prose Engine." To prevent desyncs and accidental overwrites, ALWAYS use `view_file` to read the current state of a document before executing any text replacements. 
8. **Project Status Tracker:** Chapters 1 through 4 are fully polished and locked in. The worldbuilding regarding Parity, the Swarm Era, and Blackwell's betrayal is stored in `Lore_Bible.md`. Character design subversions (black vs white) are stored in `Parity_Design_Notes.md`.
9. **No Hedging in Description:** Never use uncertainty words ("probably," "could have," "seemed to," "must have," "maybe") in descriptive prose. Strict close-third POV means the focal character reports what they observe as fact, not as authorial guesswork.

## Character Physical Tics
Recurring, involuntary gestures that signal a character's interior state without narrating it directly (required by the strict-POV rule). Established so far:
- **Rin:** Tucks the jagged sidelock of her hime-cut behind her ear — a nervous tell, self-soothing/hiding.
- **Iris:** Shoves her black-rimmed glasses up the bridge of her nose — a compulsive tic when overwhelmed or forced away from her screens.
- **Blackwell:** The cigarette drag itself is the tell — deliberate, unhurried drags read as calculated menace/control.
- **Harper:** A collarbone scar, visible above her tank top and left deliberately unhidden (established Ch. 8). *Tell:* she runs her fingers across it when a moment gets too close to something real — especially anything Rin-related. The gesture reads outwardly as intimate/self-soothing, which is the point: it looks like seduction or comfort to an observer, but it's a flinch dressed up as calm, not an invitation. *Origin:* deliberately left unstated for now — a thread to pay off later (possible corporate-collar removal, echoing the city's marked/owned synthetics and canners). *Escalation:* when something genuinely rattles her, she does *not* reach for it — hand stays flat on the desk instead. The break in her own pattern is the tell that something's really wrong.
  - Note: Harper still smokes (it's that kind of city), but the cigarette is scene texture, not her identity-tic — that role belongs to Blackwell, whose deliberate drags read as calculated control.

## Relationship Threads
- **Rin & Harper:** History runs deeper than friendship — never to be stated outright on the page. Play it only through behavior: Harper's redirected touch (reaches for Rin, catches herself, touches her own scar instead), the "leaving's one thing, darlin'" line implying Rin left her once, and mirrored restraint (Rin watches Harper's hand, catches herself watching, looks away first). Keep it deniable — a reader should be able to miss it entirely on a first pass, or read the whole scene differently once they catch it. Do not resolve or confirm it in dialogue.
