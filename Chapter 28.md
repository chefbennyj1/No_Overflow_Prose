The lights in the sterile hospital room buzzed and flickered. 

Iris stood at the window, her hands trembling inside the thick rubber gloves of the Level-4 bio-containment suit. Below, the Oshima cloud cover glowed with auburn light, pierced by plumes of dark smoke from the riots.

The lights surged back to a harsh white. 

"The Company is going to lock down the hospital," Iris whispered. "We're out of time."

Elena Marlowe lay in the mechanical bed, ribs wrapped tight, her battered frame swallowed by a thin gown. Iris paced at the foot of the mattress. 

"I can't do this," Iris said. "Pretending to be on staff is one thing. But there are two Renewal agents outside that door. If I wheel you out there, they are going to shoot us."

"Iris. Look at me."

Elena was physically broken, but her eyes held a hardened, clinical resolve. 

"You are a doctor," Elena said quietly. "You are in control. Zip the bag. It's time to go."

Iris swallowed hard. "They're going to want to see the body."

"Then you don't let them. Clear the floor."

Iris swiped her stolen medical badge at the room's terminal. She brought up the patient network, manually triggering critical vital alarms for three patients in the intensive care unit down the hall. 

A Code Blue bell sounded across the ward. Shouting triage nurses and the squeal of crash carts erupted in the corridor. 

"Now," Elena breathed. "Flatline the monitor."

Iris tapped the console, overriding the life support sensors. The rhythmic beeping of Elena's heart monitor instantly dropped into a continuous, piercing tone. 

Iris unrolled the white body bag across the mattress. The thick plastic was stamped with a yellow bio-hazard symbol. She pulled it over Elena's face and dragged the silver zipper closed.

"I can't do this," Iris whispered. 

"You can," Elena spoke from beneath the plastic. "Get us out."

Iris slammed her hand against the electronic door release. 

The door slid open. The flatline alarm flooded the corridor. 

Two Corporate Renewal agents instantly turned, clad in identical black tactical armor, vertical crimson seams cutting down their faceless visors.

Iris shoved the gurney into the hallway, steering straight at them. 

"Move!" Iris yelled through the rubber hood. "Code Orange! The asset flatlined!"

The guard on the right held up a black, mechanical hand, catching the silver zipper. "Doctor, protocol requires visual confirmation—"

"Do not break the seal!" Iris snapped. "Level 4 Airborne Infection Protocol. The asset was in direct contact with an unknown pathogen. Open that bag, and you contaminate this entire wing." She glared up at his visor. "If you want to explain to the Board why you forced them to incinerate this entire hospital, go ahead and unzip it."

The guard froze. He looked down at the yellow bio-hazard symbol, lifted his hand away, and took a half-step back.

Iris put her weight into the gurney and pushed it rapidly down the corridor.

Ahead, the service elevator chimed. The steel doors slid open. 

A four-man Renewal Extraction Team stood inside, black rifles held tight across their chests. 

Iris white-knuckled the metal rail. 

The lead agent stepped out, looking from the body bag to Iris's yellow suit. He grabbed the edge of the elevator door. 

"This is the asset."

"The asset has been in isolation for a month. She succumbed to her injuries." 

"Fine, Doctor. We'll accompany you to the sub-level morgue." 

"You are not wearing the proper protective equipment," Iris rasped.

"Exposure is a non-issue."

"Very well." She pushed the gurney in and slammed the sub-level button. 

The heavy steel doors sealed Iris inside with four corporate killers. 

As the elevator descended, the white plastic of the body bag quietly rose and fell. A necessary breath. Iris forced her gaze to the indicator panel. 

The lead agent pressed two fingers to his helmet. "Extraction Five One, Lead. Update primary objective. The asset is unsuitable for retrieval. Viability zero. We are escorting the remains to sub-level for incineration."

The elevator jolted to a stop. The numbers stopped counting down. 

The floor indicator switched to read *MAIN LOBBY*. 

The ceiling speaker crackled. *"Phase One Recall in effect. Upper floors are sealed. All cars are returning to the main lobby."*

"Lobby," the lead agent said, his voice dropping an octave.

"No," Iris said, panic spiking inside the yellow hood. "We need to go down. The pathogen—"

A rhythmic, echoing pop rattled up the elevator shaft from the floors below. Gunfire.

"Overridden," the agent said. He racked the bolt of his rifle. The other three agents raised their weapons, shifting into a tight tactical formation facing the steel doors. "Doctor, the lobby is compromised. When these doors open, step to the side for your own safety."

*Step to the side.* Elena was lying in the direct center of the car, zipped inside thin plastic, unable to take cover. 

Iris hauled the gurney backward, shoving it against the rear wall behind the agents' broad backs. She pressed herself into the corner.

*"Main floor,"* the automated voice announced. 

The steel doors slid open. 

A high-caliber rifle shot shattered the air. 

Two Renewal agents were thrown backward against the metal wall, their tactical armor cratered by heavy munitions. They crumpled in a spray of sparks. 

The remaining two agents lunged out of the elevator, firing their rifles into the smoke of the burning lobby. 

Iris dropped to her knees, tore the zipper down, and frantically shed the awkward yellow hazmat suit. 

Outside, fire alarms wailed over the crack of automatic gunfire. The lobby was a warzone. The brass Takeda-Holden crest hung by one corner over the broken reception desk. Waiting chairs were ripped from their bolts, piled into a barricade against the windows. Sprinklers rained over the marble.

Iris ripped the silver zipper of the body bag open. Elena gasped for air, clutching her battered ribs.

"Holy shit," Iris yelled over the gunfire.

She grabbed Elena's arm, dragging her off the mattress to the elevator floor. The steel doors were locked open.

"We're stuck on the ground floor," Elena moaned.

The only way out was through the fire.

Iris threw Elena's arm over her shoulder and stood up under the dead weight. They stepped out of the bullet-riddled elevator.

The lobby was in fragments. The red glow of the exit sign. The deafening crack of a rifle. Iris looked at the next three meters of floor and stepped over the sparking torso of a Renewal agent. 

A body lay in their path. A man in ratty street clothes, lying in his own blood with a Renewal rifle beside him. Not a soldier. Just the riot pushing in.

Iris kept going. The lobby sizzled and hissed with the sprinklers. 

"Almost there," Iris breathed.

Outside the entrance, a crowd of voices rose together.

The front of the hospital blew in.

Iris didn't hear it; she felt it. The heat threw her off the tile. The glass of the front entrance flew across the lobby in a glittering sheet. The revolving doors folded in on themselves and crashed to the floor.

Then, no sound at all.

A single thin whine filled her head. The fire moved without crackling. Somewhere to her right, a rifle flashed silently.

She was lying on her back. Dust hung in the smoke above her, falling too slowly. Her glasses were still on her face, one lens cracked straight across.

A shape leaned over her. Elena's lips were moving, shaping words Iris couldn't hear. A hand knotted itself in her collar—black fingers, carbon-fiber joints—and pulled.

"—on! Come on!"

The sound rushed back. Gunfire. Alarms. The roar of the Dead Circuits pouring in through the blown-out front of the hospital. Elena was on her knees, one arm clamped around her own ribs, the prosthetic hand locked in Iris's collar.

"Come on!"

Iris rolled onto her stomach. They crawled over the broken glass. 

A few feet away, a hospital orderly lay dead in a soaked white uniform. The impact had cracked his skull open. There was no blood or bone—only shattered silicone, wire tracking, and a vinyl face peeled back from a titanium jaw. A synth. 

Elena stopped crawling. She stared at the ruined face. Her prosthetic hand lifted off the glass and hung above the shattered jaw—black carbon fiber over bare titanium. The same joints. The same spacing.

"Elena!" Iris screamed.

Elena was trapped in the dead synthetic eyes. 

Iris grabbed Elena's gown, dug her boots into the glass, and pulled with everything she had. She dragged her away. 

They reached the blown-out frame of the doors, stumbled through the jagged opening, and hit the bitter, soot-choked wind of Oshima.

The street was worse.

A barricade of overturned mag-cars burned across the intersection. The crowd moved like water against the black shields of a Renewal line. A hundred voices threw back a megaphone's slogan.

Behind them, the fog swallowing the upper floors of the hospital glowed orange from the fires inside.

"Iris." Elena's weight sagged. "We can't walk out of this."

Thirty meters down the sidewalk sat the ambulance bay. Two white vehicles were tipped onto their sides. The third rested on its cushion, rear doors hanging open. 

Iris got Elena to the back of the ambulance and rolled her onto the stretcher. Iris slammed the doors and ran for the cab.

The driver's seat was warm. The dashboard was dark.

A scanner glowed. Iris swiped her stolen badge.

The dashboard woke. The cushion engaged with a shudder, lifting the ambulance off the concrete.

Iris had never driven anything larger than a commuter car. She stared at the controls through her cracked lens—Throttle. Cushion trim. Siren.

A white star bloomed in the windshield glass, inches from her face.

Iris hit the siren.

The sound tore down the street. She pushed the throttle forward. The ambulance lurched, scraped a burning car with a shriek of metal, and nosed out into the chaos.

"Iris," Elena's voice came from the back, thin under the siren. "Where are we going?"

Iris pushed her cracked glasses up her nose.

"I don't know," she said, and drove.
