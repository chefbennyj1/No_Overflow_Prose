The lights in the sterile hospital room buzzed and flickered. 

In the corner of the room, a muted wall-mounted television played the local broadcast. The news anchor—a polished public relations synthetic—was speaking impassively over frantic, handheld footage of the lower city. The screen flashed with images of rising smoke, shattered barricades, and swarms of people moving against Corporate Renewal riot lines. The upheaval had finally boiled over. 

Iris stood by the window, looking back at the screen. Her stolen white lab coat fell from her shoulders, stopping inches above the floor. She pushed her black-rimmed glasses up her nose, her hands trembling. 

Out the window, the Oshima cloud cover extended out below her. In the distance, red and auburn light glowed and swelled beneath the clouds. Plumes of dark smoke rose and dirtied the air the color of soot.

The lights overhead flickered again. This time, they stayed dim longer before surging back to a harsh white. 

Iris backed away from the window, her breathing shallow and rapid. "It's chaos out there," she whispered, her voice tight with panic. "The Company is going to lock down the hospital. We're out of time."

Elena Marlowe lay in the mechanical bed, her ribs wrapped tight, a thin hospital gown clinging to her battered frame. She watched Iris pace at the foot of the mattress. 

"Iris," Elena rasped, her throat still raw. 

"I can't do this," Iris said. "I can't do this. Pretending to be on staff is one thing, but escape. There are two Renewal agents outside that door. If I wheel you out there, they are going to look at me, and they are going to know, and they are going to shoot us."

"Iris. Look at me."

She stopped pacing, and looked at the woman in the bed. Physically broken, barely able to sit up, but Elena's eyes held the hardened, clinical resolve of the architect who had help built the Mnemosyne engine. 

"You are a doctor," Elena said, her voice quiet and absolute. "You are not pretending. You are in control. Put the sheet over me. It's time to go."

Iris swallowed hard. "They're going to want to see the body."

"Then you don't let them." Elena shifted her weight, falling back against the pillow. "But first, you need to clear the floor."

Iris understood. Turning to the room's integrated terminal, she swiped the stolen medical badge pinned to her coat. The panel's blurred privacy screen came into focus. She scanned the list of inpatient units. 

The screen flashed with the floor's telemetry network. Iris didn't hesitate. She dragged her finger across the display, manually overriding the vitals for three patients in the intensive care ward down the hall. 

A massive, synchronized Code Blue alarm sounded on the quiet of the floor. 

Through the door, they could hear the frantic shouting of the triage nurses and the squeal of crash carts sprinting past their room. The corridor descended into instant chaos. 

"Now," Elena breathed. "Flatline the monitor."

Iris turned to the console. She tapped the screen, overriding the localized life support sensors. The rhythmic beeping of Elena's heart monitor instantly flatlined into a piercing, continuous alarm. 

Iris pulled the heavy white hospital sheet up, dragging it over Elena's chest, over her face, until the woman was hidden. 

"I can't do this," Iris whispered to the sheet. 

"You can," Elena's muffled voice came from beneath the fabric. 

Iris shoved her glasses up her nose one last time. She slammed her hand against the electronic door release. 

The heavy door slid open. The piercing sound of the flatline alarm flooded the quiet corridor. 

The two Corporate Renewal agents instantly turned. They were imposing, clad in identical black tactical armor. Thin, vertical crimson seams cut down the center of their faceless black helmets.

Iris shoved the gurney into the hallway, steering it straight at them. 

"Move!" Iris yelled, her voice cracking with desperate authority. "The asset flatlined!"

The guard on the right held up a heavy, gloved hand, blocking her path. "Doctor, protocol requires visual confirmation of the—"

"The power surge fried her neural implant!" Iris snapped, slamming the gurney into his armored chest to force him back. "If I don't get this body into the sub-level cryogenic scanners in the next ninety seconds, the Company loses all of her residual brain data." She glared up at the black visor, her chest heaving. "If you want to explain to Silas Blackwell why his multi-billion dollar project is gone, go ahead and lift the sheet."

The guard froze. The name carried too much weight. He took a half-step back. 

Iris didn't wait. She put her weight into the gurney and pushed it rapidly down the polished corridor, fighting to keep the wheels straight.

The caster wheels squeaked and spun against the linoleum. From beneath the sheet, Elena whispered, "Keep moving."  

Up ahead, the digital display above the heavy freight elevators chimed. The steel doors slid open. 

Iris's blood ran cold. 

A four-man Renewal Disposition Team stood inside the car. They were fully armed, their black rifles held tight across their chests, dispatched to secure the exact asset Iris was pushing toward them. 

Iris's hands white-knuckled the metal rail. If they stepped out, the floor guards would tell them the asset was on the gurney. She couldn't make a mistake.

Before the lead agent could step onto the floor, Iris shoved the gurney violently into the elevator car, forcing the four heavily armed men to step backward to make room. 

"Going down!" Iris yelled, her voice frantic as she slammed her hand against the sub-level button. 

The lead agent grabbed the edge of the elevator door, stopping it from closing. He looked down at the sheet, then up at Iris. "We are here for the Jane Doe."

"You're too late," Iris fired back, her hands trembling on the gurney rail. "The asset flatlined. I have two minutes to scan the residual data before the neural implants degrades." She locked eyes with his black visor. "Do not make me miss this window."

The agent stared at her. Slowly, he released the door. "We will escort the body to the sub-level."

The heavy steel doors slid shut, sealing Iris inside a metal box with four corporate killers and the woman they were sent to dispose of. 

The elevator descended. The silence was absolute. Iris stood paralyzed, staring at the floor indicators counting down. Beneath the thin white sheet, inches from the commandos' combat boots, Elena didn't breathe. 

When the doors finally opened to the dark, echoing sub-level morgue, Iris shoved the gurney out into the corridor. The commandos followed. 

"I need the room clear for the scan," Iris ordered, her voice trembling but sharp. "Wait by the lifts."

She didn't look back. She pushed the gurney around the corner, instantly out of their line of sight. 

"Leave the bed," Elena whispered from under the sheet, already throwing the fabric back. She slid off the mattress, landing unsteadily on her bare feet, clutching her ribs. "It's got patient tracking in it."

Iris threw Elena's arm over her shoulder. She hit the wall panel, sending the nearest empty service elevator up to the top floor to scramble the telemetry. Together, they vanished into the darkness of the lower grid as the Disposition Team waited by the morgue doors.

"We did it," Iris breathed, looking at the blinking exit sign at the end of the dark corridor. "We actually did it."
