The lights in the sterile hospital room buzzed and flickered. 

Iris stood at the window. The yellow Level-4 bio-containment suit made every movement clumsy. She pushed her black-rimmed glasses up her nose, her hands trembling inside the thick rubber gloves.

The Oshima cloud cover lay below her. In the distance, red and auburn light swelled beneath it, and plumes of dark smoke rose through the clouds and dirtied the air.

The lights overhead flickered again. This time, they stayed dim longer before surging back to a harsh white. 

Iris backed away from the window, her breathing shallow and rapid. "The Company is going to lock down the hospital," she whispered. "We're out of time."

Elena Marlowe lay in the mechanical bed, her ribs wrapped tight, a thin hospital gown clinging to her battered frame. She watched Iris pace at the foot of the mattress. 

"Iris," Elena rasped, her throat still raw. 

"I can't do this," Iris said. "Pretending to be on staff is one thing... But escape? There are two Renewal agents outside that door. If I wheel you out there, they are going to look at me, and they are going to know, and they are going to shoot us."

"Iris. Look at me."

She stopped pacing and looked at the woman in the bed. She was physically broken, barely able to sit up. But her eyes held a hardened, clinical resolve. 

"You are a doctor," Elena said, her voice quiet and absolute. "You are not pretending. You are in control. Zip the bag. It's time to go."

Iris swallowed hard. "They're going to want to see the body."

"Then you don't let them." Elena shifted her weight, falling back against the bed. "—you need to clear the floor."

Iris turned to the room's hospital terminal and swiped the stolen medical badge pinned to her containment suit. The panel's blurred privacy screen came into focus. She scanned the list of inpatient units. 

The screen flashed with the floor's patient network. Iris didn't hesitate. She dragged her finger across the display, manually triggering the critical vital alarms for three patients in the intensive care room at the other side of the floor. 

The bell alarm of a Code Blue sounded across the ward. 

Through the door, they could hear the shouting of the triage nurses and the squeal of crash carts sprinting past their room. The corridor erupted into action. 

"Now," Elena breathed. "Flatline the monitor."

Iris turned to the console. She tapped the screen, overriding the localized life support sensors. The rhythmic beeping of Elena's heart monitor instantly flatlined into a continuous tone. 

Iris unrolled the white body bag across the mattress. The thick plastic was stamped with a yellow bio-hazard symbol. She pulled the material over Elena's chest, over her face, and pulled the silver zipper closed.

"I can't do this," Iris whispered to the bag. 

"You can," Elena spoke from beneath the plastic. "We need to get out of here."

Iris pushed her glasses up her nose one last time. She slammed her hand against the electronic door release. 

The door slid open, and the containment field dropped. The piercing sound of the flatline alarm flooded the corridor. 

The two Corporate Renewal agents instantly turned. They were imposing, clad in identical black tactical armor. Thin, vertical crimson seams cut down the center of their faceless black helmets.

Iris shoved the gurney into the hallway, steering it straight at them. 

"Move!" Iris yelled, her voice muffled but frantic through the rubber of the bio-suit. "Code Orange! The asset flatlined!"

The guard on the right held up a black, mechanical gloved hand, blocking her path. He reached down, his armored fingers catching the silver zipper of the body bag. "Doctor, protocol requires visual confirmation of the—"

"Do not break the seal!" Iris snapped. "This is a Level 4 Biosafety Airborne Infection Protocol. The asset was in direct contact with an unknown pathogen in the Mnemosyne lab. If you open that bag in this hallway, you will contaminate this entire wing." She glared up at his faceless black visor, her chest heaving. "If you want to explain to the people upstairs why you forced them to incinerate this entire hospital ward, go ahead and unzip it."

The guard froze. His faceless helmet turned down toward the yellow bio-hazard symbol on the white body-bag. He lifted his hand away from the zipper, and took a half-step back.

Iris didn't wait. She put her weight into the gurney and pushed it rapidly down the corridor, fighting to keep the wheels straight.

The caster wheels squeaked and spun against the linoleum. From beneath the plastic, Elena whispered, "Keep moving."  

Up ahead, the digital display above the service elevators chimed. The steel doors slid open. 

Iris lost a breath. 

A four-man Renewal Extraction Team stood inside the elevator. They were fully armed, their black rifles held tight across their chests. 

Iris's hands white-knuckled the metal rail. She couldn't make a mistake.

The lead agent stepped out onto the floor. He looked down at the white body bag, then up at Iris's yellow suit. He looked past her toward the room they had come from. He grabbed the edge of the elevator door, stopping it from closing. 


"This is the asset." The agent asserted in a modulated voice.

"The asset has been in a coma and in isolation for a month. She finally succumbed to her injuries." 

The agent nodded to her. Her grip on the metal rail loosened.

"Fine, Doctor. We'll accompany you to the sub-level morgue." 

Iris swallowed. Her voice rasped, "You are not wearing the proper protective equipment to be in close quarters with the subject."
The agent's faceless visor locked onto her. Inside the ballooned bio-suit, a line of sweat crawled down Iris's spine.

"Exposure is a non-issue, Doctor." The modulated voice of the agent was firm.

"Very well, Agents." She pushed the gurney into the elevator with the four agents, and slammed her hand against the sub-level button. 


The heavy steel doors slid shut, sealing Iris inside with four corporate killers and the woman they were tasked to extract. 

The elevator descended. Inches from the agents' waist, the white plastic of the body bag quietly rose and fell. A necessary breath. Iris forced her head up, snapping her gaze to the elevators  indicator panel, counting down the floors above the door. 

The lead agent pressed two armored fingers to the side of his helmet. "This is Extraction Five One, Lead. Update primary objective. The asset is unsuitable for retrieval. She has expired. Viability is at zero." He paused, listening to a voice in his earpiece. "Affirmative. We are escorting the remains to sub-level for incineration."

The elevator stopped its descent with a sudden clunky sound, jolting the lift. Iris reached out to steady herself against the wall. The digital display above the door flashed, and the numbers stopped counting down. 

Iris felt her stomach drop. The floor indicator switched to read *MAIN LOBBY*. 

The lead agent turned his head toward the display. "Phase One Recall," he said, his modulated voice dropping an octave.

"No," Iris said, panic spiking inside the yellow helmet. "We need to go down. The pathogen—"

"Overridden," the agent said. He racked the bolt of his rifle. The other three agents raised their weapons, shifting into a tight tactical formation facing the steel doors. "Doctor, the lobby is compromised. When these doors open, I will need you to step to the side for your own safety."

Iris's pulse pounded. *Step to the side.* Elena was lying on the gurney in the direct center of the car, zipped inside a thin plastic bag, unable to take cover. 

Iris grabbed the rails of the gurney and hauled it backward, shoving it against the rear wall of the elevator behind the broad backs of the agents. She pressed herself into the corner.

The bell chimed. *"Main floor,"* the automated voice announced. 

The steel doors slid open. 

A high-caliber rifle shot shattered the air. 

Before the doors were fully parted, two of the Renewal agents were thrown backward against the metal wall, their bullet-proof tactical armor violently cratered by the heavy munitions. They crumpled to the floor, sparking and leaking dark fluid. 

The remaining two agents didn't hesitate. They lunged out of the elevator, firing their rifles into the smoke-filled, burning chaos of the hospital lobby to tackle the shooter. 

Iris dropped to her knees. She grabbed the collar of the hazmat suit and tore the zipper down, frantically shedding the awkward yellow plastic. 

Outside the elevator, fire alarms wailed over the crack of automatic gunfire. From the floor of the car, Iris could see a slice of the lobby through the open doors. White stone rose three stories to a skylight. Above the reception desk, the brass Takeda-Holden crest hung by one corner. The waiting chairs had once stood bolted to the floor in neat rows. Someone had torn them up and piled them into a barricade across the entrance. Sprinklers rained over the marble, and black smoke rolled along the ceiling, lit orange from underneath.

Iris crawled to the gurney and ripped the silver zipper of the body bag open. Elena gasped for air, her eyes wide, clutching her battered ribs.

"Holy shit," Iris yelled over the gunfire.

She grabbed Elena's arm and dragged her off the mattress. They hit the floor of the elevator hard. Elena let out a sharp cry. The steel doors were locked open.

"We're stuck on the ground floor," Elena moaned through the pain.

The only way out was through the fire.

Iris threw Elena's arm over her shoulder. She was an analyst. She had never been shot at. She stood up under the dead weight of a woman with broken ribs, and they stepped out of the bullet-riddled elevator.

Iris saw the lobby in fragments. 

The red glow of the exit sign. The broken marble. Elena's arm slipping down her shoulder. The deafening crack of a rifle somewhere to the left. Iris didn't look left. She looked at the next three meters of floor.

The weight of Elena slowed her progress. Dark synth fluid pooled across the tile, slick as oil. Iris stepped over the sparking torso of a Renewal agent. Her boot caught the slick edge of the fluid. 

She went down. Her shoulder hit the floor. Elena landed on top of her. The breath was forced out of her lungs. Gunfire tore through the drywall above their heads, raining white dust over them. Getting back up took more strength than running. 

Iris forced herself to her knees. She hauled Elena up by the gown. 

The smoke was coming down. It had sunk halfway to the floor, a black ceiling dropping lower with every breath. Iris looked at it. "We need to stay low," Iris yelled. 

She pulled Elena back to the tile. They crawled. Iris dragged herself forward on her elbows, pulling Elena by the fabric of her gown. 

A body lay in their path. 

Not a soldier. A man in ratty street clothes. He was lying in blood. He had died pressing his hands against his stomach wound. 

Iris kept crawling. She couldn't stop. The lobby sizzled with the crackle of fire, the hiss of broken water mains. Iris and Elena had made their way close to the front entrance of the hospital.

"Almost there, Elena."

Outside the entrance, a crowd's worth of voices rose together.

The front of the hospital blew in.

Iris didn't hear it. She felt it. A hand of heat picked her up off the tile and set her down a meter to the left. Safety glass came across the lobby in a glittering sheet. The revolving doors folded in on themselves and fell.

Then there was no sound at all.

A single high note filled her head, thin and pure, like a wet finger drawn around the rim of a glass. Everything else was gone. The fire moved without crackling. The sprinklers fell without a hiss. Somewhere to her right, the muzzle of a rifle flashed, and flashed again, and made no noise.

She was lying on her back. She didn't remember turning over. Dust hung in the smoke above her, falling too slowly. Her glasses were still on her face, one lens cracked straight across, splitting the ceiling in two.

A shape leaned over her. Its mouth was moving.

Elena.

The note held. Iris watched Elena's lips shape words she couldn't hear. A hand knotted itself in her collar—black fingers, carbon-fiber joints—and pulled.

The note thinned. Under it, far away, something like a voice.

"—on! Come on!"

The sound came back all at once. Gunfire. Alarms. The roar of the Dead Circuits pouring in through the hole where the front of the hospital had been. Elena was on her knees above her, one arm clamped around her own ribs, the prosthetic hand locked in Iris's collar.

"Come on!" Elena screamed.

Iris rolled onto her stomach. They crawled.

They crawled over the broken glass. A man lay sprawled inside the ruined entrance, wearing the ragged clothes of the lower city. His fingers were locked around the grip of a heavy, matte-black automatic rifle. It looked exactly like the weapons the Renewal agents carried. 

A few feet away, a hospital orderly lay dead. His white uniform was soaked in dark fluid. His head was turned toward them. 

The impact had cracked his skull open. Inside the fissure, there was no bone. Only shattered silicone, wire tracking, and a vinyl face peeled back from a titanium jaw. A synth. 

Elena stopped crawling. 

She stared at the ruined face. Her hands went slack. She had designed that jaw. She had built the tracking in those eyes. The war tearing the city apart was being fought over the architecture in her head.

"Elena!" Iris screamed.

Elena didn't move. She was trapped in the dead synthetic eyes. 

Iris grabbed the collar of Elena's gown. She dug her boots into the glass and pulled with everything she had. She dragged the architect away from her creation. 

They reached the blown-out frame of the doors. Iris hoisted Elena up. They stumbled through the jagged opening, out of the sterile air of the hospital, and into the bitter, soot-choked wind of Oshima.
