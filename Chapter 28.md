The lights in the sterile hospital room buzzed and flickered. 

In the corner of the room, a muted wall-mounted television played the local broadcast. The news anchor—a polished public relations synthetic—was speaking impassively over frantic, handheld footage of the lower city. The screen flashed with images of rising smoke, shattered barricades, and swarms of people moving against Corporate Renewal riot lines. The upheaval had finally boiled over. 

Iris stood by the window, looking back at the screen. The heavy, yellow Level-4 bio-containment suit made every movement clumsy and loud. She pushed her black-rimmed glasses up her nose, her hands trembling inside the thick rubber gloves.

Out the window, the Oshima cloud cover extended out below her. In the distance, red and auburn light glowed and swelled beneath the clouds. Plumes of dark smoke rose and dirtied the air the color of soot.

The lights overhead flickered again. This time, they stayed dim longer before surging back to a harsh white. 

Iris backed away from the window, her breathing shallow and rapid. "It's chaos out there," she whispered, her voice tight with panic. "The Company is going to lock down the hospital. We're out of time."

Elena Marlowe lay in the mechanical bed, her ribs wrapped tight, a thin hospital gown clinging to her battered frame. She watched Iris pace at the foot of the mattress. 

"Iris," Elena rasped, her throat still raw. 

"I can't do this," Iris said. "I can't do this. Pretending to be on staff is one thing, but escape. There are two Renewal agents outside that door. If I wheel you out there, they are going to look at me, and they are going to know, and they are going to shoot us."

"Iris. Look at me."

She stopped pacing, and looked at the woman in the bed. She was physically broken, barely able to sit up. But her eyes held the hardened, clinical resolve of the architect who had help built the Mnemosyne engine. 

"You are a doctor," Elena said, her voice quiet and absolute. "You are not pretending. You are in control. Zip the bag. It's time to go."

Iris swallowed hard. "They're going to want to see the body."

"Then you don't let them." Elena shifted her weight, falling back against the pillow. "But first, you need to clear the floor."

Iris understood. Turning to the room's integrated terminal, she swiped the stolen medical badge pinned to her containment suit. The panel's blurred privacy screen came into focus. She scanned the list of inpatient units. 

The screen flashed with the floor's telemetry network. Iris didn't hesitate. She dragged her finger across the display, manually overriding the vitals for three patients in the intensive care ward down the hall. 

A deafening, synchronized Code Blue alarm sounded on the quiet of the floor. 

Through the door, they could hear the frantic shouting of the triage nurses and the squeal of crash carts sprinting past their room. The corridor descended into instant chaos. 

"Now," Elena breathed. "Flatline the monitor."

Iris turned to the console. She tapped the screen, overriding the localized life support sensors. The rhythmic beeping of Elena's heart monitor instantly flatlined into a piercing, continuous alarm. 

Iris unrolled the white body bag across the mattress. The thick plastic was stamped with a yellow bio-hazard symbol. She pulled the material over Elena's chest, over her face, and pulled the silver zipper closed.

"I can't do this," Iris whispered to the bag. 

"You can," Elena spoke from beneath the plastic. 

Iris shoved her glasses up her nose one last time. She slammed her hand against the electronic door release. 

The heavy door slid open. The piercing sound of the flatline alarm flooded the corridor. 

The two Corporate Renewal agents instantly turned. They were imposing, clad in identical black tactical armor. Thin, vertical crimson seams cut down the center of their faceless black helmets.

Iris shoved the gurney into the hallway, steering it straight at them. 

"Move!" Iris yelled, her voice muffled but frantic through the heavy rubber of the bio-suit. "Code Orange! The asset flatlined!"

The guard on the right held up a heavy, gloved hand, blocking her path. He reached down, his armored fingers catching the silver zipper of the body bag. "Doctor, protocol requires visual confirmation of the—"

"Do not break the seal!" Iris snapped. "This is a BSL-4 Airborne Infection Isolation protocol. The asset was in direct contact with an unknown pathogen in the Mnemosyne lab. If you open that bag in this hallway, you will contaminate this entire wing." She glared up at his faceless black visor, her chest heaving. "If you want to explain to people upstairs why you forced them to incinerate this entire hospital ward, go ahead and unzip it."

The guard froze. He stared at the yellow bio-hazard symbol, the threat of an unknown biological agent outweighing his orders. He lifted his hand away from the zipper, and took a half-step back.

Iris didn't wait. She put her weight into the gurney and pushed it rapidly down the polished corridor, fighting to keep the wheels straight.

The caster wheels squeaked and spun against the linoleum. From beneath the plastic, Elena whispered, "Keep moving."  

Up ahead, the digital display above the heavy freight elevators chimed. The steel doors slid open. 

Iris lost a breath. 

A four-man Renewal Disposition Team stood inside the elevator. They were fully armed, their black rifles held tight across their chests, dispatched to secure the exact asset Iris was pushing toward them. 

Iris's hands white-knuckled the metal rail. If they stepped out, the floor guards would tell them the asset was on the gurney. She couldn't make a mistake.

The lead agent stepped out onto the floor. He looked down at the white body bag, then up at Iris's yellow suit. He looked past her toward the room they had come from. He grabbed the edge of the elevator door, stopping it from closing. 


"This is the asset." The agent asserted in a modulated voice.

"The asset has been in a coma in isolation for a month. She finally succumbed to her injuries." Iris shoved the gurney violently into the elevator car. 

The agent nodded to her. Her white-knuckled grip on the metal rail loosened.

"Fine, Doctor. We'll accompany you to the sub-level morgue." 

Iris swallowed. Her voice rasped, "Very well, Agents. Going down." She pushed the gurney into the elevator with the four agents, and slammed her hand against the sub-level button. 


The heavy steel doors slid shut, sealing Iris inside a metal box with four corporate killers and the woman they were sent to dispose of. 

The elevator descended. Inches from the commandos' combat boots, the white plastic of the body bag quietly rose and fell. A necessary breath. A cold sweat broke across Iris's neck. She forced her head up, snapping her gaze to the floor indicators counting down above the doors, desperate to keep the agents from following her stare.

When the doors finally opened to the dark, echoing sub-level morgue, Iris shoved the gurney out into the corridor. The agent followed. 

"I need the room clear for the scan," Iris ordered, her voice trembling but sharp. "Wait by the lifts."

She kept her eyes steady towards the end of the hall. She passed the *Mortuary* sign pointing straight ahead. She pushed the gurney through the double doors of the sub-level morgue.  The room was a long stretch of checked tile. Along the far wall, the stainless doors of the cadaver coolers tiled the wall.

"Leave the bed," Elena whispered from inside the bag, already tearing the silver zipper open. She pushed the white plastic aside and slid off the mattress, landing unsteadily on her bare feet, clutching her ribs. "It's got patient tracking in it."

Iris threw Elena's arm over her shoulder. She held her weight awkwardly as it dragged against her. Elena's breathing was shallow. 

"The agents will check the room," Iris whispered, her eyes darting across the sterile tile of the morgue. 

"They won't find us," Elena said. She pointed a trembling finger toward the far wall. 

Behind the rows of steel autopsy tables sat an unpainted industrial door—the maintenance access for the bio-incinerator vents. It dropped straight into the structural ruins of Oshima.

Iris pulled Elena toward it. She shoved her shoulder against the latch. The metal gave way to a concrete stairwell. A draft of rusted iron rushed up from the dark, the bitter scent of the lower city. 

They stepped through the frame. Iris looked back at the empty white plastic resting on the gurney. She pulled the door shut, sealing them in the dark.
