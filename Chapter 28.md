Iris stood at the window. The yellow Level-4 bio-containment suit made every movement clumsy. Its hood hung down her back. She pushed her black-rimmed glasses up her nose, her hands trembling inside the thick rubber gloves.

The Oshima cloud cover lay below her. In the distance, red light swelled beneath it, and smoke rose through the clouds.

The lights overhead flickered and stayed dim before surging back to white.

Iris backed away from the window. "The Company is going to lock down the hospital," she whispered. "We're out of time."

Elena Marlowe lay in the mechanical bed, her ribs wrapped tight under a thin hospital gown. Iris paced at the foot of the mattress.

"Iris," Elena rasped, her throat still raw.

"I can't do this," Iris said. "Pretending to be on staff is one thing... But escape? There are two Renewal agents outside that door. If I wheel you out there, they are going to look at me, and they are going to know, and they are going to shoot us."

"Iris. Look at me."

Iris stopped pacing. Elena could barely sit up, but her eyes were steady.

"You are a doctor," Elena said, her voice quiet and absolute. "You are not pretending. You are in control. Zip the bag. It's time to go."

Iris swallowed hard. "They're going to want to see the body."

"Then you don't let them." Elena fell back against the bed. "You need to clear the floor."

Iris swiped the stolen badge at the room's terminal. The floor's patient network came up. She dragged her finger across it and triggered the critical alarms for three patients in intensive care, at the far end of the floor.

The bell alarm of a Code Blue sounded across the ward.

Nurses shouted in the corridor. Crash carts squealed past the door.

"Now," Elena breathed. "Flatline the monitor."

Iris tapped the console. Elena's heart monitor dropped into one long, flat tone.

Iris unrolled the white body bag across the mattress. The plastic was stamped with a yellow bio-hazard symbol. She pulled it over Elena's chest, over her face, and zipped it closed.

"I can't do this," Iris whispered to the bag.

"You can," Elena said from under the plastic. "We need to get out of here."

Iris pulled the hood up over her head and sealed it. She slammed her hand against the electronic door release.

The door slid open, and the containment field dropped. The flatline alarm flooded the corridor.

The two Renewal agents turned, the red seams glowing down the center of their helmets.

Iris shoved the gurney into the hallway, steering it straight at them.

"Move!" Iris yelled, her voice muffled by the hood. "Code Orange! The asset flatlined!"

The guard on the right held up a hand. Then he reached down and caught the zipper of the body bag. "Doctor, protocol requires visual confirmation of the—"

"Do not break the seal!" Iris snapped. "This is a Level 4 Biosafety Airborne Infection Protocol. The asset was in direct contact with an unknown pathogen in the Mnemosyne lab. If you open that bag in this hallway, you will contaminate this entire wing." She glared up at his visor. "If you want to explain to the people upstairs why you forced them to incinerate this entire hospital ward, go ahead and unzip it."

The guard froze. His helmet tipped down toward the bio-hazard symbol. He let go of the zipper and took a half-step back.

Iris didn't wait. She put her weight into the gurney and pushed it down the corridor, the wheels squeaking on the linoleum.

Up ahead, the digital display above the service elevators chimed. The steel doors slid open.

Iris lost a breath.

A four-man Renewal Extraction Team stood inside, rifles held across their chests.

Iris's hands white-knuckled the metal rail.

The lead agent stepped out and caught the elevator door. He looked down at the body bag, then up at Iris.

"This is the asset."

"The asset has been in a coma and in isolation for a month. She finally succumbed to her injuries."

The agent nodded to her. Her grip on the metal rail loosened.

"Fine, Doctor. We'll accompany you to the sub-level morgue."

"You are not wearing the proper protective equipment for close quarters with the subject," Iris said.

The agent's visor locked onto her. Inside the suit, a line of sweat crawled down Iris's spine.

"Exposure is a non-issue, Doctor."

"Very well, Agents." She pushed the gurney into the elevator and slammed her hand against the sub-level button.

The heavy steel doors slid shut, sealing Iris inside with four corporate killers and the woman they were tasked to extract.

The elevator descended. Inches from the agents, the plastic over Elena's mouth crinkled each time she breathed. Iris made herself watch the floor numbers.

The lead agent pressed two fingers to the side of his helmet. "Extraction Five One. The asset has expired." He paused, listening. "Affirmative. Escorting the remains to sub-level for incineration."

The elevator jolted. Iris caught herself against the wall. The floor numbers stopped, then switched to *MAIN LOBBY*.

The ceiling speaker crackled. *"Phase One Recall in effect. All cars are returning to the main lobby."*

The lead agent turned his head toward the display. His modulated voice dropped an octave. "Lobby."

"No," Iris said, panic spiking inside the yellow hood. "The pathogen—"

The sound of gunfire rattled up the shaft from below.

"Overridden," the agent said. He racked the bolt of his rifle. The other three raised their weapons toward the doors. "Doctor, the lobby is compromised. When these doors open, step to the side."

*Step to the side.* Elena lay on the gurney in the middle of the car, zipped inside a plastic bag, with nowhere to take cover.

Iris hauled the gurney back against the rear wall, behind the agents, and pressed herself into the corner.

The gurney hit the wall. Inside the bag, Elena gasped.

The two agents at the back of the car looked down. The plastic over Elena's mouth crinkled, pulled in, crinkled again. Both helmets snapped to Iris.

One of them stepped toward the front of the car. "Sir. The asset is—"

The lead agent turned his head.

The bell chimed. *"Main floor."*

The steel doors slid open.

A high-caliber rifle shot shattered the air.

The lead agent was still looking back when it hit him. He and the agent beside him were thrown backward, their tactical armor cratered by the heavy munitions. They crumpled to the floor in a spray of sparks.

A round tore through the middle of the car, where the gurney had been a moment before.

The last two agents lunged out of the elevator, firing into the smoke of the burning lobby. One of them looked back at Iris before the smoke took him.

Iris dropped flat behind the fallen agents. Rounds sparked off the doorframe above her. Inside the hood, her own breathing was the loudest thing in the world.

She crawled to the gurney and found the zipper of the body bag. The thick gloves slid off it. She tried again. On the third try it caught, and she ripped the bag open. Elena gasped for air, clutching her ribs.

"Holy shit," Iris said. It came out muffled, too loud inside the plastic.

She dragged Elena off the gurney and down behind the bodies. Elena cried out.

The only way out was through the fire.

Iris threw Elena's arm over her shoulder. She stood up under the dead weight of a woman with broken ribs, and they stepped out of the bullet-riddled elevator.

The faceplate fogged with every breath. Iris saw the lobby in fragments. The red glow of the exit sign. The broken marble. Elena's arm slipping down her shoulder. A rifle cracking somewhere to the left, dull through the plastic. She looked at the next three meters of floor.

Iris stepped over the sparking torso of a Renewal agent. Gunfire tore through the drywall above their heads, raining white dust down the faceplate.

A body lay in their path. A man in ratty street clothes, lying in his own blood. He had died pressing his hands against his stomach. A Renewal rifle lay beside him.

Iris kept going. Sprinklers rained over the marble. Above the broken reception desk, the brass Takeda-Holden crest hung by one corner.

They reached a pillar near the entrance. The shooting had moved deeper into the lobby. Iris eased Elena down against the stone and dragged the hood off her head.

Air hit her wet face, cold and full of smoke. Without the plastic, the alarms were sharp and close. Through the doors, the street was right there.

*Oh my god,* she thought. *We did it.*

"Almost there, Elena."

Outside the entrance, a crowd of voices rose together.

The front of the hospital blew in.

Iris didn't hear it. She felt it. The heat threw her off the tile and she hit the ground. The glass of the front entrance flew across the lobby in a glittering sheet. The revolving doors folded in on themselves and crashed to the floor.

Then no sound at all. A single thin high whine filled her head. Everything else was gone. The fire moved without crackling. The sprinklers fell without a hiss. Somewhere to her right, the muzzle of a rifle flashed, and flashed again, and made no noise.

She was lying on her back. She didn't remember turning over. Dust hung in the smoke above her, falling too slowly. Her glasses were still on her face, one lens cracked straight across, splitting the ceiling in two.

A shape leaned over her. Its mouth was moving.

Elena.

Iris watched Elena's lips shape words she couldn't hear. A hand knotted itself in her collar—black fingers, carbon-fiber joints—and pulled.

"—on! Come on!"

The sound came back all at once. Gunfire. Alarms. The roar of the Dead Circuits pouring in through the hole where the front of the hospital had been. Elena was on her knees above her, one arm clamped around her own ribs, the prosthetic hand locked in Iris's collar.

"Come on!" Elena screamed.

Iris rolled onto her stomach. They crawled.

They crawled over the broken glass.

A few feet away, a hospital orderly lay dead. His white uniform was soaked in dark fluid. His head was turned toward them.

The impact had cracked his skull open. There was no blood or bone. Only shattered silicone, wire tracking, and a vinyl face peeled back from a titanium jaw. A synth.

Elena stopped crawling. Her face went gray. She had designed that face.

"Elena!" Iris screamed.

Elena didn't move.

Iris grabbed the collar of Elena's gown. She dug her boots into the glass and pulled with everything she had. She dragged Elena away.

Iris hoisted Elena up, and they stumbled out through the blown-out doors into the soot-choked wind of Oshima.

The street outside was worse.

Overturned mag-cars burned across the intersection. Beyond them, a crowd pushed against the shields of a Renewal line.

"Iris." Elena's weight sagged against her. "We can't walk out of this."

Thirty meters down the sidewalk, two ambulances lay tipped on their sides. A third sat on its cushion with its rear doors hanging open, the Takeda-Holden crest printed on its flank.

Iris got Elena to the back of the ambulance and half lifted, half rolled her onto the stretcher. Elena bit down on a scream and held it. Iris slammed the rear doors and ran for the cab.

The driver's seat was still warm. The dashboard was dark.

A scanner glowed beside the steering wheel, waiting for a credential. Iris pulled the stolen badge off her suit and pressed it against the scanner.

The dashboard woke. The cushion engaged with a shudder, and the ambulance lifted off the concrete and hung there, rocking.

She had never driven anything larger than a commuter car. Through her cracked lens, she read the console the way she read the boundary readouts in the lab. Throttle. Cushion trim. Siren.

Something hit the windshield. A white star bloomed in the glass, inches from her face.

Iris froze.

"Drive!" Elena screamed from the back.

Iris shoved her cracked glasses up her nose and hit the siren. She pushed the throttle forward. The ambulance lurched, scraped a burning car with a shriek of metal, and swung out into the street.
