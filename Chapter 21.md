"Four weeks, Rin." Detective Victor Graves sounded exhausted. 

The orange, one-piece county jumpsuit Rin wore was a size too big. It hung loose off her shoulders, exposing the white cotton tank top underneath. A serial number was stenciled across the breast in block lettering: **D.O.C. 398**. 

"How's the food?" Victor asked, a sly, humorless grin crossing his face.

This wasn't the first time over the last month she had found herself sitting at this stainless steel table, her wrists cuffed to the center ring. The interrogation room's dirty white square tiling hadn't been scrubbed in years, and the harsh fluorescent bulbs overhead cast an unflattering, sickly pallor over everything. 

A security camera tracked their interaction from the upper corner of the ceiling. A massive, one-way mirror spanned the length of the far wall.

Victor dropped a digital manila folder on the table. He pinned it under his index finger and leaned forward, staring down at her. "That file keeps getting bigger." 

Rin didn't speak. She knew better than to open her mouth. She just kept her head down, glaring up at him through her bangs.

"Let's go over it again," he said, pushing away from the table to pace the small room. It was the exact same set of questions he had been asking for a month. "Just tell me about the Filament, Rin. Where do you meet her? How do you meet her?"  

Rin knew exactly who he meant, but there was no way a beat detective from the lower city was going to learn anything about the Madame of the Filament. Especially not from her.

"Okay." Victor's voice lost its patience. "Corporate espionage, network subversion, infrastructure sabotage." A sharp edge of pride bled through his tone as he listed her rap sheet, before it shifted into something colder. "And let's not forget the most exciting part of your spree—your little escapade on the 15th floor of the Company tower with a gas canister." 

Victor stopped pacing and looked directly at her. His expression hardened. Rin could feel his colorless eyes bearing down on her. "That's my partners favorite part." He look up and stared through his reflection in the large mirror at the end of the room. Making eye contact with some invisible person on the other side of the glass.

"Four months," Victor muttered, shaking his head. "Four months the Tech Division chased Null. Chased you."

Rin had finally had enough. The silence broke. "A division?" she laughed, a dry, cynical sound. "What? A division of one?"

Victor's posture instantly shifted. He closed the distance, leaning over the table until she could feel his breath against her cheek. His voice was absolute. "Clever. Funny. Do you want to know why you're still sitting here, kid?" He didn't wait for her to answer. "Renewal and the Company don't know you're in my custody. They don't know we caught their little security leak." 

Rin blinked her eyes, turned her head and  looked up at him. For the first time in a month, the reality clicked into place. It answered the single question she had been asking herself repeatedly in her cell: *Why hadn't Renewal come to take care of her?* 

Victor leaned in closer. He shot a subtle glance toward the security camera in the top corner of the room. "They think you're sitting in county for auto theft, Rin. I'm not interested in whatever corporate garbage you found in the Company's files. I want the Filament, and you're going to give them to me."

Rin gritted her teeth. She turned her head, refusing to back away, and stared directly into the icy amber of his eyes. 

"If you knew anything, Detective Graves, you would care what I found." She paused, letting the silence stretch. Victor's gaze flickered. She could see the gears turning behind his eyes. "But you already know what it is. Don't you? You saw the schematic in the office tower on the exact same night I did." Rin leaned forward against the cuffs. "You just don't understand what you're looking at."
