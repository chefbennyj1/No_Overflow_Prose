XXSilas Blackwell took a drag from his cigarette, the ember flaring a harsh orange against the blue of the dawn. He was an erratic figure; a mop of white hair blew untamed in the morning wind, contrasting with the sharp lines of his dark collared shirt and tie. Over it all, he wore a lab coat that whipped around his legs in the cold air. His eyes were hidden behind a pair of circular sunglasses, rendering his expression unreadable. Despite the calculated perfection required of his station, the cigarette held between his fingers betrayed an obsessive energy. From the wind-swept terrace of the Mnemosyne Lab, he watched his world.

He didn't own this view. He didn’t have the inheritance to buy a single square foot of the platinum-laced composite he was standing on. The tower belonged to the "Company", Saito Takeda Corporation—a monument of extreme corporate wealth. But as the Head of Synthetic Integration, Blackwell knew this building belonged to him in ways the Company would never comprehend. 

The night’s heavy rain had washed the air clean, leaving behind a suffocating mist that settled beneath the high-altitude pedestrian tiers. To Blackwell, the fog was a necessary filter. It formed an opaque floor of white vapor that swallowed the Dead Circuits below, hiding the rusted scaffolding and the decrepit foundation of the lower world. Down there, ancient structures sat in partial ruin, forming a sprawling labyrinth of poverty and failing grids. The millions scurrying in the dark among those old buildings were just noise in a system. 

He exhaled, the grey smoke blurring the Zen-like patterns etched into the company's expensive alloy railing. There were no sprawling billboards up here; the corporate elite didn't need to be sold. The only illumination came from floating holographic displays, streaming flickering Japanese diagnostics in silence. The polished walkways were empty, this early. It was a calculated perfection. The peak of Oshima City wasn't a metropolis; it was a sleeping monster, ready to awake. 

He didn't need to turn around to know he was no longer alone. He could feel the slight shift in the air pressure as the glass doors of the lab slid open behind him.

"Are we ready, Miss Marlowe?" Blackwell asked, his voice calm, carrying over the wind.

Elena Marlowe stood in the threshold, framed by the icy glow of the massive holographic displays inside. She was an imposing presence, her lab coat a stark contrast against the dark server racks of the control room. Her natural hair framed a face set in a look of unyielding focus. 

"Yes, Doctor Blackwell," she replied, her golden eyes catching the cyan reflection of the cascading data streams inside. "The Mnemosyne Engine is connected to the city's main fusion reactor. You have three hundred and twenty terawatts at your disposal."

Blackwell raised the cigarette to his lips one last time, inhaling until the cherry burned close to the filter. He held the smoke in his lungs for a long second, then exhaled and dropped the butt over the edge of the railing. He didn't watch it fall into the white abyss. 

He turned on his heel and walked past Elena into the lab. The air inside vibrated with a bone-deep hum—the terrifying reality of the city's reactor channeling into the floor beneath their feet. 

He moved past the primary control consoles, approaching the reinforced railing that encircled the center of the room. He stopped, gripping the cold metal, and looked down.

Below him sat the Mnemosyne Engine—or at least, the very tip of it. The staggering bulk of the machine was buried deep beneath the lab's concrete foundation. What protruded into this observation chamber was a cluster of stark white pillars of varying heights. They rose sharply from the floor, terminating in angled points. A vertical display panel ran up one side of each pillar. Currently, every single screen was an empty black.

The floor of the engine pit was constructed of white paneling, though it was obscured. A heavy layer of dense fog rolled across the base of the pillars, breaking against the stark geometry like silent rapids before cascading over the edges like a waterfall. The air at the center of the array was bitter cold, a necessary extreme to keep the dormant core stabilized. The great engine was powered down, silent and waiting.

"A drop in the bucket for what we're asking it to unearth, Miss Marlowe," Blackwell said, his voice dropping into a gravelly rasp. He pulled a fresh cigarette from his coat pocket and slid it between his lips, though he didn't light it. Not in here. 

He leaned forward, bracing his weight against the railing, his reflection distorted in the reinforced glass. "The board wants better data compression. A smarter artificial intelligence to predict their margins. They think I'm building them a calculator."

He let out a humorless breath that fogged the glass. "I'm building a monument. Harnessing my singularity."

"Doctor Blackwell. Silas," Elena's voice interrupted, a note of warning creeping into her tone. "The board just dialed in."

Above the primary consoles, a massive display flickered to life. The encrypted feed partitioned the screen into a grid of digital monitors. Each square displayed nothing but the featureless silhouette of a corporate executive. 

"Perfect," Blackwell murmured, his lip curling into a sinister smile. "Just what we need. Extra eyes."

"The Saito Takeda Corporation expects success today, Blackwell," a synthesized voice echoed from the grid. 

Blackwell didn't bother to look back at the screen or at Elena, keeping his gaze fixed on the dormant core below. "Very well, Miss Marlowe," he murmured. "Give them a show."

Elena didn't blink. She remained where she was, her posture shifting from subordinate to commander as she projected her voice across the sprawling floor of the control room. Several other technicians in white coats looked up from their consoles.

"Ladies and gentlemen! This is not a test," Elena's voice cut through the hum of the reactor, authoritative. "We bring the quantum gravity acceleration online like professionals. I don't need to tell you what happens if the Mnemosyne Engine processor backfires."

The room erupted into motion. Fingers flew across holographic keyboards in a synchronized frenzy. 

Below Blackwell, the engine responded. Dense fog pooling at the base of the pillars surged upward, flooding the pit as the massive cooling valves released a torrent of thick gas. Then, one by one, the black screens on the sides of the white pillars began to ignite. They flickered at first, before stabilizing into cascades of crisp white and blue text, complex readouts began flowing down each pillar, illuminating the swirling fog in a spectral light.

The hum of the reactor deepened, shifting into a rhythmic pulse. It wasn't just a sound; it was a physical vibration that crept up through the concrete floor and resonated deep within their bones. The very air in the lab felt thick and heavy with the sheer gravity of it.

Blackwell’s sinister smile widened. He watched the data stream, entranced by the magnitude of the activation. He was calm, unbothered by the bone-rattling force of his creation.

Behind him, the reaction was different. The technicians on the floor were exchanging nervous glances. The Mnemosyne Engine was the first of its kind; no one knew what the environmental effects of a full power cycle would feel like. The sheer physical presence of the machine was terrifying. 

Elena stepped up to the railing beside Blackwell, ignoring the palpable anxiety radiating from the floor. The harsh light washed over her serious features. She studied a datapad in her hand, her tense posture relaxing just a fraction. "The initialization sequence is holding steady," she said, a note of genuine satisfaction breaking through her professional facade. She stepped closer to him, so only he could hear, "The containment calculations were flawless, Silas. Your calculations were perfect. Congratulations."

"Of course they were," Blackwell murmured, the unlit cigarette bobbing between his lips.

On the massive display above the floor, the grid of silhouettes leaned forward. A synthesized murmur of approval—or at least, predatory interest—emanated from the feed. The Saito Takeda Corporation was watching their investment come to life. Elena glanced up at the screens, aware of the corporate eyes bearing down on them. The show had to be flawless.

"Doctor Blackwell! Ma'am!" 

The frantic voice shattered the calm. Mina, a junior technician, marched up behind them, though she never once looked up from the glowing datapad in her hands. She was a stark contrast to the sterile perfection of the room; her hair was an unkempt bird's nest defying the lab's strict dress code. She shoved her black-rimmed glasses back up the bridge of her nose—a nervous tic born from having her head down, either buried in data or avoiding eye contact. 

"Ma'am, local space-time..." Mina stammered to the floor, holding the tablet out between them like a shield with a trembling hand. "It's... some kind of a mass-displacement anomaly. There is a severe fluctuation in the event horizon. The power dra—"

Elena spun around, her demeanor hardening. She shot a razor-sharp glance up at the Board's display before stepping into Mina's personal space. She didn't even look at the data. 

"Negative," Elena said, her voice dropping into a scolding hiss through gritted teeth. "That is a predicted resonance."

Mina flinched, pulling the tablet back against her chest. She swallowed hard, terrified of both the machine and her supervisor. "Yes, ma'am. Of course. All systems nominal."
