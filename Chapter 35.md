Victor's car sat at the edge of the clearing, still running on its cushion. Both doors hung open. Snow drifted in and settled on the seats. The street around it was empty.

It got smaller in the rear glass of the Madame's cruiser.

High above the fog, the first wash of morning had begun to touch the upper spires, practically invisible from down below. Here in the lower grid, the streets were still drowned in murk. Every few seconds, the glowing orange of a sodium streetlight cut through the tinted glass, dragging slow, rhythmic lines across the cabin and streaking amber over the passengers inside.

At the fog line, the Minders had taken their guns and walked them to the car.

The back of the cruiser was warm and smelled of leather and smoke. Two benches faced each other. Victor sat on the rear one, by the door. Across from him, Harlan sat between the two Minders with his hands in his lap. The Minder on the left had Victor's revolver resting on his thigh.

Up front, behind a glass partition, a synthetic drove. Its serial showed blue in the rearview mirror.

The Madame sat beside Victor with her legs crossed. She fitted a fresh cigarette into the long filter, and one of the Minders leaned across to light it. The snow on her shoulders had melted into dark spots on the silk.

The cruiser moved at a walking pace. Outside, the army walked with it.

They walked on both sides of the car, close enough to touch the glass. Plastic sheets hung off their shoulders and dragged through the snow. Feeding tubes trailed from their arms, cut loose from whatever had held them, and dripped blue onto the street. Every one of them wore a tag on its wrist.

There weren't many faces. The same few came past the window, again and again.

Harlan hadn't taken his eyes off the revolver.

"Jackie's gonna kill me," he said.

Victor didn't answer. He had put Harlan in this car. He was going to have to get them out of it.

He worked it through.

The Madame had her army. She had them marching beside the car.

*They built a black hole in the middle of the city,* Erin Adler had said. *And they don't have control over it.*  Blackwell's Mnemosyne laboratory. Iris Ito had warned them about the dangers of Blackwell's lab experiment, she was adamant they had run out of time. He had ignored her requests for help because he wanted to catch Null. He wanted to catch the Madame.   

"This was never about the riots, or the synths," Victor said.

The Madame drew on her cigarette.

"You're going for what's in that lab. Whatever they built under that tower."

She looked at him over the top of her wire-rimmed glasses.

"The Company has developed the ability to breed something extremely powerful, Detective. But they have dropped it," she said. "I intend to pick it up."

"Erin Adler said it got out."

"Erin Adler?" the Madame laughed. "I knew that little street rat lifted more then my access keys." She cleared her throat. "If what's in that lab got out, then it will need a new owner."

The Madame hadn't seen the blinding power of the synthetics eyes like he had. She had no idea what she was messing with.  

"Why bring us?" Victor asked.

She tapped her ash into the tray. "I know who you are, Graves. I've heard I'm something of a celebrity in your little world." She laughed a long raspy laugh. "You should see how it ends."

The cruiser crept on. 

"Your father used to sit there," she said.

Victor went still.

*The Madame don't just trust anyone, ya know.* His father had said it a hundred times, pointing at the window with a shaking hand. Victor had never once pictured the car.

Across from him, the Minder still held the revolver. Polished loafers. Eyes on nothing.

This was what his father had been.

Victor's thumb found the striker of the lighter in his pocket. He rolled it once. He didn't light it.

"Give the detective his gun," the Madame said.

The Minder held the revolver out, grip first.

Victor took it. The rails woke against his palm, a charge crawling up into his wrist. She sat close enough to touch, her face turned to the window.

Across from him, the other Minder slid a hand inside his coat, pressing a gun hard into Harlan's ribs. Harlan froze.

"Vic," Harlan said.

Victor laid the revolver on his knee.

The cruiser left the snow and went back into the fog. The road tilted up under them, and they started the climb toward New Veridia.

Through the rear glass, the rest of the army climbed after them. The rows came up the ramp out of the fog, and there were still more inside it.

Then the cruiser broke out of the fog, and the morning sun filled the car. Victor shut his eyes against it.

New Veridia was empty. Nobody stood on the terraces, and nobody crossed the sky-bridges overhead. Frost covered the glass of every tower they passed, thickest on the sides that faced the Takeda-Holden building.

The cruiser rolled onto the tower's front plaza and stopped. Along its edges, the black statues of gods and goddesses stood white with frost.

The plaza was full of armed people from the lower city. Barricades stood half-built between the statues. Nobody was fighting. Nobody was moving at all. Every face on the plaza was turned up.

A Renewal line held the top of the tower steps. The agents stood behind locked shields, the red seams glowing down the centers of their helmets. Every rifle was pointed straight up. Spent brass lay on the frost around their boots.

Behind the cruiser, the army stopped with it.

A Minder opened Victor's door, and the cold rushed in.

They got out.

Hundreds of people, and not one of them made a sound. Victor followed their faces up.

Above them, the tower went up in a single sheet of frosted glass, its edges lensed and shifted. At the top of the spire, something white hung in the air.

It was shaped like a woman, with long black hair hanging straight down her back. She held a man by the collar, out over the drop. The wind up there whipped his coat around him. It didn't touch her hair. His legs kicked. He was alive.

Renewal had fired at her. There wasn't a mark on either of them.

Victor tried to keep his eyes on her. He couldn't. The white of her hurt to look at. His eyes watered, and the edges of everything went bright. He dropped his eyes to the frost on the stones and waited for the burning to stop.

On the other side of the cruiser, Harlan was holding the roof with both hands.

"Vic." His voice cracked. "That's a person. It's holding a person."

"I see it." Victor made himself look again, a second at a time. A person in a lab coat, at the top of the tower. "Blackwell," Victor said.

Harlan stared at him. "The doctor?"

Whatever the doctor had built was holding him off the top of his own building.

Victor turned to the Madame and waited for her face to change.

It didn't. She drew on the cigarette and let the smoke out. Then she walked out to the front of the plaza alone.

The crowd turned to watch her go, and saw what was standing behind the cruiser. People stumbled back between the statues.

On the steps, the rifles came down out of the sky. The whole line turned to face the army.

Her back was to him. The revolver hung at his side. The Minder behind Harlan kept a hand on his collar.

Victor didn't raise it.

"Vic," Harlan said quietly. "That thing in the interview room. Is that it up there?"

"I'm not sure." Victor kept his eyes on the figure. 

At the front of the plaza, the Madame lifted one hand.

Behind the cruiser, the army took one step. Hundreds of bodies, all at once.

The Renewal loudspeaker came on, a synthesized voice ordering them to disperse.

The Madame lowered her hand toward the steps.

The army walked.

It went past Victor on both sides. Plastic brushed his coat. A feeding tube dragged across his shoe and left a blue streak on the frost.

Ahead of it, the crowd split and pressed back against the statues.

The Renewal line fired.

The front rows jerked and went down on the stones. The rows behind them stepped over the bodies and kept walking. None of them fired back. None of them ran. They walked into the guns.

Behind them, the crowd didn't move. The bodies took every round.

Victor looked up one more time. It hurt.

At the top of the tower, the white figure had turned its head. It was watching them come.
