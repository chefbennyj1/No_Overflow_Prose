The cruiser rolled into the plaza and stopped beside a Company truck.

Victor let the car coast to the edge of the plaza and parked behind one of the statues. He cut the engine. Neither of them had said much since the top of the avenue.

The truck sat at the head of a service ramp that sloped down from the plaza into the fog. The Takeda-Holden crest was painted on its side, and under it, in plain block letters, *FACILITIES & SANITATION*. Its back doors hung open. Nobody was inside.

Victor knew where that ramp went. Four weeks ago, he had brought Null up it in cuffs, out of the dark and away from the rows of sleeping synths.

"They already got in," Harlan said.

"Yeah." Victor looked at the open doors. "Sometime last night."

He got out of the car.

The cold went straight through his coat. His breath clouded in front of him. The statues of gods and goddesses that lined the plaza were covered in frost, their faces glittering in the early light.

He looked up.

From the avenue, it had been a shape at the top of the spire. From here, it had a body.

It was shaped like a woman. Her skin was white. Long black hair hung straight down her back. The wind up there was strong enough to whip the man's coat around his legs, and it didn't touch her hair. She held him by the collar, out over eighty stories of air. His legs kicked. He was alive.

Victor tried to keep his eyes on her. He couldn't. The white of her hurt to look at. His eyes watered, and the edges of everything went bright. He looked down at the frost on the stones and waited for the burning to stop.

On the other side of the car, Harlan was holding the roof with both hands.

"Vic." His voice cracked. "That's a person. It's holding a person."

"I see it."

"He's in a lab coat."

Victor made himself look again, a second at a time. A lab coat, at the top of this tower.

A month ago, he had come up here to ask Silas Blackwell what he was building. He had seen him once, a man in round glasses looking down at him over the atrium railing. Then the man had walked away.

"Blackwell," Victor said.

Harlan stared at him. "The doctor? From the logs?"

Victor didn't answer. Whatever the doctor had built was holding him off the top of his own building.

He reached into his coat for a cigarette and got it between his lips. He thumbed the striker on his lighter. Nothing. He tried again. His hand was shaking so hard the flame jumped and went out. On the third try it caught.

Twenty years in the lower grid had taught him what to do with anything he could put in cuffs. Nothing had taught him what to do with this.

Across the plaza, the cruiser's back door opened.

The Minder got out first, in his polished loafers. He looked once around the plaza. His eyes passed over Victor's car and kept going. Then he held the door.

The Madame stepped out.

Victor had never seen her face. He had seen her name in files and her money in the pockets of dirty cops. He had seen what was left of his father.

She was old, in a white silk dress, and a cigarette burned at the end of a long filter between her fingers. She looked up at the thing on the spire.

Victor watched her face. He waited for it to change.

It didn't. She drew on the cigarette and let the smoke out. Then she looked away from the sky.

*The Madame don't just trust anyone, ya know.*

His father had said that from the corduroy chair, pointing at the window with a shaking hand. Arthur had watched windows for this woman until there was nothing left of him to watch with.

Victor's hand went to his revolver.

"Vic." Harlan kept his voice down. "She's right there."

"I see her."

"So go get her."

"Not yet."

Harlan stared at him.

"Arrest her for what?" Victor said. "Standing in a plaza? She'd be out before the paperwork." He watched her walk toward the ramp. "I want her coming back up that ramp with it in her hands."

Two of her men went down the ramp ahead of her. She followed them without hurrying. The fog took her a little at a time, the silk going gray, and then she was gone.

The Minder stayed at the top of the ramp, beside the truck.

They waited.

The cold got into Victor's hands. He kept the cigarette going and kept his eyes off the sky.

"We should go," Harlan said.

"After this."

"You said that last night."

"I meant it last night."

Harlan blew into his hands. He looked at the ramp, then down at his shoes.

"Jackie called the school yesterday," he said. "The one by the water. They have a place for both kids."

Victor nodded.

"That thing in Interview Two," Harlan said. "The canner. Before it lit up the room, it asked you something."

Victor drew on the cigarette.

"It asked if you were a good man." Harlan kept his eyes on the ramp. "You never answered it."

"Ask me after this," Victor said.

Harlan almost laughed. It didn't come out.

They heard the crowd before they saw it.

A low roar came up the avenue out of the fog, and under it, a chant Victor couldn't make out.

At the tower doors, boots hit the stone in rhythm. A Renewal squad came down the steps at a run and spread out across the front of the plaza, the red seams glowing down the center of their helmets. Their shields locked together, and rifles came up over the top.

The first of the crowd came up out of the fog at the top of the avenue. Dirty masks. Coats soaked through. They kept coming, hundreds of them, spilling between the frozen statues until they filled the plaza from the avenue to the steps.

Some of them looked up at the tower and stopped. The people behind them pushed them forward.

A bottle came out of the crowd and broke against a shield.

The Renewal loudspeaker came on, a synthesized voice ordering the crowd to disperse. Nobody dispersed.

Harlan grabbed Victor's sleeve and pulled him down behind the car. They crouched against the door panels with the crowd on one side and the rifles on the other.

"Vic!" Harlan had to shout it. "We need to leave!"

Victor was watching the ramp.

The fog at the bottom of it moved.

The Madame came up out of it first. She climbed the ramp the same way she had gone down, without hurrying, the cigarette still burning at the end of its filter. White silk. Above her, at the top of the tower, the other white figure hung in the sky.

A synthetic walked up out of the fog behind her. Then a whole row of them, shoulder to shoulder, climbing the ramp in step. Plastic sheets still hung off some of them. Every one of them wore a tag on its wrist.

More rows came up behind the first. The ramp filled, and there were still more of them inside the fog.

Their eyes had the ordinary glow of every canner on every street in Oshima.

At the front of the crowd, people saw them. The quiet started there and moved back through the plaza. The chanting stopped. Behind their shields, the Renewal agents didn't move.

The Madame reached the top of the ramp and stopped beside the truck.

Behind her, a thousand bodies stopped with her, all at once.
