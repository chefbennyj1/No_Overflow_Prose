The cigarette burned in Victor's fingers. He didn't blink. His hands gripping the steering wheel so hard his knuckles were white. 

Harlan sat beside him in the passenger seat of the rusted cruiser and stared up at the sky. 

High above the Takeda-Holden plaza, the shape hung motionless in the morning light. It was a silhouette, suspended against the rising sun. From its outstretched arm, a second figure dangled above the spire. 

"Vic," Harlan whispered. His voice was ragged. "What is that?"

"I don't know."

"Drive away," Harlan said, talking to himself. "Put it in reverse and drive away."

Harlan didn't move his hands. The engine of the old cruiser idled, rattling the floorboards. 

A sharp screech of tires tore through the morning air. 

Victor snapped his attention down to the plaza. Two black cars with mirrored windows vaulted the curb and slammed to a halt in front of the glass doors of the Mnemosyne lobby. They didn't park. They formed a barricade. 

A second later, a heavy sanitation van barreled into the plaza, flanked by two more. They were unmarked, painted a flat, matte black. They skidded into a perimeter formation around the two cars. 

"Company?" Harlan asked, finally dropping his hands from the wheel. He reached under his seat and pulled out his service pistol. 

"No," Victor said. He drew his own weapon. He checked the magazine and slammed it home. "The Company seals its doors. It doesn't break them."

The back doors of the lead sanitation van kicked open. 

Four figures stepped out. Then four more. They didn't wear Renewal riot gear. They wore standard-issue gray jumpsuits. They moved with identical, terrifying precision. Every step was calculated. Every head turned at the exact same angle. 

Victor recognized the blank, unblinking faces. The serial numbers glowing under their eyes. They were the empty shells from Deep Storage. The sleepers. 

And they were all carrying stolen Renewal auto-rifles. 

A woman in a tailored suit stepped out of the lead black car. The Madame. She adjusted her collar, ignored the dead god hanging in the sky, and pointed a single, gloved finger at the Mnemosyne lobby doors. 

The sleepers raised their rifles. 

"They're taking the tower," Victor said. 

"Vic, we are two beat cops with handguns." Harlan racked the slide of his pistol. "Those are heavy-caliber munitions."

Victor kicked his door open. The morning air was freezing. He stepped out of the cruiser and leveled his weapon at the plaza. 

"I know," Victor said. 

Above them, the tower warped the light around it, doubling the glass. The dead god watched the city.
