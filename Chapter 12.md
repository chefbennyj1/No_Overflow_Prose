The Dead Circuits didn't get cold. At the base of the Takeda-Holden tower, the concrete under Harper's boots was ice.

The fog that hung over the Circuits broke around the tower, creating a strange cylinder of clear air rising upward. 

Harper tipped her head back. Far above, the pedestrian sky-bridges crisscrossed between the skyscrapers. It looked like the tower walls narrowed to a point, vanishing into the early morning sky. The tops of the surrounding skyscrapers were lost in the smog. 

She stared up through the clearing. Stars. A handful, faint against the predawn and the glow of the city, pinholes of white between the sky-bridges. She hadn't seen one in years. In Sonora, they came all the way down to the ground.

"You're staring," Rin said. 

Rin stood a few steps away, her collar turned up against the cold, one hand pressed flat over the inside pocket of her jacket.

"We're never gettin' through that lobby," Harper said. She'd built her whole rig out of Company hardware. She knew what they spent on locks.

"Don't need the lobby," Rin replied shaking her head. She pulled out her wire-wrapped SBC. The small green crystal display listed a basic string of text instructions downloaded back at the apartment. "Company buildings have old storage in the sub-levels. From before the mergers. Sealed up and forgotten."

Harper leaned over to read the glowing green text. "Takeda-Holden transit terminal, sub-level four, door 4002. What's even down there?"

"Don't know," Rin said, turning the SBC off. "But it's what it wants."

Harper smiled. The knot under her ribs stayed where it was. 

***

The Takeda-Holden Transit Terminal's interchanges fell four tiers below them, surrounded by arches of polished white stone and chrome. 

Frictionless magnetic trains surged through the tunnels, pulling the air after them. Every arrival dragged a wash of the city's warm air across Harper's face before the cold settled back against her cheeks.

Static, automated intercom voices reverberated in the terminal, announcing the arriving and departing trains in three different languages. Harper watched her breath plume in the overhead lighting. The air crawled along her skin with a prickle of static electricity.

The escalators were fenced off with security gates. Commuters moved past the turnstiles, tapping glass chips against the readers before heading down to the tracks. Neither she nor Rin carried the identification required to stand legally on Company property. 

They descended the terminal's empty staircase, ignored by the commuters for its inconvenience. Each level was marked with a wall-sized number designating the tier. They continued until they hit sub-level four. 

Morning passengers, Company office workers, and high-end synthetics packed the platform. Some waited for trains, while others moved toward the tower's executive lifts, speeding up to the pedestrian sky-bridges far above them. 

As they reached the platform, the temperature plummeted further. The floors were black marble. Brightly lit transit information inlays, embedded in the concourse floor, extended across the terminal. 
Even at this level the people of New Veridia let the city make their decisions for them.

Harper and Rin both wore the grime of the Dead Circuits. Commuters parted around them without looking up from their devices. The way you'd step around garbage in a gutter.  

A man in a dark suit, and holographic glasses knocked Harper's shoulder with his, pushing her a half-step back. "Hey, asshole. Watch who you're knockin'," she said. 

The man paused and turning his attention back to her, his face painted with an irritated sneer. He looked at her worn brown suede jacket, then Rin's scuffed boots before turning back and continuing through the terminal concourse.

Harper's eyes darted toward the far end. "Where is it?"

An automated voice announced another train arrival as a long mag-train streaked past them into the level four terminal. 

"Look, there," Rin said. She tucked the loose lock of hair behind her ear. Harper had seen her do that before every bad idea she'd ever had. Rin gestured toward where the train had entered from. A concrete catwalk ran into the tunnel along both walls of the train tube. Green steel doors were set into the wall behind it, each stenciled with a faded white number.

"End of the line," Rin said, pulling her SBC from her pocket. She turned, following the tracks, cutting toward the secluded maintenance doors. 

They found it right inside the tube. The faded number on the door read: **4002**. 

Rin didn't hesitate. A blank magnetic card dangled from a thin ribbon cable on the SBC. She swiped it through the door's reader. The lock flashed a harsh red. 

"Keep watch," Rin said. The bare circuit board in her hand flickered with raw data, running a brute-force decryption against the door's card reader. 

Harper stepped back, looking toward the platform at the end of the tube. Corporate executives flowed past. 

The crowd parted as two Renewal agents made their way through the terminal platform, patrolling the marble floors. They turned the corner, their opaque visors sweeping the corridor. 

"Rin," Harper said. "We have a problem."

The lead agent's helmet locked onto them. He raised his control-baton and pointed it through the crowd, his voice booming over the noise of the terminal. "Hey! Step away from the door!"

"I need time," Rin said, watching the flow of numbers move down the green display of the SBC.

"No time!" Harper backed against the wall as both agents surged down the platform. The crowd scattered, clearing a path. 

A sharp beep chirped from the SBC, the crystal display reading a series of hashed numbers and letters. 

The female agent moved into position at the entrance of the tunnel, blocking any retreat to the platform, while the male agent stepped directly onto the concrete ledge, closing the distance down the narrow walkway.

Behind them, the tunnel began to groan. Pressure built in Harper's ears.

Over the intercom the voice announced, "Takeda-Holden terminal sub-level four. Arrival. Please step back from the incoming train for your own safety."

The commuter train tore past them through the tube, into the terminal, inches from the catwalk. The displaced air slammed Harper flat against the concrete wall, her hair whipping across her eyes. Rin shouldered  the deafening gust, holding the SBC steady. 

The bulk of the train walled them in, cutting off the platform entirely. 

The male agent pushed through the slipstream, boots grinding on the catwalk grit. "You have been ordered to stop!" 

Rin dragged the card through the reader a second time. The magnetic lock clacked.  

The agent closed the distance and raised his baton to strike. Harper stepped squarely in front of Rin, bracing herself to take the blow. 

Rin threw her weight forward, forcing the steel door inward with her shoulder. She caught Harper's collar with her free hand and pulled, hard. They fell through the entrance, into the dark together. The baton swung through empty air.

Harper caught the edge of the heavy door with her boot and kicked it shut. The latch slammed home, severing the white lights of the transit terminal. A second later, a fist hammered against the steel plate from the other side. 

Before them, a grated staircase stepped down onto a steel walkway, suspended in a concrete tunnel. Long yellow light tubes flickered as the mag-trains entered and exited the terminal above them.
