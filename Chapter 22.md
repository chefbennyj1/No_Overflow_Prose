Four weeks, Rin." Detective Victor Graves sounded exhausted.

The orange, one-piece correctional jumpsuit Rin wore was a size too big. It hung loose off her shoulders, exposing the white cotton tank top underneath. A serial number was stenciled across the breast in block lettering: D.O.C. 3 9 8.

"How's the food?" Victor asked, a humorless grin crossing his face. 

He leaned over the stainless steel table, his colorless eyes catching the harsh fluorescent light. He studied the dark, fresh bruising along the side of her jaw and the scabbed split on her lip. The humor drained out of his expression, replaced by a flash of genuine guilt. "Looks like they aren't taking care of you in there."

Rin didn't speak. This wasn't the first time over the last month she had found herself sitting here, pulled out of her cell, her wrists cuffed to the center ring so the detective could stare at her. 

Victor cleared his throat, keeping his eyes on her jaw. He swallowed hard dropping a digital manila folder on the table, and pinning it under his index finger. He stared down at her. "That file keeps getting bigger."

Rin didn't speak. It was the exact same set of questions he had been asking for a month. She knew better than to open her mouth. She kept her head down, glaring up at him through her bangs.

"Let's go over it again," Victor said, pushing away from the table to pace the small room. "Corporate espionage, network subversion, infrastructure sabotage... it's a long sheet."

He stopped pacing and looked directly at her, softening his voice. "But I know how this works, kid. You didn't plan this. You didn't know you were going to get in so deep with her. You didn't really know what she was asking you to do."

Rin's jaw tightened. She knew exactly who he meant, but there was no way a beat detective was going to learn anything about the Madame of the Filament from her. "I don't know what you mean," she lied.

He was asking the wrong questions. Focused on the wrong part of his case.

"Four months," Victor said, shaking his head. "Four months the Tech Division chased Null. Chased you."

Rin had finally had enough. The silence broke. "A division?" she laughed, dry and cynical. "What? A division of one?"

Victor slammed his hand on the metal table. "Clever. Funny. Whatever corporate nonsense you got yourself tied up in—why you were in deep storage—I don't care. This is about the syndicate. I want the Filament."

Rin shot back, her voice tightening. "If I give you the Madame, the Filament will kill me before you can protect me."

Victor's posture instantly shifted. He closed the distance until she could feel his breath against her cheek. His voice was absolute. "Do you want to know why you're still sitting here, kid?" He didn't wait for her to answer. "Renewal and the Company don't know you're in my custody. They don't know we caught their little security issue."

Rin blinked her eyes, turned her head and looked up at him. For the first time in a month, the reality clicked into place. It answered the single question she had been asking herself repeatedly in her cell: Why hadn't Renewal come to take care of her?

Victor shot a subtle glance toward the security camera in the top corner of the room. "They think you're sitting in a cell for auto theft. The Madame left you to rot here for a month. She already burned you. I am the only wall between you and Renewal."

The reality of her total isolation settled over her. The Madame had abandoned her. But, the detective was playing a dangerous game with Renewal—if they ever found out he had her. 

He leaned in closer, dropping his voice to a quiet, commanding tone, "I found you. In that place, at that moment. I'm the only person alive who knows where you're sitting." 

Rin heard his voice, *I found you in this place, at this moment.* She stopped fighting, and slumped back in the steel chair, breaking eye contact to stare at the dirty tiles on the floor.

"Now, why did you take the job, Erin?" he asked, pinning her with his colorless eyes. "Did you steal that data because you wanted to help the Filament burn this city to the ground... or did you just do it because you were desperate to buy your way out of the slums?"

Rin gritted her teeth. She turned her head, refusing to back away, and stared directly into his colorless eyes.

"If you knew anything, Detective Graves, you would care what I found." She paused, letting the silence stretch. Victor's gaze flickered. She could see he was in deep thought. "Huh—But you already know what it is. Don't you. You saw the schematic in the office tower on the exact same night I did." Rin leaned forward against the cuffs. "You just don't understand what you were looking at."

"The same night?" Victor paused. "Got ya."
